# DeRivean architecture

| Module | Ownership |
| --- | --- |
| [`@simulation`](../@simulation) | The natural world as a pure function of seed, age, and place, and the definition of every material |
| [`@engine`](../@engine) | Authoritative playable world: voxels streamed from the simulation, player edits, material behavior, structural failure, Jolt collision, far terrain, and frame projections |
| [`@napi`](../@napi) | Node-API binding, xmake addon build, frame serialization, and bridge integration tests |
| [`@game`](../@game) | Electron launcher, worker and IPC, input, Three.js renderer, and HUD |

The engine runs in a Node worker owned by Electron's main process. The sandboxed renderer submits one in-flight command over the [bridge](../@game/contract/GameBridge.ts) and updates its look direction locally. The engine advances in fixed 120 Hz steps while play is active. It returns player and debris transforms, changed or evicted terrain and water meshes, the authoritative target, and voxel particles. Three.js expands the packed faces into render geometry and instantiates water, sand, and foam cubes.

A [game](../@engine/src/game/type/Game.hpp) is played on one simulated planet, chosen by seed and age. Starting it blocks the worker while the simulation evolves the planet and finds a shore, where the engine [puts the player](../@engine/src/game/fn/Spawn.cpp); after that `advance` never waits for the simulation. Taking a world down waits for the tile builds in flight; a process that is about to exit [lets the world go](../@game/main/engine.mjs) instead. The bridge contract, the [addon](../@napi/src/addon.cpp), and the [worker's](../@game/main/engine-worker.cjs) transfer list change together.

## World and support

The [voxel world](../@engine/src/world/type/VoxelWorld.hpp) uses sparse 4 m cubic chunks in all three axes. Each occupied 1 m tile spans 8 × 8 × 8 voxels. A uniform tile stores its material once and expands to a 512-entry array when it contains mixed materials. Terrain masks, meshes, and colliders use local dense samples. Everything in it is ordinary material that can be excavated at any altitude.

Materials are defined by the simulation; the engine's [view of them](../@engine/src/voxel/constant/Materials.hpp) carries density, friction, and load capacity per voxel face under the same voxel id. The [support check](../@engine/src/structure/fn/Collapse.cpp) discovers connected pieces around cuts, tests their load against local contacts, and accounts for weaker bearings such as leaves. Contacts with real material at the search boundary anchor the local calculation. In a world that continues beyond its voxels that boundary is the ground itself, so whatever reaches it stands and only a piece cut free on every side falls; the load test and the weak bearings decide in a world that is whole in memory, which today only the tests build. Detached solids enter Jolt as weighted rigid bodies, and their loose voxels become grains that settle back as the material they were. The build stock and excavated mass are reported separately.

## Water and grains

[Water state](../@engine/src/water/type/Water.hpp) stores 256 integer volume units per full voxel. A 30 Hz transfer step visits changed water cells and their neighbors, moves volume down openings, and uses surface head for lateral flow. A [connected backwater pass](../@engine/src/water/fn/Backwater.cpp) can move pressure head through a full, roofed passage. A gate or a newly placed solid blocks transfer across its occupied cells. Placing a solid in bulk water displaces the volume into available connected space before committing the edit.

A drop detaches an actual amount from a cell. [Airborne parcels](../@engine/src/water/fn/WaterParcels.cpp) use gravity, preserve that volume through flight and contact, and merge into the receiving water or terrain pool. Fast impacts emit short-lived foam cubes. [Sand](../@engine/src/granular/fn/Sand.cpp) detaches when support gives way or the neighboring slope exceeds its angle of repose. Grains settle back into voxels or remain in motion. Both types query static terrain and sweep against moving Jolt bodies, including the player's capsule.

The only way out of the world is its floor. Water, grains, and detached rock whose highest point has sunk below the simulation's world floor go into their escaped ledgers, and [one test](../@engine/src/world/type/VoxelWorld.hpp) decides that for all of them. The invariant is `seeded water = in cells + airborne + escaped`; sand grains and detached rock track their own losses. A player below the floor starts over at the spawn.

Voxel water is finite: a volume exists only because something seeded it. A future spring can release from a finite catchment or groundwater store using the head difference at a physical outlet, with the same volume debit. An impermeable dam can stop downstream flow until water finds an outlet or overtops it. Current fluid behavior approximates pressure and flow on the voxel grid, with a single gravity direction.

## Planet streaming

[Nature](../@engine/src/nature/service/Nature.hpp) holds the simulated world and two pools of engine workers. Voxel columns, far terrain, and collider shapes are built on one, as large as the machine's performance cores, with the voxels round the player ahead of everything far; the weather is stepped on the other. Neither kind of work runs on the simulation's own pool: a task there cannot be interrupted, so a chunk under the player would wait for a tile build that takes seconds. The pools share the cores by the scheduler, and the thread that advances the game is [favoured](../@engine/src/threads/type/Threads.hpp) over both, which only macOS offers. The game thread reads the simulation only through calls that cannot start or wait for a build.

The engine [asks for tiles](../@engine/src/nature/fn/TileRequests.cpp) by what the eye gains from them: how coarse the data a tile replaces is against how far away it lies, with what a fast flight is heading for counted as nearer. That puts the region level over the whole view first, 330 km around, then the local level over 64 km, the site level over 3 km, and the ground level around the feet. The tiles the player's own place is made of, from the region down to the ground under the feet, come before all of that and may use every worker; the rest of the view leaves a quarter of the workers free, so the chain down to the ground does not wait at every link for a far tile to finish. A tile is asked for once its parents are finished, and no more requests are in flight than the simulation has workers, because a queued tile build cannot be cancelled. Finished tiles stay in the simulation's atlas for the session; the engine cannot evict them.

The [terrain stream](../@engine/src/terrain/type/TerrainStream.hpp) keeps voxels around the player. Its unit is a column of 4 m chunks: the band that holds the ground, the chunks within a brush's reach of the player at any depth, and every chunk the player has edited. A [column](../@engine/src/terrain/type/Column.hpp) is generated in the background once the site and ground tiles its samples reach are finished, which makes its voxels a pure function of seed, age, and chunk key whatever was loaded before. The same task meshes the chunks against the untouched world around them; a chunk next to edited ground is meshed on the game thread instead. Finished columns enter the world and the frame within a per-frame time budget.

The played [voxel world](../@engine/src/world/type/VoxelWorld.hpp) continues beyond its voxels. Rock that is not loaded reads as solid, so water does not flow into it, cuts cannot reach it, and the support check treats it as bearing. Open sky above a column's band reads as air without being stored. A grain, a water parcel, or a detached body that reaches ground that is not loaded waits there unchanged and resumes when the ground arrives, so where it ends up does not depend on what happened to be loaded; none of that counts as escaped.

Every voxel write to a streamed world is journalled. The journal becomes [per-chunk edits](../@engine/src/terrain/type/Edits.hpp) that are applied on top of the generated voxels whenever the chunk loads again, and a log of what changed hands, by volume and material, that `takeEdits` hands to whoever will feed it back to the simulation.

Terrain colliders around the player come from the same voxels. Their shapes are built in the background ahead of the player; an edited chunk and the chunks around a moving body get theirs at once. A player on foot whose surrounding chunks or colliders are not there yet [waits in place](../@engine/src/game/fn/Player.cpp) and the frame reports `streamingReady = false`. When voxels arrive around a body that overlaps them, it is lifted straight up, keeping its place on the map, to the first height at which it fits. Under a roof that is the floor of the pocket it is in, not the surface; a body caught sideways in a wall has no such height below the roof and comes out on top. Flight has no gravity, follows the view, collides where colliders exist, and never waits.

## Clock and weather

The [game](../@engine/src/game/type/Game.hpp) owns the world clock: the planet's age and the calendar seconds played since. Play always runs in real time; each command says how much calendar passes with it, from none to a year a second. [One function](../@engine/src/game/fn/Game.cpp) moves the natural world to the clock's date, and everything that lives on calendar time is advanced there.

The simulation owns the weather; the engine only [keeps its state](../@engine/src/nature/type/Weather.hpp) and has it stepped on a pool of its own, an hour of calendar at a time. The game thread reads a finished copy and never waits for a step. A calendar that outruns the stepping shows as lag in the frame, and the weather catches up in the background. The state depends on the start date and the calendar alone, not on frame rate or on how the calendar got there. The frame carries the sun's direction and the weather at the player, and about once a second the clouds and the snowpack of the whole planet on the weather grid. The renderer draws from that and adds detail below a cell; it never makes weather up. Snow on the ground is the atmosphere's: it keeps snow per weather cell at a ladder of elevations, the frame passes the ladder and each cell's snow line on unchanged, and the renderer reads them at the height of the ground it draws. The engine invents none and bakes none into the far terrain, because snow moves with the calendar; glacier ice is ground.

## Far terrain

The far terrain is moving from height-field patches to cubes; a world makes one of the two, chosen when it starts, until the renderer draws cubes only.

### Cubes

Beyond the voxels the world is shown [at coarser cube sizes](../@engine/src/horizon/type/CubeChunk.hpp): level k holds cubes of the voxel edge times 2^k, from half a metre to 1024 m, on the lattice the voxels lie on. A cube is solid if the world is solid at its centre and is made of what is there, so a crest keeps its height and a wall its place; the top cube of a column is made of what covers the surface. The side of a step is mostly the slope the cubes were cut from, so it is made of that cover too wherever the slope is one its loose cover lies on; only ground steeper than that, or bare, shows the [bed](../@engine/src/horizon/type/Beds.hpp) at its depth. Columns are cut from the simulated surface at the level whose cells the cubes can tell apart, and from the planet's own grid where no tile is finished; they hold no overhangs, which only the voxels have. Chunks are 32 cubes along every edge and meshed by the voxels' own mesher, every face with the occlusion of its corners; in a world of cubes the bridge packs the quads of voxel chunks and far chunks alike into two words each.

[Each level](../@engine/src/horizon/type/FarCubes.hpp) is a square of columns of chunks that follows the eye, 128 cubes to each side by default, which keeps a cube a few pixels wide wherever its level is drawn; the coarsest reaches 260 km. A level is whole inside its box, also under the finer levels. The frame carries the box of every level, centred on the eye and nested, and the renderer draws a level inside its box and outside the next finer one. A box shrinks while chunks are missing, so a level that is not built yet or cannot keep up with a fast eye leaves the coarser one showing, never a hole. Levels too fine for the eye's height or speed rest. Coarse levels are built first, and a chunk is swapped for a better build of itself when finer tiles under it finish.

[Standing water](../@engine/src/horizon/type/StandingWater.hpp) is one level per water body, sent as top faces over the wet columns. A column under water ends below the level and the dry column beside it at or above it, so rounding to whole cubes neither floods land nor lifts a lake bed out of its lake.

### Patches

Beyond the voxels the [horizon](../@engine/src/horizon/type/Horizon.hpp) shows the simulated surface as height-field [patches](../@engine/src/horizon/type/Patch.hpp) of 32 × 32 cells on a world-aligned quadtree, out to a view distance of 260 km. Cell size doubles per level from 1 m to 1024 m. Inside 125 of its cells from the eye a patch gives way to its four children where they would show: where one more level would move the surface by more than about half a pixel. That allows cells of 4 m out to 1 km, 16 m to 4 km, 64 m to 16 km, and 256 m to 64 km, and leaves flat ground and deep water coarser. A patch already split stays split a tenth beyond those distances.

A patch reads the simulation level whose cells its samples can still tell apart, and where no tile is finished yet the planet's own 65 km grid, so the far view is whole from the first frame and a distant range stands on the skyline before any tile is built. It is rebuilt when finer data under it arrives, nearest first, a budgeted number per frame. Where fine data ends at the edge of a tile whose neighbour is not built, samples ease into the coarser level over sixteen cells, so the change of data draws no line across the country. Each patch says which level it was built from and which it wants, and the frame counts the tiles the view wants and has at every level.

What the renderer holds only changes by whole swaps: a patch for its four children, siblings for their parent, a patch for a better build of itself. It never shows two patches over the same ground or opens a hole in ground it already showed. Each vertex also carries the height of the parent level's surface. Blended fully onto it, a patch meets a coarser neighbour exactly, so a border between two levels needs no stitching; the dropped border only covers neighbours built from different simulation levels while tiles are still arriving.

Every patch also carries a shading raster at twice the density of its vertices: a normal that keeps edges, the id of [the material a voxel there shows](../@engine/src/horizon/type/Tint.hpp), and how much sky the place sees. Vertex colours come from the same material rule and the voxels' own palette, so the hand-off between patches and voxels shows one material. The rule knows what a surface sample knows; which stones a coarse cover is made of and beds exposed inside one cell are the voxel column's. Standing water travels as a level per vertex: the flood level of a lake or of the sea, read from the finest data there is whatever the patch's level, carried across ground that lies below it and one vertex into the dry shore. A wet point counts as standing water only if the point one data cell away is wet at the same level, so neither a stream's depth on a slope nor a one-cell pit of coarse data makes a sheet; running water has none yet. It is not voxel water: the player wades and floats in it, and digging next to it does not flood.

Nothing measured from the world's origin crosses the bridge in single precision. Patch heights are measured from the patch's own origin, particles from an origin sent with them, and faces from their chunk.

## Display

The [stage](../@game/src/scene/service/WorldStage.ts) draws one world. World positions are doubles that can lie millions of metres from the origin, so everything hangs under a group offset by a render origin near the camera. Meshes carry positions relative to their own chunk or patch, and shaders, shadows, and uniforms only see small render-space numbers. The origin moves in whole repeats of the voxel grain, which keeps that pattern fixed to the world. The depth buffer is logarithmic, because one view holds voxels a few centimetres from the eye and terrain hundreds of kilometres away.

Voxel chunks own the ground inside `Frame.voxelBox` and [far patches](../@game/src/terrain/service/FarTerrain.ts) own it outside: patch fragments inside the box and chunk fragments outside its footprint are discarded. Patches blend onto their parent's heights over the distances `WorldInfo.far` gives, and a dropped border covers what blending cannot. The renderer never smooths a height. Plain shading (L) drops albedo, clouds and most of the air for judging geometry; the blue in it is natural water.

### What the ground's look is made of

| Part | Where it comes from |
| --- | --- |
| Shape, material, standing water, sun direction, cloud field | Simulated; the renderer draws what the frame carries. |
| Colour of a place | The palette colour of the simulated material, on voxels and on patches. Nothing colours ground by height or slope. |
| Stains, mottling, specks, beds within one rock | Procedural: [one world-anchored variation](../@game/src/scene/constant/ground.ts) of that colour, the same on both sides of the hand-off. A voxel takes it at its middle, so a cube stays one piece. |
| Wet ground | On patches, the engine's water level beside a shore vertex. Voxels have no such level near them and are drawn dry. |
| Light on far slopes | The patch's shading raster. A texel blends only with neighbours facing much as it does, so a crease keeps both its slopes. Below a texel the variation tilts the light as a small relief. |
| Mixed cover | The raster dithers two material ids; the [patch surface](../@game/src/terrain/fn/patchSurface.ts) weighs neighbouring texels evenly, which shows the mixture and no chequerboard. A trick on top: the border between two materials wanders by up to a texel instead of following the raster's steps. |
| Hollows and creases | A trick, not light transport, kept under plain shading: patches darken by the raster's share of visible sky, voxels by a [volume of the solid cells](../@game/src/scene/service/VoxelOcclusion.ts) round the player, sampled half a voxel off each face. |
| Shadows | Three maps, all from the simulated sun: the voxels' near the player, [the far terrain's on itself](../@game/src/terrain/service/TerrainShadow.ts) across 120 km, which is what makes relief read under a low sun, and the clouds'. |
| Distance | [Aerial perspective](../@game/src/sky/constant/airFade.ts): light lost per colour along the path through air and haze that thin with height, replaced by the sky's. At the view distance the ground has become the horizon. |
| Brightness | The exposure follows the light falling on level ground, part of the way, as an eye or a camera does. |

The [sky](../@game/src/sky/service/Sky.ts) owns sun, sky light, air and exposure. Under cloud the ground is lit by what the cloud lets through instead of by blue sky, and the air near the eye takes the cloud's grey; the cloud is the one over the eye, so far ground still fades into the clear horizon the dome draws.

Natural water is drawn from the patches' water levels with tint and opacity that grow with depth, a mirror of the sky where the view grazes it, and a thin line where the depth runs out. Standing water is level: a cell whose corners carry levels too far apart to be one surface is left open, and F3 counts those cells. Voxel water keeps its own meshes.

## Map

The map (M) is drawn from [charts](../@simulation/src/chart/fn/Chart.hpp): the simulation's answer for a square of the world at a raster size, each pixel read from the finest finished tile level that the pixel pitch can show, down to site cells, and from the planet where no tile is. Reading a chart never builds or waits for a tile, so the same square comes back sharper as tiles arrive. A chart is the untouched world: the session's own changes to ground and water are written by the session thread and are not read from another. The [bridge call](../@napi/src/chart.cpp) returns at once and reads the chart on libuv's pool; the session goes on advancing meanwhile, and a restart or the end of the session first waits for the charts still reading its world.

The [overlay](../@game/src/map/service/WorldMap.ts) asks for a coarse chart of a new view and then the sharp one as side-by-side panes, asks again while tiles arrive, and [colours the rasters on the GPU](../@game/src/map/fn/chartShader.ts). It frees the pointer, which rests the world's controls, and keeps the session simulating through a pilot that only waits. A Shift-click on the map goes through that pilot as the engine's own teleport, which settles the traveller on the ground there. Charts follow the world as landscapes read it, at unwrapped positions: the planet repeats under them every `WorldInfo.planetSpan`, but the offset through which landscapes read the planet does not, so the repeat is close and not exact.

## Capture

`DERIVEAN_CAPTURE=<plan.json> mise run play` starts the app without the launcher, walks the shots of a [capture plan](../@game/src/capture/type/CapturePlan.ts), writes a PNG and a JSON record per shot, and quits. It exits non-zero when the run fails or hangs.

A shot waits for the world to settle. When nothing has arrived for a while and the engine still reports unfinished tiles, it is taken anyway and its record says `stalled`. Every record carries `dataLevels`, the share of the ground round the eye that the simulation held at site, local and region detail; below 1 the picture shows coarser stand-ins somewhere.

[`@game/capture/look.json`](../@game/capture/look.json) is the set the ground's look is judged by against the [reference landscapes](reference-landscapes.md): one place from the ground and from 300 m, 2 km and 8 km, towards the four compass points, under a high and a low sun; one fixed view of a mountain range for comparing rounds; and two half-minute boost flights, with and without clouds, whose records carry the frame times.
