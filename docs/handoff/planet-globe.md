## Stage 1: kinematics and pieces

- **Epochs** (`tectonics/fn/Euler`): `eulerEpochAt` takes index, start, end and seed from the flat `epochAt` of the same world, so both histories reorganise together. n = lattice² sites (9 to 25) on a Fibonacci lattice (González 2010), turned at random per epoch and jittered by up to 0.4 of the site spacing; reach 0.62–1.55; pole uniform on the sphere. `plateAt` is the weighted Voronoi by angular distance / reach after a 3D-noise warp of the query point (amplitudes 0.42 and 0.06 of the spacing at one and six cycles per spacing, as the flat warp). `swellAt` is the flat dynamic topography with 3D noise.
- **Euler vectors** (`setEuler`): ω_p = pole · v_p / (R √⟨sin² γ⟩_p) over the plate's pieces at the epoch's entry. `PlateSpeeds::Today` draws v_p as the flat history does (4–30 mm/yr); `PlateSpeeds::Earth` gives mix(65.5, 22.8, c_p) mm/yr × U(0.7, 1.3), c_p the continental share (Argus et al. 2011; Forsyth & Uyeda 1975).
- **Applied vectors** (`planet/fn/Rotate.cpp` `applyKinematics`, once per planet step): ω̃_p = activity · ω_p / (1 + resistance/JamScale)², the jam decaying over 30 Myr; then NNR from the pieces as they stand, `withoutNetRotation` solving M ω_net = Σ I_p ω̃_p exactly (3 × 3, Cramer), ω̂_p = ω̃_p − ω_net. `JamScale` is the flat 2.5e6 m·cells in m³ (× `PlanetCellArea`), retuned in stage 4.
- **Pieces** (`planet/type/Globe.hpp`): `CrustPiece{stock (m³ felsic incl. sediment, m³ basalt, double), column (float crust = felsic / area, the intensive fields), record, rotation Q, key, birth, area, plate}`; `PieceKey{step (-1 primordial), birth cell, order}`. Pieces stay in key order (`sortPieces`); new pieces append with a later step, dead ones are compacted out stably, so storage order is key order and no free list is needed (deviation from §4's free list: the same determinism, less state).
- **Primordial body** (`makeGlobe`): one piece per cell, Q = I, `primordialColumnAt` (the flat cooling with 3D noise at the flat noise's 4 194 km wavelength), basalt 7 km × area (`OceanicCrust`, White et al. 1992), marks from the closed-form past.
- **Rotation** (`rotatePieces`): one `rotationOf(ω̂_p, dt)` per plate composed on the left of Q and renormalized; `substepCount` = max(1, ⌈|v_rel| dt / shortest edge⌉). `materialOf(piece, x)` = R Q⁻¹ x, the 3D material point of S3m/S3e.
- **Coupling** (`couple`): per piece either exact (S3a `candidateCells` + `overlapArea`, the sum before normalisation required to 1e-9 of the area, a fatal `require` in every build) or cloud-in-cell (the piece as one cell of its centre's face coordinates over up to four cells of that face, clamped at the face edge, normalised to the area). Per-cell lists are in key order. `viewOf` normalises over the covered part; `dominantPiece` picks the largest weight.

## Stage 2: boundaries

- **Band** (`planet/fn/Band`): per step, from the coupling at its start. Core = cells with pieces of two plates within their ring (`Globe::rings`, edge and vertex neighbours); `sweep` = ⌈max_p |ω̂_p| R dt / shortest edge⌉ rings; deciding cells = core widened by sweep + 1, region = core widened by 2 · sweep + 3 (a ring more than sweep and footprint need, for the ring metric against the geodesic one), band pieces = the pieces listed in region cells. Only band pieces are coupled exactly in the substeps (`coupleBand`, sum to the area required to 1e-9); nothing is decided from cloud-in-cell weights. `requireBandHeld` is fatal if a piece outside the band reaches a deciding cell while it turns: after each substep's turn of the pieces outside the band, for those whose centre lay within sweep + 3 rings of a deciding cell at the step's start (`nearBand`), at its end position and back along its arc at points at most half the shortest edge apart (Codex DREV-CLAUDE-005-04: it used to check the start positions).
- **Substeps** (`planet/fn/Boundary.cpp` `advancePlates`): n = `refine` (1 in the history; 2 and 4 in test 14) × `substepCount` over the fastest relative motion of two plates met in a deciding cell or its ring. Each substep turns the band pieces, couples them exactly, consumes, then bears. Pieces outside the band turn by the same n rotations after the loop, so equal histories keep equal bits. Each substep carries its own date and length (`Substep`). At the end the consumed are dropped, births are put in key order (keys are unique, so a plain sort), and the globe is coupled again, exact for band pieces and cloud-in-cell elsewhere.
- **Union** (`geometry/fn/Union`): gnomonic tangent plane, Sutherland–Hodgman clip, Clipper2 `Union`/`NonZero` into a `PolyTree64` at 10¹² per plane unit, each contour's area as a signed fan of spherical excesses (van Oosterom & Strackee 1983), signs checked contour by contour (fatal). Pre-checks avoid the union: Σ < ½ a gives no and max ≥ ½ a gives yes (for consumption; a birth treats one overlap as closing only strictly above ½). Before the union, duplicate and collinear corners are trimmed and polygons with no area left are dropped (`TrimCollinear`): a sliver folded onto a shared edge made Clipper2 nest a whole neighbouring part as a hole on the level-7 history (fatal sign, +9e8 m²; `GlobeUnion.cpp checkSliver`).
- **Consumption** (`planet/fn/Consume.cpp`): a live band piece k that touches a deciding cell is a candidate when the pieces of overriding plates cover ½ of its footprint or more by union, measured in k's own tangent plane (the union of their footprints clipped to k; the same number as §4's Σ_c over cells). l overrides k only where the two plates close at k's centre (convergence > 0 with n̂ from k towards l) and either k sinks under l (`sinksUnder`: ocean under continent, the older plate's ocean under the younger's by `GlobeMotion::oceanAge`, the area mean of each plate's ocean age as the epoch begins (stage 2b), the higher plate index on a tie; continents never sink; a chain from ocean to ocean is a subduction, never a collision) or both are continents and k's plate arrives: its own velocity along n̂ is larger than l's against it, the lower plate index on a tie. Receiving plate = largest union over k, lower index on a tie; receivers in proportion to |k ∩ l|. Behind = up to 8 live pieces of k's plate stepping back along −v̂_p one piece size at a time. Sediment = min(felsic, cover thickness × area); room = max(0, StackLimit · area − felsic) in double (Codex DREV-CLAUDE-005-02: from the stock, not the float column).
- **Plan** (`planet/fn/Plan`): exactly §4 (chains to survivors with the kind decided between donor and final survivor; cycles defer their lowest key and restart the round; room reserved from nothing in every round in donor key order; the lowest-key unplaced donor cancelled, with what it could not place in that round as its `shortfall`; at most N + 1 rounds, else fatal). Each consumed piece is resolved once per round into its survivors with merged shares (`reach`, by survivor key, the receivers' order then the keys' as the fixed order of summation), so converging chains are not walked again: 21 layers of two candidates (42) resolve in one round, 84 transfers, well under 0.1 s, where walking every route is 2²¹ per donor. The A/B/C fixture settles in 3 rounds with nothing consumed.
- **Booking**: in transfer order. Subduct → interior (`ledger.subducted`, `interior`); Accrete → `addDeposit` with the facies of `Crossing.cpp:83`, `ledger.accreted`; Collide and Back → `giveFelsic`, the donor's cover as it stood before any transfer scaled by its share and by donor area / receiver area, `stackCover`, orogen marks, `resistance` of the donor's plate += the moved crust, `ledger.stacked`. Rounding left on a consumed piece goes to the interior and to `ledger.subducted`, so `interior.felsic = subducted` holds exactly. A cancelled donor adds its shortfall (the m³ it could not place in the round that cancelled it, once, after settlement, nothing of that round reserved) × substep / `PlanetStep` to its plate's `resistance`, so a nearly full receiver brakes little and the brake does not grow with the substep count. A piece born in the history and consumed within `ChurnAge` (10 Myr) of its `bornAt` counts as churn (`ledger.churned` m², `stats.churned`; primordial crust never). A candidate that pieces of two or more overriding plates cover counts in `stats.contested`. A donor without felsic crust stacks no cover. `ledger.consumedFelsic` sums the felsic stock of every consumed piece.
- **Births** (`planet/fn/Spread.cpp`): a deciding cell (ascending) is considered when the union of its live pieces (this substep's births included, `forLive`) leaves ½ of it or more uncovered, or when their sum is under 0.9 of it (a hole of the plates' lattice that straddles mantle cells). The two nearest plates are the nearest live band piece of each within two rings as the substep started (`forListed`: a birth of this substep would make its own plate the nearer for the next cell across the axis too), chord from the cell centre, lower key on a tie; they must open (convergence < 0 with n̂ along the chord from the nearer to the other). The plate whose edge there is older (`bornAt`) bears first, the nearer on a tie, then the other: a ridge's floor goes to its plates by turns. The piece continues that plate's lattice (`openSlot`): for each of the plate's pieces T over the cell and its ring, the cell beside T's birth cell across T's edge facing the gap, turned by T's Q, is a slot; a slot whose union leaves ½ or more of it open and that the plate's own pieces cover by under 2 % is taken, the most open first (lower key of T, then lower edge, on a tie); in an open cell with no such slot, the slot covering most of the cell among those still open by ¼ or more (`SlotFill`; one the other plate already covers leaves the cell to the weld). Slots whose overlaps sum to ½ or less are ranked by that sum, a bound of their union. The new piece has T's Q, that birth cell and its area. Only when neither plate has a slot and the cell is open does it weld: the cell moved along n̂ by the union of the nearer plate's pieces behind it (`weldTurn`; `stats.welded`). Key (step, decision cell, substep), `bornAt` the end of the substep (births follow its turn). Necking as the flat `openGap`: when the bearing plate's pieces across the cell's edges are continental (≥ ½ `ContinentalLimit`) and the other does not keep pace (v_q · v̂_p < ½ |v_p|), the piece takes 40 % of their felsic, basalt and cover, split by the length of the shared edge, and copies the intensive column of the widest; otherwise ocean floor: no felsic, 7 km × area of basalt from the interior, basement `Oceanic`, age 0. Marks reset as the flat history does. A giver to a neck gives once, with the summed length of its shared edges.
- **Cover** (`planet/fn/GlobeCover`): §4's local measures, from an exact coupling and the union of the pieces over every cell in its tangent plane: hole A − U, overlap Σ|P| − U, worst of each as a share of the cell, the counts over 0.1, 0.2 and 0.5, and the totals as shares of the sphere, optionally skipping cells (the band's deciding cells for "outside the band"). Tests 7 and 14 and `terra globe` read it; one number for all of them (Codex DREV-CLAUDE-005-02).

### Deviations from the design

- **New crust continues its plate's lattice.** §4 gives a birth the footprint of its cell, Q = I. At the ½ threshold that footprint overlaps its own plate's receding pieces by up to half a cell at every birth, for good, because both then move together (1.76 × the trench's area born and the sphere covered 1.38 times in 200 Myr). Moving the cell onto its plate's trailing edge (`weldTurn`, the first fix) still left the plate's pieces shifted against the mantle grid by fractions of a cell, and every birth left a hole or an overlap: 4.1–5.8 % of the sphere open and up to 7.8 % doubled by Codex's union (DREV-CLAUDE-005-01). A new piece therefore takes the slot next to a piece of its plate, with that piece's Q, so ridge crust tiles its plate exactly as Earth's oceanic lithosphere grows on at the ridge as one plate; the weld remains a fallback that the fixtures never reach. The birth cell is the slot's lattice cell, not the deciding cell; the key keeps the deciding cell.
- **Ridge floor goes to the plates by turns.** §4 gives the birth to the nearer plate. With lattice slots that lets the cell order decide every tie between the plates, and the axis walked by half a cell at each such birth: 2.3 cells off after 200 Myr. The plate whose edge is older bears first, so the two plates take turns; on the hemispheres each plate takes 48 / 52 % of the floor and the age gradient is 0.96 / 1.04 × 1/ω on the two sides. The 2 % moves the axis by up to 2.5 cells against the frame of the mean plate motion over 200 Myr; the test takes 3.
- **Ridges do not consume.** Overriding requires a closing contact at k's centre (§3's rule applied pairwise), so pieces sliding past at a ridge or a transform never consume each other. The transform runs show no churn at all.
- **Fabric** is not set on the sphere (the flat history writes a strike at stacking); S3m/S3e decide.
- **Holes in the band stay under half a cell.** Outside the band no cell is open or doubled at all (0 in every fixture). Within the deciding cells a gap stands open until it bears and a trench overlaps until it consumes, so §4's ½ A_c is the threshold itself: the worst hole is 0.12 on the ridge, 0.41 at the triple junctions and 0.41–0.48 on the oblique and vertex boundaries. Tests 7 and 14 gate worst < ½ and under 1 % of the sphere open or doubled in every run, the vertex boundary included.
- **Test 14's ordering**: the substeps adapt to the motion (Codex V4), so dt and dt/2 can run the same substep length and their difference is not asked to exceed that of dt/2 against dt/4. The test asserts that dt and dt/2 agree with dt/4 to 2 %, and controls the substep at dt directly (`refine` 1, 2, 4): the difference against ×4 must be under 2 % and must not grow from ×1 to ×2.
- **The axis is measured in the frame of the mean plate motion.** NNR adds a common turn as the plates' areas change (here the trench consumes one plate), so the ridge migrates over the mantle at the mean of the two vectors, as Earth's ridges do over hotspots; test 7 measures births against that frame.

## Stage 2b: reorganisations and the real generator

Codex DREV-CLAUDE-005-03/-04 ran the kinematics of the real generator (`makeGlobe(42, 5|6)`, 16 plates, today's speeds) and found that dealing the pieces to a new epoch's plates (`enterGlobeEpoch`) moves the old boundaries' holes and overlaps inside one plate, where nothing closed them (outside the band 0 → 0.50 of a cell on L5, 0.65 on L6, overlap 0.96), and that the band itself left cells over half open in the first epoch (0.55 on L5, 0.74 on L6). The lead decided (27 Nov): the gate is §4's cover slip, inside a plate in the plate's own frame; no remap, since the pieces' material identity is a design invariant. Probes: `.out/s3b/probe/{classify,epochs,holes,history,slots}.cpp`.

- **Where the defects sat** (L5, seed 42, step 100, `classify.cpp`): cells with two plates within their ring are 54 % of the sphere at 16 plates; holes were 3.5 % of their area alike at opening, sliding and closing contacts, overlaps 3.5–6 %; plate interiors had none.
- **Lattices** (`planet/fn/Interior`): pieces of one lattice share their rotation bit for bit (a slot copies its template's rotation; the pieces of a plate turn by the same rotations in the same order), so they tile without overlap; only pieces of two lattices of one plate can overlap (`sameLattice`). A plate dealt pieces of two old plates holds two lattices.
- **Gates in the plate's frame** (`interiorOpening`, `test/GlobeGaps.hpp`): no piece is half covered by other pieces of its own plate, and no slot of a piece's lattice (the cell across one of its birth cell's edges, turned by its rotation) is half open where no other plate is over the slot's mantle cell or its ring. A rigid plate keeps both whatever it crosses.
- **Seams close at a reorganisation** (`planet/fn/Seam`, `reorganisePlates`, called by `stepGlobe` in place of `enterGlobeEpoch`): the band of the ending epoch (sweep 1) is read from the coupling; the pieces are dealt; then, exactly coupled over that band, a piece that pieces of other lattices of its own plate cover by half or more is consumed by a transfer plan through the boundary's accounts (`applyPlan` in `planet/fn/Transfers`: ocean to the interior, crust onto the pieces over it within `StackLimit`, the rest to up to 8 pieces of its plate around it; `stats.sutured`), and every slot inside one plate next to the old deciding cells and open by half or more gets a new piece of that plate (`fillSlot`, `stats.fossil`); an old deciding cell where two new plates meet and that a suture left open by half takes a piece as at a closing contact (`fillCell`), so the new boundaries start under half open. As Earth's abandoned boundaries: a suture where crust doubled, the last spreading of a fossil ridge where it gaped. Sutures and fills repeat until neither changes anything, the band re-coupled each round so fossils become candidates (`SeamRounds` = 8; `stats.unsettled` counts closings that did not settle). A fossil's key is (step, cell, −1 − (round · 3 + n)), a boundary fill's (step, cell, −25 − round).
- **Seams close in the band too** (`sutureBand`, every substep after the births; lead, 27 Nov): a weld or birth over a seam that leaves a piece half covered by its own plate is sutured as at a reorganisation, the substep's own births included (their cells from `exactWeights`). A suture is no collision: it neither brakes nor marks an orogen (`applyPlan`'s `boundary`). Nothing holds a suture back, so it may open a cell; sutures follow every round of births and the births come again after a suture that consumed something, up to `BearRounds` = 6 rounds a substep (`stats.unsettledSubsteps` counts substeps that use them all; the key's order is (substep · 6 + round) · 3 + n).
- **A birth consumed in its own substep is gone** (`forLive` in `Band.hpp`): the substep's births were read without their dead mark, so a birth that a suture consumed still covered its slot for the next round.
- **Gaps inside a plate fill in its frame** (`bearCell` in `Spread.cpp`, `fillSlot` in `planet/fn/Lay`): a deciding cell first takes a piece in the most open interior slot near it, open by half or more, whose mantle cell is a deciding cell or next to one and whose two rings lie in the band's region (so every piece that may reach it is listed); a cell with one plate within two rings takes nothing else; a cell that looks covered is still looked at when two lattices of one plate meet over it, since a seam's overlaps can sum it to full beside a gap. The crust necks from the slot's continental neighbours, or the ocean floor is as old as the piece the slot continues. A seam that crosses a mantle cell diagonally opens it by up to √2 of the seam's width, and a weld there would double the plate.
- **Consumption never opens a cell by half** (`holdOpening` in `Consume.cpp`): candidates are taken in the band's order (pieces by key, then the step's births as born); one is held back for the substep when consuming it with those kept before it would leave a region cell under it less than half covered that was half covered with it; a cell already open, as a seam's gap at a trench, holds nothing. A held candidate waits until the overriding plate covers more (`stats.held`; not a cancellation, no brake).
- **Open cells fill at closing and sliding contacts** (`fillCell`): a deciding cell open by half or more whose two nearest plates close or slide gets a piece of the nearer plate in the most open slot of its lattice over the cell, else on the cell itself (`stats.filled`), with crust necked from continental neighbours or ocean floor as old as the nearest piece's when that is too thin to neck (deviation from §4's "no ocean of age 0 without opening": the hole is the trench's rounding, not new floor, so the fill copies the age).
- **A cell may take three births in a substep** (`BirthsPerCell`): the most open slot near a deciding cell can graze it, and the oblique ridge of test 14 then stood 0.97 open at the end of a step. The key's order is (substep · BearRounds + round) · 3 + the birth's number. Taking the slot that covers most of the cell instead walked the ridge's axis by 3.8 cells in 200 Myr (2.98 now; gate 3). Test 14 gates the worst hole every 16 Myr of every run.

### Deviations from §4

- **Inside a reorganised plate the gate is in the plate's frame**, not per mantle cell. - **The cohesion gate over the whole history** is proposed, not set (lead). ## Stage 3: surface and view

- **History** (`planet/fn/GlobeEvolve`): `evolveGlobe(seed, time, level, speeds, workers)` = `startGlobe`, `stepGlobe` per planet step, `finishGlobe`, in the order of the flat `evolvePlanet`: epoch, strata retire and bury per piece, water, `advancePlates`, contacts, arcs/rifts/plumes, flow of thick crust, ocean age, elevation, the sea every 4 steps, the climate every 16, surface processes, marks at every region anchor. The first epoch is the one at TectonicsStart, as in the flat history. `stepGlobe` first calls `restoreKeyOrder` (a check; a store out of key order is sorted and coupled exactly). The water inventory is the flat 4 200 m column × U(0.95, 1.05) over 4πR², not over PlanetSpan², so the globe holds the same column of water (1.81 × the flat world's m³). Serial apart from the climate solve, which runs on the flat view.
- **Contacts** (`planet/fn/GlobeContacts`): owner = plate of the dominant piece (0xffff on a bare cell). Sources are the cells with an edge neighbour of another owner, the fastest-closing one where several meet; a multi-source spread over the rings carries each cell's source and the geodesic distance to it, up to ContactReach + 2 √A. The contact distance is that less half the source cell, the normal is the tangent towards the source, convergence and shear come from the epoch's Euler vectors at its ramps, not slowed by the jam, as the flat contacts (`Outline.cpp`, `Contacts.cpp`), so a jammed collision still builds its arc; NNR, a common turn, does not change a relative motion. Arcs, rifts and plumes have the flat numbers: an arc where the cell's own side is the overriding one (looked up across the contact at 2 d + √A), a rift on a continental cell closer than √A to an opening contact; both erupt only into the pieces of the cell's own plate, by their weights over what they cover, and write the flat strike atan2(n_x, −n_z) of the contact's normal in the flat world (`flatStrikeOf`) as `fabric`, as stacking does with the strike between donor and receiver; 12 plumes per 150 Myr era at hashed points of the sphere fixed in the mantle frame, 1 800 m × the flat cell's area per step × vigour. Every eruption books into the pieces over the cell by weight (`forPiecesOver`) and debits the interior; a bare cell erupts nothing. Thick crust flows as one conservative flux per edge of the mantle grid, 0.24 dt / PlanetStep × mobility × min(PlanetCellArea · edge / distance, the smaller cell) per metre of difference.
- **Surface** (`planet/fn/GlobeSurface`): the flat `shapeSurface` with real areas, the cells round a cell and geodesic slopes; loads are m³ and settle over three hops; erosion strips cover and exhumes through the pieces; carbonate is shared by shelf room and warmth (|latitude| < 45°). What reaches a bare cell or leaves at the last hop goes to the interior, so no mass is lost. The sea is solved exactly: cells sorted by height, the piecewise linear volume walked.
- **Heat** (`planet/fn/GlobeHeat`): the flat melt heat per cell's view, spread by the flat [¼ ½ ¼]² kernel read on the ring (centre ¼, edges ⅛, corners 1/16), weighted by area and normalised.
- **Flat view** (`planet/fn/FlatView`, §8): G(x, z) = (cos φ cos λ, cos φ sin λ, sin φ) with φ = latitudeAt(z), λ = 2πx / PlanetSpan; J = diag(k cos φ, k northwardZ(z)), k = 2πR / PlanetSpan = 2.386. Each flat cell takes the dominant piece's column and record from the mantle cell under G(centre), and the cell's views of elevation (the one the sea was solved on, so the view's shore is the globe's), swell, heat, erosion, deposition, temperature and rain. Plate velocities are the mean over the plate's flat cells of J⁻¹ v weighted by cos φ; contact normals Jᵀ n normalised, distances along the normal by J⁻¹; resistance per flat cell area. `readClimateBack` samples temperature and rain linearly at G⁻¹ of every cell centre on sheet A.
- **`terra globe`** (`tool/GlobeCommand.cpp`, `tool/GlobeRocks.cpp`): `--seed --age --level (7) --speeds today|earth --out`. The numbers of `terra planet` by the real cell areas, then continental crust (share of area with ≥ `OceanicLimit`, volume), ocean volume and mean depth, RMS plate speed from the applied vectors, the share of boundary that closes, opens or slides, cover (footprints / sphere; §4's local hole A − U and overlap Σ|P| − U from a union in every cell outside the band, worst and the share over 0.1 of a cell), the boundary statistics, the wall time of each phase, the ledger and the balance against the primordial body, rock and basement shares of the land (flat samples weighted by cos φ, the area each stands for), the straight-shore share of the view (`measure/fn/Regularity`, the flat history's 37 %) and the grain of the shore on the sphere: the share of shore cells whose Sobel direction of elevation lies within ±5° of the face's grid lines (11 % for a shore with no preferred direction, §11 test 12). PNGs `globe-{relief,plates,rain}.png` from the flat view.
- **Tests**: 10 (`GlobeDeterminism.cpp`, serial): the same history twice and a history whose store was shuffled are bit for bit the same (pieces member by member, since `CrustColumn` and strata slots carry padding; fields; sea; ledger). 11 (`GlobeBridge.cpp`): φ(G) = latitudeAt, a point and its mirror across a pole are one point, east is +x with no z, (J⁻¹v)·(Jᵀn) = v · n to 1e-12, all vectors finite, |v_x| ≤ 111 |v| / k, vectors across both folds mirrored, J against a finite difference of G (10 m, to 1e-5 k), the view's contacts closing where the globe's do and, within 30° of the equator, pointing at the other plate (> 90 %), constant fields constant both ways. ### Deviations

- **Degenerate contours of a union** (`geometry/fn/Union.cpp`): the L7 history met an outer contour of −2e-8 m², the rounding of its fan. The sign is checked only above 1e-14 R² per vertex (0.4 m²).
- **Test 11's 111**: the cell centre nearest a pole is at 89.484375°, cos φ = 0.0089995, so the bound is 111.12 |v| / k; the test takes 111.2.
- **Cloud-in-cell at cube vertices stays**: surface writes go to the pieces a cell sees, over its covered part, so mass is conserved where the weights do not reach across a face edge; only where the mass lands moves by up to a cell there.
