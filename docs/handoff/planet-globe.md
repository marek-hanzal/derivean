## Stage 1: kinematics and pieces

- **Epochs** (`tectonics/fn/Euler`): `eulerEpochAt` takes index, start, end and seed from the flat `epochAt` of the same world, so both histories reorganise together. n = lattice² sites (9 to 25) on a Fibonacci lattice (González 2010), turned at random per epoch and jittered by up to 0.4 of the site spacing; reach 0.62–1.55; pole uniform on the sphere. `plateAt` is the weighted Voronoi by angular distance / reach after a 3D-noise warp of the query point (amplitudes 0.42 and 0.06 of the spacing at one and six cycles per spacing, as the flat warp). `swellAt` is the flat dynamic topography with 3D noise.
- **Euler vectors** (`setEuler`): ω_p = pole · v_p / (R √⟨sin² γ⟩_p) over the plate's pieces at the epoch's entry. `PlateSpeeds::Today` draws v_p as the flat history does (4–30 mm/yr); `PlateSpeeds::Earth` gives mix(65.5, 22.8, c_p) mm/yr × U(0.7, 1.3), c_p the continental share (Argus et al. 2011; Forsyth & Uyeda 1975).
- **Applied vectors** (`planet/fn/Rotate.cpp` `applyKinematics`, once per planet step): ω̃_p = activity · ω_p / (1 + resistance/JamScale)², the jam decaying over 30 Myr; then NNR from the pieces as they stand, `withoutNetRotation` solving M ω_net = Σ I_p ω̃_p exactly (3 × 3, Cramer), ω̂_p = ω̃_p − ω_net. `JamScale` is the flat 2.5e6 m·cells in m³ (× `PlanetCellArea`), retuned in stage 4.
- **Pieces** (`planet/type/Globe.hpp`): `CrustPiece{stock (m³ felsic incl. sediment, m³ basalt, double), column (float crust = felsic / area, the intensive fields), record, rotation Q, key, birth, area, plate}`; `PieceKey{step (-1 primordial), birth cell, order}`. Pieces stay in key order (`sortPieces`); new pieces append with a later step, dead ones are compacted out stably, so storage order is key order and no free list is needed (deviation from §4's free list: the same determinism, less state).
- **Primordial body** (`makeGlobe`): one piece per cell, Q = I, `primordialColumnAt` (the flat cooling with 3D noise at the flat noise's 4 194 km wavelength), basalt 7 km × area (`OceanicCrust`, White et al. 1992), marks from the closed-form past.
- **Rotation** (`rotatePieces`): one `rotationOf(ω̂_p, dt)` per plate composed on the left of Q and renormalized; `substepCount` = max(1, ⌈|v_rel| dt / shortest edge⌉). `materialOf(piece, x)` = R Q⁻¹ x, the 3D material point of S3m/S3e.
- **Coupling** (`couple`): per piece either exact (S3a `candidateCells` + `overlapArea`, the sum before normalisation required to 1e-9 of the area, a fatal `require` in every build) or cloud-in-cell (the piece as one cell of its centre's face coordinates over up to four cells of that face, clamped at the face edge, normalised to the area). Per-cell lists are in key order. `viewOf` normalises over the covered part; `dominantPiece` picks the largest weight.

## Stage 2: boundaries

- **Band** (`planet/fn/Band`): per step, from the coupling at its start. Core = cells with pieces of two plates within their ring (`Globe::rings`, edge and vertex neighbours); `sweep` = ⌈max_p |ω̂_p| R dt / shortest edge⌉ rings; deciding cells = core widened by sweep + 1, region = core widened by 2 · sweep + 3 (a ring more than sweep and footprint need, for the ring metric against the geodesic one), band pieces = the pieces listed in region cells. Only band pieces are coupled exactly in the substeps (`coupleBand`, sum to the area required to 1e-9); nothing is decided from cloud-in-cell weights. `requireBandHeld` is fatal at the step's end if a piece outside the band has its centre cell or a cell of its ring among the deciding cells.
- **Substeps** (`planet/fn/Boundary.cpp` `advancePlates`): n = `substepCount` over the fastest relative motion of two plates met in a deciding cell or its ring. Each substep turns the band pieces, couples them exactly, consumes, then bears. Pieces outside the band turn by the same n rotations after the loop, so equal histories keep equal bits. Each substep carries its own date and length (`Substep`). At the end the consumed are dropped, births are put in key order (keys are unique, so a plain sort), and the globe is coupled again, exact for band pieces and cloud-in-cell elsewhere.
- **Union** (`geometry/fn/Union`): gnomonic tangent plane, Sutherland–Hodgman clip, Clipper2 `Union`/`NonZero` into a `PolyTree64` at 10¹² per plane unit, each contour's area as a signed fan of spherical excesses (van Oosterom & Strackee 1983), signs checked contour by contour (fatal). Pre-checks avoid the union: Σ < ½ a gives no and max ≥ ½ a gives yes.
- **Consumption** (`planet/fn/Consume.cpp`): a live band piece k that touches a deciding cell is a candidate when the pieces of overriding plates cover ½ of its footprint or more by union, measured in k's own tangent plane (the union of their footprints clipped to k; the same number as §4's Σ_c over cells). l overrides k only where the two plates close at k's centre (convergence > 0 with n̂ from k towards l) and either k sinks under l (`sinksUnder`: ocean under continent, older ocean under younger, the higher plate index on a tie; continents never sink; a chain from ocean to ocean is a subduction, never a collision) or both are continents and k's plate arrives: its own velocity along n̂ is larger than l's against it, the lower plate index on a tie. Receiving plate = largest union over k, lower index on a tie; receivers in proportion to |k ∩ l|. Behind = up to 8 live pieces of k's plate stepping back along −v̂_p one piece size at a time. Sediment = min(felsic, cover thickness × area); room = (StackLimit − crust) × area.
- **Plan** (`planet/fn/Plan`): exactly §4 (chains to survivors with the kind decided between donor and final survivor; cycles defer their lowest key and restart the round; room reserved from nothing in every round in donor key order; the lowest-key unplaced donor cancelled; at most N + 1 rounds, else fatal). The A/B/C fixture settles in 3 rounds with nothing consumed.
- **Booking**: in transfer order. Subduct → interior (`ledger.subducted`, `interior`); Accrete → `addDeposit` with the facies of `Crossing.cpp:83`, `ledger.accreted`; Collide and Back → `giveFelsic`, the donor's cover as it stood before any transfer scaled by its share and by donor area / receiver area, `stackCover`, orogen marks, `resistance` of the donor's plate += the moved crust, `ledger.stacked`. Rounding left on a consumed piece goes to the interior and to `ledger.subducted`, so `interior.felsic = subducted` holds exactly. A cancelled donor adds its felsic stock × substep / `PlanetStep` to its plate's `resistance`, so the brake does not grow with the substep count. A donor without felsic crust stacks no cover. `ledger.consumedFelsic` sums the felsic stock of every consumed piece.
- **Births** (`planet/fn/Spread.cpp`): a deciding cell (ascending) is born into when the union of its live pieces leaves ½ of it or more uncovered and the two nearest plates (nearest live band piece of each within two rings, chord from the cell centre, lower key on a tie) open: convergence < 0 with n̂ along the chord from p's to q's nearest piece. The piece belongs to the nearer plate p, key (step, cell, substep). Necking as the flat `openGap`: when p's pieces across the cell's edges are continental (≥ ½ `ContinentalLimit`) and q does not keep pace (v_q · v̂_p < ½ |v_p|), the piece takes 40 % of their felsic, basalt and cover, split by the length of the shared edge, and copies the intensive column of the widest; otherwise ocean floor: no felsic, 7 km × area of basalt from the interior, basement `Oceanic`, age 0. Marks reset as the flat history does. The union of a cell also counts the pieces born earlier in the same substep (by exact overlap with the cell when their centre is within 3 √A), and a giver to a neck gives once, with the summed length of its shared edges.

### Deviations from the design

- **New crust welds onto its plate.** §4 gives a birth the footprint of its cell, Q = I. At the ½ threshold that footprint overlaps its own plate's receding pieces by up to half a cell at every birth, for good, because both then move together: on the two hemispheres it bore 1.76 × what the trench consumed and the footprints covered the sphere 1.38 times after 200 Myr, so test 7 failed by its own numbers. A new piece is therefore born with Q = a turn of its cell along n̂, away from its own plate, by the share of the cell p's live pieces behind it still cover (centres on the far side of n̂), as a length across the cell (`weldTurn`). For a straight trailing edge the footprint is flush; for a ragged one it leaves about as much gap as overlap. Bisection to zero overlap was tried and left holes (coverage 0.9895); counting p's pieces beside the cell along the ridge over-shifted. The material frame of a new piece is its turned birth cell; nothing else reads Q at birth.
- **Ridges do not consume.** Overriding requires a closing contact at k's centre (§3's rule applied pairwise), so pieces sliding past at a ridge or a transform never consume each other. The transform runs show no churn at all.
- **Fabric** is not set on the sphere (the flat history writes a strike at stacking); S3m/S3e decide.
- **Holes outside the band exceed half a cell.** §4 and test 14 ask the worst hole outside the band < ½ A_c. A birth leaves its cell open by under half, but a hole split across two mantle cells at its birth later turns over one cell whole: the worst share is 0.46–0.65 at L5 (exact weights, a lower bound), with and without births seeing each other, and 2–4.6 % of the cells outside the band are open by a fifth or more (§4's third measure). The tests take < 0.75 and < 6 %; the long run reports both.
- **Test 14's ordering**: the substeps adapt to the motion (Codex V4), so dt and dt/2 can run the same substep length and their difference is not asked to exceed that of dt/2 against dt/4. The test asserts that dt and dt/2 agree with dt/4 to 2 %.

### Open

- **Cube vertices**: a boundary along face diagonals over-covers by 1.2–1.8 % in 192 Myr (births there bear 2.5–3.8 % more than the trench consumes); the cells are staircase-shaped against the boundary and the weld is area-neutral only on average. Watch it in the 4.4 Gyr run of stage 3/4.
- **Cloud-in-cell at cube vertices**: the weights stay on the face of the piece's centre, so a mantle cell across a face edge near a cube vertex may see up to 0.65 of itself unweighted although exact footprints cover it (measured on the hemispheres). Views normalise over the covered part, but surface writes through `bookCell` (stage 3) go only to the pieces a cell sees. Stage 3 should decide whether cells near the vertices take exact weights.
- **Not covered by tests yet**: the dye's second moment and the Eulerian comparison of test 5 (needs the surface processes, stage 3), test 4's three gates of §5 over the whole history (stage 3), coverage over 4.4 Gyr on seed 42 (`terra globe`, stage 3/4), the triple-junction and tie fixtures of test 7 through `consumeInBand` itself (they are tested on `planTransfers` and `unionArea`), and determinism over thread counts (stage 5; everything is serial now).

### Review of stage 2

The independent reviewer's findings, all handled in the fix commit after 2e9d7e9: the band is checked at every step's end (`requireBandHeld`) and its region widened by a ring; a cancelled donor brakes by substep; test 7 compares the interior with the consumed felsic less the wedge instead of with `subducted`, which it equals by construction; test 5 asserts that every piece whose stock did not change kept its record, in the band or not; test 14 asserts the holes per run and reports the larger gap; ocean-to-ocean chains subduct; a donor without felsic crust stacks no cover; births see the births of their substep; a neck giver gives once; substeps carry their own date; sorting by unique key; two comments that claimed more than the code does. Churn of test 14 (pieces born and consumed within 10 Myr) is not counted: pieces carry no birth time in the ledger; the transform's zero births and consumptions bound it there.
