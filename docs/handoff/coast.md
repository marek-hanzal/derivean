## 1. Goal

Own `@simulation/src/coast/**` (+ `test/Coast*.cpp`, `tool/Coast*.cpp`): shores of seas and lakes that arise from simulated waves working on what the land offers, at the Region, Local and Site levels. ## 5. Decisions and why

- **One hook after `cutAndFill`.** Rivers deliver first, then waves rework what they brought. The face above a wave-cut foot is not failed by coast code: the next step's `breakSlopes`/`settleDebris` (landscape) do that.
- **Waves as eight trains** (one per neighbour direction), state = H² in m². Carried cell to cell downwind (`sweepDownwind`), grown along the fetch-limited curve of the water's depth (SPM 1984 eq. 3-39: H = L·tanh(H_deep/L), L the highest sea the depth holds under that wind, H_deep the SMB deep-water growth `FetchGrowth·U²` per metre; each cell re-enters the curve at its own depth from the deep-water height that stands for the fetch behind), capped at the fully developed sea, cut by depth (`H ≤ 0.5·depth at high water`). A sea higher than L (swell from deeper water) loses height to the bed (`dH/dx = −0.0106·H²/h²` times the intermediate-depth share of linear theory for a wind sea of steepness 0.04; a fifth on a loose bed) down to L, not below. Rejected: deep-water growth plus friction everywhere (it double-counts the bed losses SPM's shallow curves already contain and gave shallow lakes 60 % of measured heights). Land lets nothing through, so shelter and exposure by the angle of the coast to the wind need no extra rule; a 20 % blend from each side stands for refraction and directional spread. Rejected: ray tracing per shore cell (cost, and no natural boundary from a parent level).
- **Wind**: every quarter gets `StormGain·(|W|·cos² + StormCalm)`; trains are not weighted by frequency.
- **Still water** (`spreadWater`): breadth-first from cells the flood left wet, through every cell covered at high water (`level + swing`), plus the land cells next to them. `swing` is `SpringTide` (1 m) for the sea and 0.25 m for a lake. `Shore::stood` is captured once per step because `Domain::level` of a cell the coast lowers is stale (it would read as a lake at the old cliff top).
- **Rock**: threshold `Hc = 0.02·MPa·sound·(1 − 0.85·weakness)/quality` (Sunamura's force balance ρgHc ≈ 2·10⁻⁴·strength; rejected `0.063·√MPa`, which gave hard rock platforms as wide as soft rock's), rate `0.02·(H − Hc)/Hc²` m/yr per train, averaged over the eight. `Shore::notch` is the cut-back of a column's foot; at cell width the column comes down to the bench (`still − ½·swing`, Region 2 m deeper). Self-limiting: a bench makes the waves break (`Breaker`) and lose height to its bed, so retreat stops at a width set by rock and waves, independent of the number of steps. At coarse levels the bench narrower than a cell lives in `notch` (sub-cell platform width). Time per step is `min(step, StandTime = 6 kyr)`, cut in `CutSlices = 8` slices; a face cut through before its share of time is spent comes down and hands the rest of the time to the ground it laid bare (waves raised again between slices), so a weak face goes back by several cells in a step and a strong one by none.
- **Mud** of a fallen column is suspended after the waves are raised again: the cloud walks down the bed (across a level bench, away from where it rose), sinks a share only in calm water, settles whole in a calm hollow and otherwise leaves the domain (`CoastLedger.left`). Rejected: laying it at the first local minimum, which buried the new bench.
- **Sea caves** only where the face is being cut (`notch ≥ NotchLeast`), on master joints (spacing 8–60 m), as long as `min(CaveLongest, CaveReach·ln(struck/withstand), faster·notch)`, where `faster` is how much faster the crushed joint goes back than the face. Rejected: a hash share of joints (a stamp).
- **Standing water** is judged against `Domain::surface` (the ground of the flood), and `spreadWater` does not cross into dry ground lower than the water's low stand (beyond its sill): a channel cut after the flood is not a lake, and a lake's level does not run down its outlet.
- **Debris** of a fallen column goes to `Shore::pending` (surf debris, not in the height field), blocks ground down by the rock's `abrasion` over `SurfTravel`. Rejected: putting it on the cell as cover — a 60 m column would leave a 50 m rubble pile, while in nature the debris is removed far faster than a face crosses a cell.
- **Sand**: per train, a load is carried downwind through cells within reach (closure depth 2.5·H); capacity per year by CERC for the train's height (`DriftRate·E^1.25`), the share of the year a train works (`StormShare`) and the sweep's years, spread over the surf zone out to closure depth on Dean's shoreface; excess is laid (not above high water, nor above still water on a stream), deficit is picked up (`Lift` of the capacity per cell, a bed not dug deeper under the cell ahead than sand holds); a class may not climb a slope steeper than it holds (`profileSlope`, Beach.cpp: the beach face above the step, the equilibrium shoreface below, fines a flat), the blocked part is turned to the two cells 45° to either side — that is longshore drift; at the shore the swash lays sand then gravel on their faces up to the run-up height (the berm). Undertow moves a class back down where it lies steeper than it holds; fines only ever move that way. Rejected: pairwise slope relaxation (anti-diffusive for slopes below equilibrium, one cell per round), and a full CEM shoreline tracer (too large).
- **Time in the sand**: each sweep stands for `SweepYears` (25 yr) and a step takes as many as its years hold, at most `MostRounds` (8); the waves are raised again between rounds, so a bank cut back in one lets the next reach further. Rejected: a fixed number of sweeps per level with rates scaled by its share of the step (a shore then followed its step count: 0.56 against 0.98 m³/m/yr at 20 and 40 steps).
- **Banks of loose ground** go back at the rate of wave impact, 4·Cs·(R − z_toe)²/T per wave (Larson, Erikson and Hanson 2004; Cs = 1.7·10⁻⁴), cut to the foot and on into the bank across ground already cut down within `ScarpReach` (32 m); what the train cannot carry waits in the surf at the foot. Rejected: undercut bounded by the train's spare capacity at the last cell (its wave there is the broken swash, so banks went back a cell a step whatever the waves), and a cut down to the beach face over the cell before (at 4 m cells that stands above the run-up, so the swash never reached the next cell).
- **Square waves still fill a bay** (spit test). The eight trains are the storm seas of separate quarters, each in its own time (`StormShare`). With the prevailing wind square to the shore, the two side quarters' storms blow at 0.61 of the oblique storm, 2.5·(7·½ + 2) against 2.5·(7 + 2) m/s, so a fetch-limited sea has 0.61^2.5 ≈ 0.3 of its drift; one of them drives the western sand into the bay. That is gross drift under a climate with no net drift (Komar 1998; a shoreline under it diffuses, Ashton and Murray 2006), not a grid artefact or exchange across the shore. Measured 0.35 of the oblique fill, which is held by supply as much as by capacity; the check bounds it to 0.1–0.4. Rejected: netting the trains' alongshore thrust per cell (Longuet-Higgins 1970 for one sea). It cut the fill to 0.01 but treats separate storms as one sea (review).
- **Streams keep their channels** (`findStreams`, `Shore::stream`): where running water keeps the bed open the waves lay nothing above still water, and `keepChannels` returns to the surf the sand and gravel they laid there in a step (the mud goes with the current, `CoastLedger.left`). Marked: dry cells of a river the coarser level draws (`FlowScratch::gathered` at least the parent level's `channelHeadOf`, the Region its own; the test by which a Site keeps its parent's rivers, Fluvial.cpp); a lake's sill and the channel below it (dry cells whose flood ground lies at most `Film` above a lake's still water and that pass a channel of the level), whatever the lake's basin; and from a sill the path under the lake within `ApproachReach` (64 m, at least a cell; a calibration) while it carries half the sill's water (a heuristic for the outlet's main branch). Only a lake's outlet raises its water when barred: river mouths are not followed into standing water, and the sea has no sill. With waves only (`workCoast`) the lake scene holds at 400.00 m (400.02 with the waves off); with dry cells only it rose 4.9 m, with a depth bound on the approach instead of distance 1.8 m (the one-cell path moves onto cells the bound leaves to the waves). The straight sea scene at Site has no marks under water (review counted 85–111 with river mouths followed). Rejected: the level's own head (400 m³/yr at Site, a rill the swash buries), all routed paths through a lake (stripes on the shallows), a count of cells of the cell's own runoff (`StreamCells`).
- **`Domain::trend` is moved by what waves cut and lay** (in `book`, `Shore.cpp`). Without it a finer level's found uplift (`landscape/fn/Uplift.cpp`) would heal a bench or a spit back onto the parent's surface. Approved by the lead.
- **Drift at cells that do not evolve**: the train lifts from their bed what it lifts from any bed (`bedLift`) and drops what it cannot carry, their ground unchanged, and loads pass between them. Rejected: capacity × loose bed in and everything out (edge cells laid 2.8× what inner ones did), and a pure pass-through (a sink at every edge).
- **Beach profile** (`fn/Beach.cpp`): face tanβ = 0.12·(H_b·s/(2πD))^−¼ (Sunamura 1984 with the period of a wind sea of steepness s = 0.04; sand 0.5 mm, gravel 20 mm from the cover materials), capped at 0.2 for sand and 0.4 for gravel; it holds down to the step (depth = ⅓·H_b; at H_b the steep face covered a Local surf zone and drowned the spit test), below it min(face, ⅔·A^1.5/√depth) with Dean's A = 0.067·w^0.44 (Ferguson–Church fall velocity); mud a flat. Run-up R2% of Stockdon 2006 with L0 = H_b/s, at most `HighestBerm`. H_b is `Shore::crest`, not the local height: at the waterline cell the train is already broken, and its period is the one it had offshore. A train crossing ground above high water reaches as high as the run-up of its own broken height on that face: with the breakers' run-up the trains swept over every low bar and the spit test's sand never emerged. Rejected: fixed swash slopes per class (no dependence on waves), and the local height for the face and berm (gave faces too steep and run-up 30–40 % short).
- **`recordShore`** raises the final waves on a working copy: the domain it gets is the caller's, and every copy of a `Domain` shares its `Shore`.
- **Traps**: every border cell of a domain must be `Rim` (`raiseWaves` reads three neighbours of every other cell). `Domain` copies share one `Shore` (`shared_ptr`). `workShores` returns before allocating anything when no water stands.

## 7. How to build, run and verify

```sh
mise -C @simulation run build     # library, terra, tests (build directory is shared with other streams)
.out/simulation/build/macosx/arm64/release/terra coast --scene straight|bay|lake ...   # hand-built shores, map + view
.out/simulation/build/macosx/arm64/release/terra coast --find 1 [--tries 4] [--pick lake1,cliff2,sea1] [--confirm 0] --out .out/coast/find
.out/simulation/build/macosx/arm64/release/terra coast --x m --z m --level 2|3 --tiles 2 --out dir
```

`--find` ranks lowland sea coasts (`sea`), steep ones (`cliff`) and lakes at least 50 m deep (`lake`) on the Region within 300 km of the landing, then for each picked one surveys 2 × 2 Local tiles, 2 × 2 Site tiles at its best sand beach (and highest cliff) and renders a picture from the water and one from the shore (`view-<tag>-<kind>-{water,shore}.png`). A fresh process rebuilds the planet and the Region (3–8 min under load).

The coast switched off, for comparisons, without a flag in shared code: compile `Shore.cpp` with `-DworkShores=workShoresOn` and `Seas.cpp` with `-DreadSeas=readSeasOn` and link a stub that defines both empty. Seams per region of a mosaic: `seamsOf` (`measure/fn/Seams.hpp`) reads dry cells only; for the shore band and for `ShoreRecord::laid` by distance to the tile edge use a scratch driver over `gatherMosaic` and `world.atlas.find`. A scratch link must use a library copy built from the same headers as the objects linked before it, or it crashes on layout differences.

`simulation-tests` runs the tests whose name contains any of its arguments, ignoring case (`simulation-tests coast`, `simulation-tests intervention`, about 5 min under load); with none it runs them all.

When the shared library lags the headers (another stream mid-edit), compile the whole of `src/` into a scratch library (`mk-full.sh` in the scratch directory: `xargs -P 10` over `src/**/*.cpp`, then `ar`; about 20 s). Fast loop without touching the shared build: compile `src/coast/fn/*.cpp` (and `tool/*.cpp` for terra) into a scratch directory and link them before a copy of `libderivean-simulation.a`; the coast tests need a `main` calling `testCoast()` linked with `test/Coast.cpp test/CoastVoid.cpp test/LandscapeFixture.cpp`.

## 8. Open questions

- Wave spread (`WaveSpread`, 20 % to each side per cell) is a per-cell blend, so the lateral spread per metre depends on the cell size (independent review, 26 Nov). A directional spread in angle would need the trains' energy to be moved by distance, not by cells.
- The rock threshold is linear in strength; the constant 0.02 m/MPa is pinned at chalk (unchanged there) and not checked against a published value. The rate term `(H − Hc)/Hc²` is the model's own. `openCaves` (SeaVoid.cpp) reads intact strength × sound without the weakness and quality terms of `withstandOf`, and `withstandOf` does not clamp the rock-mass factor as `landscape/fn/Stability.cpp` does (review).
- `Submerged` (1.65) and `Kinematic` (1e-6 m²/s) in `Beach.cpp` belong with the shared physical constants (quartz density is also `GrainDensity` in `aeolian/constant/Wind.hpp`); the class-to-material table `CoverRock` is read from `groundwater/fn/Conductivity.hpp` but belongs with `SedimentClass` (review).
- `WaveQuantum` caps recorded heights at 12.75 m; a fully developed sea under a 10 m/s mean wind is higher. Only the Region's open rims can reach it.
- `StormShare` (1 % of the year per train) is reasoned from a Rayleigh wind (a year moves what 6–16 % of it at the storm height would, over eight quarters), not calibrated against measured drift; it sets every sand rate. Cs = 1.7·10⁻⁴ is the value of Larson, Erikson and Hanson (2004, Marine Geology 211) as used again by Larson et al. (2009); the review checked it there. It was fitted to laboratory and field dune erosion under storm surge, not to lake banks.
- `Lift` (a tenth of the capacity per cell) makes a drift fill up over about ten cells whatever their size: 40 m at the Site level, 640 m at Local. A shoreline adapts over a length set by its diffusivity and time, not by cells.
- At rates per year the drift is mostly supply-limited: the capacity a sweep has over a cell is tens to hundreds of metres of bed, so shapes follow the geometric rules (slopes held, swash, strand) more than the capacity. The Region's 750-yr sweeps are coarse for that.
- `ApproachReach` (64 m) and the half-of-the-sill flow that a path under water must carry are calibrations on the lake scene; at the Region the reach is one cell, 1024 m.
- **Lake scene in the full step loop (open, review).** With `replayCoast` (rivers and slopes too) the lake scene's lake rises with the waves off as well: 400.84 m after one step, 404.6 m after 30 (its outlet is a dead-flat channel at lake level some 600 m long, which the rivers fill). With the waves on it rises further: 402.0 after one step, 409.1 after 30. The level is then set far down that channel (150 cells from the lake), on sand the river brought down from the beach the waves built at its mouth and could not carry on over no slope; the marks of `findStreams` do not reach there. Not followed further: a scene with an outlet of real slope, or a measure of what the river takes from the beach at the outlet, would tell a defect of the coast from one of the scene.
- Drift lifted from Settled and Rim cells stands for the shore beyond; such a cell never runs out, so it is a steady source at `Lift·capacity` per sweep where a Free cell would deplete. It is booked in `CoastLedger.entered`, not in `TileLedger.sedimentIn` (which is the rivers'). Across tiles this is a boundary condition, not conserved mass.
- Glacial low stands of the sea are not in the finer levels' sea level; drowned valleys exist only where the Region's dated sea level rose.
- Survey: `platformWidth` takes the shortest platform run over a shore cell's wet sides, which along a shore-parallel strip can read kilometres (the "widest" figure); p50/p95 are the trustworthy ones.
