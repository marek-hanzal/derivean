## 5. Decisions and why

- **Cubes rule** (`@engine/src/horizon/type/CubeChunk.hpp`): a cube is solid if the world is solid at its centre. Rejected: mean height (shaves crests), "any solid" (fattens). The top cube shows the surface cover however thin; under water a column ends strictly below the level and the dry column beside it at or above it; a column counts as under water only if all eight neighbours know a level (else the carried level ran out and an open edge appeared).
- **Clipmap, not quadtree**: every level is a square of columns of chunks round the eye and is whole under the finer levels too. That costs a third more chunks and removes holes by construction: a missing or slow level just leaves the coarser one showing. Boxes per level are sent every frame and shrink to what is held.
- **Reach 128 cubes** (`FarCubes::reach`, `WorldOptions.cubeReach` 64–256): a cube is 1/128 rad at the side of its box, 5–7 px at 900–1350 px of height. 192 would be about 4 px but more than doubles the faces. Finest far level 0.5 m (level 2), coarsest 1024 m (level 13) stretched to 260 km instead of a 2 km level.
- **Chunks are 32 cubes on every axis** because the renderer's packed quad has 6-bit coordinates. A column of chunks is the unit of building and swapping; its water sheets ride on its highest chunk.
- **No volume in the far levels yet**; site and coarser data are height fields, so only ground tiles could add overhangs.
- **Riser materials** come from the planet's strata (`@engine/src/horizon/fn/Beds.cpp`), read at the column's own position; voxels blend strata on a 16 m lattice, so contacts can differ by less than a far cube.
- **`surfaceMaterial`** (`@engine/src/horizon/fn/Tint.cpp`) mirrors the simulation's cover rule from a surface sample. It cannot know frost-sorted or scalded ground, clay, or stones. The simulation should hand out the top material itself.
- **Standing water** (`StandingWater.cpp`): read at site level, a wet point needs a partner one data cell away at the same level, levels are carried at most five points over ground below them. `WaterRing = Shore + 1` is what makes two lattices agree on a shared point.
- **Occlusion** in the mesher: a quad grows along an axis only if its corner values do not change along it. Chunks therefore need all 26 neighbours for a final mesh (`meshChunk`, `markTerrainChanged`, `TerrainStream.cpp submit`, `Column.cpp mesh`).
- **Pools** (`Nature.hpp`): simulation pool (hardware − 1), crew = performance cores (`hw.perflevel0.physicalcpu`, else hardware − 2), air = 2. Two pools, not one: a pool task cannot be interrupted, so a chunk would wait behind a tile build of seconds. The thread that advances the game calls `favourCallingThread()` (macOS QoS user-interactive; nothing on Linux, unverified). Crew priorities: voxel columns and colliders 0, far cubes `level − 64`.
- **Readiness is what can be seen.** A level wants the columns reaching into its box at full reach and band; the margin column built beyond is for motion and holds no readiness back. A column is final when the corners of the square it reads are at its source level (`cubeFinal`): the square is smaller than one tile of that level, so the corners touch every tile under it and nothing is eased. The tiles asked for are what the levels read (`cubeDataReach`) and only while a level using them can be active (`cubeCeiling`): readiness then waits for nothing unread, and everything read is asked for. Rejected: fixed reaches per level (64 km local, 81 tiles, against 45 km and 42 tiles) and the margin column in `wanted` (it reads site data 3.09 km out at level 7 against 3 km asked for, so `ready.far` stayed false for good at some places).
- **Dry spawn in the engine.** A player without a wished place goes to the simulation's landing or, where that is under water, to the first dry ground of a ring search, built with blocking surveys so it depends on the world alone. Both are judged at the site level: the ground tile under the player takes one to two minutes and is built after the game opens, with the voxels, on every worker. Rejected: surveying the ground level before the game opens (it was a third of the start).
- **Landing search per tile.** `findLanding` searches one tile per level, the one under the coarser answer. A tile waits for its earlier-pass neighbours (`dependencies`), so a square over two tiles a side always waited four builds deep for 17 tiles; one tile waits one to four deep by its pass. Rejected: requiring the neighbour toward the answer (as slow as before at a tile edge); searching only pass-0 tiles (fastest, but moves the landing by up to a tile; section 4).
- **Chart at the ground level.** The chart reads Ground where the pitch resolves it, so the map, the inspector and the voxels read one level; standing water there comes from the site level because ground tiles carry the level of streams and pools too. Rejected: an inspector that reads the chart's level (it has no pitch, and the voxels are cut from the ground level).
- **Tile requests** (`TileRequests.cpp`): by gain (replaced cell size over distance); the player's own place (within 150 m, when lower than 150 m above ground) first, in a priority band above every other request, and with every worker, the rest leaves a quarter of the workers free. Only the frontier is asked for: a wanted tile hands its rank to the unfinished tiles it is built from whose own dependencies are finished. A ground tile also needs the groundwater of the local tiles its springs read, which the atlas does not list (`sim::groundwaterRead`, `sim::groundwaterSources`); it is asked for once that groundwater exists, and the groundwater once the landscape tiles it reads are finished. Asked for any earlier, a build waits inline for its chain: at the capture place every worker parked on one serial groundwater chain and `ready.far` stayed false for good. Requests go straight to the pool with a flag, and those no wanted tile leads to any more (after a jump) are called off; one already running finishes into the atlas. Rejected: `sim::requestAround` (ranks coarse levels first) and `Atlas::request` (cannot be called off). A pool of one worker keeps none free (`requestBudget`; review DREV-007: with one worker the shared budget was 0 and nothing above the ground's ceiling, not even a missing region tile, was asked for).
- **Stones in the finest far cubes.** Ground tiles lay clasts on and in the ground and the voxels show them; a far cube cut from the surface alone showed the cover under them, a mismatch the player sees where the voxel box ends. Ground-sourced levels stamp the clasts with the voxels' rule (a cube is a stone's where its centre is inside it), lifted with edited ground as the voxels' are. Their rims overhang the ground as in the voxels, so on those levels a column can have two tops (the contract says so).
- **Shutdown**: destroying a `Game` waits for every tile build in flight (the simulation destroys its atlas before joining its pool, so `~Nature` must quiesce it). `Session.abandon()` leaks the world for a process about to exit; `engine.mjs` loads the addon in the main thread too so it is not unloaded with the worker. A worker terminated in the middle of a call (at app quit) can no longer take an exception: a Node-API call fails, node-addon-api's `WrapCallback` catches the `Napi::Error`, `napi_throw` refuses it, and without `NODE_API_SWALLOW_UNTHROWABLE_EXCEPTIONS` (`@napi/xmake.lua`) it throws an empty `Napi::Error` out of its own catch and the process aborts (the renderer's capture at 16:50, crash report `Electron-2026-11-26-165919.ips`, frames symbolized to `Error::ThrowAsJavaScriptException` called from a binding's catch). With the define such a throw is dropped; `ChartWork` still catches in `OnOK`/`OnError` for the promise it settles.
- Traps: `sim::surfaceAt` locks the atlas per sample, use `SurfaceReader`; readers that call `readSurface` must call `overlayLive`; a `SurfaceReader` must not outlive its batch; the simulation tree is often mid-edit, retry builds.

Culling: only faces between solid and air of one level; no bottoms; nothing under the lowest neighbouring top; chunks without a face are not sent. Chunks under a finer level's box are kept (they are what shows when the finer level rests) and can be skipped by the renderer with a box test.

## 9. Design: deposits as partly filled voxels (approved 2026-11-26, not coded)

The owner (2026-11-26, refined the same day): a deposit is extra material on the ground and must be a real layer, not a colour on a cube top. Deposits keep the voxel's footprint, 8 per metre, and fill a voxel upward in sixteenths (7.8 mm each): snow, sand, dust or ash builds 1/16, 2/16, …; at 16/16 the cell becomes an ordinary voxel of that material and the next layer starts in the cell above. The point is to watch ash settle and grow after an eruption. Standing rules: cubes at every distance, measured against Earth, the simulation owns the state.

**1. Storage** (`@engine/src/world/type/VoxelWorld.hpp`, beside `TerrainTile`). A partly filled cell is an air cell standing on a solid voxel (or on a full deposit voxel). Each 1 m tile that has any gets a small sorted list of records: the cell's index in the tile (9 bits), the fill (4 bits, 1–15), the material (1 byte); 4 bytes a record. Decided by the lead (2026-11-26): a second, upper layer in the same cell (its own material and fill, the two summing to at most 16), for ash under fresh snow, which is ordinary after a winter eruption and must still be there when the snow melts; 8 bytes for a doubly covered cell. A third material landing on a doubly covered cell merges into the upper layer (its sixteenths are added to the upper fill and take the upper's material), and the lower layer is never relabelled. Rejected: turning the lower fill into a full voxel of its own material, which creates up to 15/16 of a voxel that never fell and lifts the ground by up to 11.7 cm at once. The merge keeps every sixteenth that fell where it fell, keeps the buried layer (the record, such as the ash) intact, and only mixes the newcomer into the top layer, which is the one melt and wind act on first (a dusting on snow is dirty snow). A material equal to the upper or lower layer's adds to that layer. When the two layers reach 16 the cell becomes a voxel by the cube rule (the material at its centre, the lower layer's if its fill is 8 or more) and what lies above 16 starts the cell above. Full voxels of a deposit are ordinary voxels of its material (`Snow`, `Sand`, `Silt`, a new loose `Ash`), so nothing below the top cell is new. A fully covered square metre holds 64 records, 256 bytes (512 with two layers), against 512 bytes for one dense `TerrainTile`; the voxel region (about 100 × 100 m) all under snow, 2.5 MB. On the simulation side a deposit is a depth per ground cell (uint16 mm, 128 KB a 64 m ground tile per kind) with its density (below). Ledges and pits need nothing special: each partly filled cell lies on its own supporting face. The journal and `EditStore` gain a record of the same shape (cell, fill and material before and after).

**2. What is a deposit** (the simulation's state; the engine projects it):
- Snow: depth = SWE / bulk density, per ground cell. SWE today is a weather-cell mean per elevation rung (`snowLadder`, cells of 131 km); the voxels need it on the ground tile's 0.25 m cells. The simulation should own: (a) the bulk density law of Sturm et al. (2010, J. Hydrometeorology 11, "Estimating snow water equivalent using snow depth data and climate classes"), ρ = (ρmax − ρ0)(1 − e^(−k1·h − k2·DOY)) + ρ0 with the parameters of the snow class (alpine, maritime, prairie, tundra, taiga, ephemeral; their Table 4, to be transcribed and checked against the paper before use), the class from the climate as in Sturm, Holmgren and Liston (1995), solved for h from SWE; (b) redistribution at the ground cell: shedding from slopes steeper than snow holds (sluffing above about 50°), wind scour and drift by curvature and exposure; (c) a non-blocking `snowDepthAt` and a depth raster per ground tile. `Material::Snow`'s density of 350 kg/m³ is then the material's only.
- Aeolian sand and loess: `WindLabels.blown` and `loess` (m, in `Tile::aeolian`) are part of the cover already but never reach the voxels. For all loose cover: `formColumn` lays the cover as full voxels as now, and hands back the remainder above the top voxel as a fill in sixteenths of that cover class's material, so loose cover thinner than a voxel becomes a deposit without a new field.
- Ash fall: `Plume.fallen` (kg/m², weather-cell mean) / tephra bulk density (fresh fall about 500–1500 kg/m³; the value must come from a measured source). Needs a loose `Material::Ash` (appended; `Tuff` and `LapilliTuff` are rock) and the fall at the ground cell, which today only `VolcanicTile.pile` holds near vents.
- Dust: sub-millimetre per event; it stays colour until it adds up to a sixteenth. Sinter and travertine are rock already.
- Asked of the simulation: `sampleBlock` returns beside the materials the fill (material, sixteenths) of each top cell it cuts; `SurfaceSample` carries the deposit's depth and material at every level for the far cubes; a step of the deposit fields per calendar step (below).

**3. Physics in play.**
- Digging and placing: the brush takes deposit fill with the cells it removes and counts it in the ledger; a placed voxel on a partly filled cell buries the fill (removed, counted as moved material). Journal and `EditStore` records as in 1, re-applied on load like voxel edits.
- Collisions (the lead's decision, 2026-11-26): a partly filled cell (1/16 to 15/16) does not collide. The player, bodies and Jolt shapes stand on the full voxel under it and the feet sink into the deposit by at most 15/16 of a voxel (11.7 cm), as they do in snow and loose sand; a fill that reaches 16/16 is an ordinary voxel and collides like one. The colliders do not change.
- Granular: a disturbed deposit becomes grains as `releaseSand` does today; landing grains add fill to the cell they stop in and convert it at 16/16. A deposit sliding off: fill flows to a lower neighbour when the drop between fill tops exceeds its material's angle of repose (`fallDirection` with heights in sixteenths).
- Water: melt is the simulation's (SWE falls, the fill drops). Into the engine's finite water only once the game seeds water at all (today only tests do): a full snow voxel at 300 kg/m³ is about 77 of the 256 `WaterUnits` of a voxel.
- What still needs the partial fill, taken: digging and placing it (journal, `EditStore`, ledger, as in 1 and above), and a fill falling as grains when the voxel under it is dug. Simplified: the support check gives a partial fill no load. In the streamed game the check (`discoverGrounded`) is topology only and reads no mass; in the small scenes' load check (`carryLoads`) a fill adds at most 15/16 of a voxel on one face, 0.6 kg of snow, 1.8 kg of ash or 2.9 kg of sand, against the 5 kg of the rock voxel under it and the column above its support, so it is left out; full deposit voxels load as any voxel.
- Steep faces: fill lies only on upward faces, so a 70° wall of 8/m steps would collect snow on every step. The depth the simulation hands out is per ground cell after shedding by the true slope (2b), so steps on steep ground get little or none.

**4. Meshing.** One mesher, one lattice. A partly filled cell is a box from its floor to fill/16: its top face lies at a fraction of the cell; its side faces span from the neighbour's height (its fill, or the floor) up to its own; the face it stands on is not emitted; the side of a full voxel next to it shows only above the fill. The packed quad gets two 4-bit insets in word 1's spare bits 16–31 (bits 16–19 lower inset, 20–23 upper inset, in sixteenths of a cell along the quad's vertical extent; for a top face the upper inset lowers its plane), so a quad still spans whole cells and a fractional edge only at its ends; greedy merging joins only equal insets. Occlusion counts a cell filled to 8/16 or more as solid. The same format serves the far cubes (5). The renderer had agreed to a separate array of fine quads for the earlier 16/m design; that is withdrawn, and the renderer agreed to the insets (2026-11-26): it copies them into its packed face, lowers a top face's plane by upper/16 as it raises water sheets by `rise`, trims a side's bottom and top cell, and draws the cube seam where an inset trims a side. A down face never carries an inset; the contract must say so.

**5. Far levels.** Every level uses the same rule at its own cube edge e: from the deposit depth d over a column (the mean over the ground the column stands for), the column carries floor(d/e) full cubes of the deposit material and a top cube filled to round(16·frac(d/e)) sixteenths. So 2 m of snow is four full cubes at the 0.5 m level, one at 2 m; 10 cm of ash is 3/16 of a cube at 0.5 m, 1/16 at 2 m and nothing from 8 m up. Where a column's top fill rounds to 0 sixteenths it keeps its ground top and the deposit shows as colour only, so the colour takes over where the geometry stops at the same depth on every level: share = min(1, d / d_cover), with d_cover the depth that hides the ground (snow about 5 cm, to be set from a measured source); a far column's share is the mean of that over its ground, not the share of its mean depth, which keeps patchy snow seamless between levels; voxel tops keep the renderer's hash rule (each cube whole or bare, the share being the fraction covered). Between the voxel box and the 0.5 m level and between levels the geometry differs by less than a sixteenth of the coarser cube, the same seam as for the ground, and the colour is continuous. The share and the deposit material travel as a grid beside each chunk's quads (one byte each per column top and per voxel top), which the renderer uploads as a texture page and updates without a remesh. A deposit that stands proud has true sides of its own material for its own height; risers of the ground below keep the renderer's riser rule, fed by the share. The shader stops computing snow from `SnowField` (`@game/src/cubes/fn/cubeSurface.ts`). Agreed with the renderer (agent a47cdb1b53f225695, 2026-11-26): the share grid, the riser rule, palette entries for every deposit material; open on its side: light bounced under a deposit.

**6. Growth, loss and compaction over time.** The simulation steps each deposit per calendar step from its fluxes, and the engine re-projects:
- Snow: + snowfall (`snowRate`), − melt and sublimation (the atmosphere's energy balance), redistribution (2b); its density from Sturm's law by depth and day of the season.
- Sand and loess: + deposition, − deflation, from the divergence of the aeolian transport the simulation computes (`aeolian`), as a rate per ground cell rather than the long-term labels it keeps today.
- Ash: + fall rate (`Plume.fallRate`) at the ground cell; − nothing in play but wind and water erosion.
- The engine turns each column's change of depth into fill: a sixteenth gained or lost re-meshes the chunk, budgeted per frame like the voxel meshing; a fill reaching 16 becomes a voxel and the next cell starts; a fill reaching 0 lowers into the voxel below (it becomes a fill of 15/16 of that material). An ash fall of 1 cm an hour changes a cell's fill about every 47 minutes of game time, snowfall of 2 cm an hour about every 23 minutes; the far levels change rarely. Where the player has dug or built, the engine applies the change of depth to the edited cells rather than the absolute depth, so new snow fills a dug hole instead of the hole vanishing. Under `jumpTo` the same applies over the jump.
- Compaction: snow keeps its mass and loses height as its density rises (the fill drops). Over seasons it becomes firn and then glacier ice: densification after Herron and Langway (1980, J. Glaciology 25) from temperature and accumulation, firn from about 550 kg/m³, ice from about 830 kg/m³. That needs layers per ground cell in the simulation (season snow, firn, ice, with their densities) and a new loose `Material::Firn`; full voxels change material when their layer crosses a threshold, and glacier ice already exists (`iceAt`). Ash to tuff is lithification over thousands of years: never in play; the simulation's volcanic pile already turns old deposits to `Tuff` and `LapilliTuff`, so only a long `jumpTo` would convert ash voxels.

**7. Cost** (estimates, to be measured in step 2): memory as in 1 (+⅙ of the near voxels when all is snow, +⅓ with two layers); mesh: a fill adds no faces on flat ground (its top replaces the voxel top) and a few side quads where fills differ; build: `sampleBlock` reads one depth per column it already reads, the mesher compares fills it already has at hand: a few percent per chunk; re-meshing as fills change: a chunk now and then, within the frame budget; far cubes: one more sample per column. Plan, snow first, each step tested before the next:
1. Simulation: the Sturm density law and snow class; depth per ground cell with shedding; `snowDepthAt`; tests against the paper's curves and a slope case.
2. Engine: fill records in `VoxelWorld`, filled from `sampleBlock`'s output, conversion at 16/16 and 0; storage tests; memory and build time measured.
3. Mesher insets and the quad bits; renderer draws them; `cubesMatchVoxels` extended to fill tops.
4. Far levels: full and partly filled deposit cubes and the colour share grid; seam check between the voxel box and level 2.
5. Calendar: snowfall and melt step the fill between frames without waiting; edits keep their holes.
6. Edits: brush, journal, `EditStore`, ledger.
7. Ash with `Material::Ash` and the plume's fall rate (the eruption case the owner asked for); then sand and loess; then firn.
