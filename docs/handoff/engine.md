## 5. Decisions and why

- **Cubes rule** (`@engine/src/horizon/type/CubeChunk.hpp`): a cube is solid if the world is solid at its centre. Rejected: mean height (shaves crests), "any solid" (fattens). The top cube shows the surface cover however thin; under water a column ends strictly below the level and the dry column beside it at or above it; a column counts as under water only if all eight neighbours know a level (else the carried level ran out and an open edge appeared).
- **Clipmap, not quadtree**: every level is a square of columns of chunks round the eye and is whole under the finer levels too. That costs a third more chunks and removes holes by construction: a missing or slow level just leaves the coarser one showing. Boxes per level are sent every frame and shrink to what is held.
- **Reach 128 cubes** (`FarCubes::reach`, `WorldOptions.cubeReach` 64–256): a cube is 1/128 rad at the side of its box, 5–7 px at 900–1350 px of height. 192 would be about 4 px but more than doubles the faces. Finest far level 0.5 m (level 2), coarsest 1024 m (level 13) stretched to 260 km instead of a 2 km level.
- **Chunks are 32 cubes on every axis** because the renderer's packed quad has 6-bit coordinates. A column of chunks is the unit of building and swapping; its water sheets ride on its highest chunk.
- **No volume in the far levels yet**; site and coarser data are height fields, so only ground tiles could add overhangs.
- **Riser materials** come from the planet's strata (`@engine/src/horizon/fn/Beds.cpp`), read at the column's own position; voxels blend strata on a 16 m lattice, so contacts can differ by less than a far cube.
- **`surfaceMaterial`** (`@engine/src/horizon/fn/Tint.cpp`) mirrors the simulation's cover rule from a surface sample. It cannot know frost-sorted or scalded ground, clay, or stones. The simulation should hand out the top material itself.
- **Standing water** (`StandingWater.cpp`): read at site level, a wet point needs a partner one data cell away at the same level, levels are carried at most five points over ground below them. `WaterRing = Shore + 1` is what makes two lattices agree on a shared point.
- **Occlusion** in the mesher: a quad grows along an axis only if its corner values do not change along it. Chunks therefore need all 26 neighbours for a final mesh (`meshChunk`, `markTerrainChanged`, `TerrainStream.cpp submit`, `Column.cpp mesh`).
- **Pools** (`Nature.hpp`): simulation pool (hardware − 1), crew = performance cores (`hw.perflevel0.physicalcpu`, else hardware − 2), air = 2. Two pools, not one: a pool task cannot be interrupted, so a chunk would wait behind a tile build of seconds. The thread that advances the game calls `favourCallingThread()` (macOS QoS user-interactive; nothing on Linux, unverified). Crew priorities: voxel columns and colliders 0, far cubes `level − 64`.
- **Readiness is what can be seen.** A level wants the columns reaching into its box at full reach and band; the margin column built beyond is for motion and holds no readiness back. A column is final when the corners of the square it reads are at its source level (`cubeFinal`): the square is smaller than one tile of that level, so the corners touch every tile under it and nothing is eased. The tiles asked for are what the levels read (`cubeDataReach`) and only while a level using them can be active (`cubeCeiling`): readiness then waits for nothing unread, and everything read is asked for. Rejected: fixed reaches per level (64 km local, 81 tiles, against 45 km and 42 tiles) and the margin column in `wanted` (it reads site data 3.09 km out at level 7 against 3 km asked for, so `ready.far` stayed false for good at some places).
- **Dry spawn in the engine.** A player without a wished place goes to the simulation's landing or, where that is under water, to the first dry ground of a ring search, built with blocking surveys so it depends on the world alone. Both are judged at the site level: the ground tile under the player takes one to two minutes and is built after the game opens, with the voxels, on every worker. Rejected: surveying the ground level before the game opens (it was a third of the start).
- **Landing search per tile.** `findLanding` searches one tile per level, the one under the coarser answer. A tile waits for its earlier-pass neighbours (`dependencies`), so a square over two tiles a side always waited four builds deep for 17 tiles; one tile waits one to four deep by its pass. Rejected: requiring the neighbour toward the answer (as slow as before at a tile edge); searching only pass-0 tiles (fastest, but moves the landing by up to a tile; section 4).
- **Chart at the ground level.** The chart reads Ground where the pitch resolves it, so the map, the inspector and the voxels read one level; standing water there comes from the site level because ground tiles carry the level of streams and pools too. Rejected: an inspector that reads the chart's level (it has no pitch, and the voxels are cut from the ground level).
- **Tile requests** (`TileRequests.cpp`): by gain (replaced cell size over distance); the player's own place (within 150 m, when lower than 150 m above ground) first, in a priority band above every other request, and with every worker, the rest leaves a quarter of the workers free. Only the frontier is asked for: a wanted tile hands its rank to the unfinished tiles it is built from whose own dependencies are finished. A ground tile also needs the groundwater of the local tiles its springs read, which the atlas does not list (`sim::groundwaterRead`, `sim::groundwaterSources`); it is asked for once that groundwater exists, and the groundwater once the landscape tiles it reads are finished. Asked for any earlier, a build waits inline for its chain: at the capture place every worker parked on one serial groundwater chain and `ready.far` stayed false for good. Requests go straight to the pool with a flag, and those no wanted tile leads to any more (after a jump) are called off; one already running finishes into the atlas. Rejected: `sim::requestAround` (ranks coarse levels first) and `Atlas::request` (cannot be called off).
- **Stones in the finest far cubes.** Ground tiles lay clasts on and in the ground and the voxels show them; a far cube cut from the surface alone showed the cover under them, a mismatch the player sees where the voxel box ends. Ground-sourced levels stamp the clasts with the voxels' rule (a cube is a stone's where its centre is inside it), lifted with edited ground as the voxels' are. Their rims overhang the ground as in the voxels, so on those levels a column can have two tops (the contract says so).
- **Shutdown**: destroying a `Game` waits for every tile build in flight (the simulation destroys its atlas before joining its pool, so `~Nature` must quiesce it). `Session.abandon()` leaks the world for a process about to exit; `engine.mjs` loads the addon in the main thread too so it is not unloaded with the worker. A worker terminated in the middle of a call (at app quit) can no longer take an exception: a Node-API call fails, node-addon-api's `WrapCallback` catches the `Napi::Error`, `napi_throw` refuses it, and without `NODE_API_SWALLOW_UNTHROWABLE_EXCEPTIONS` (`@napi/xmake.lua`) it throws an empty `Napi::Error` out of its own catch and the process aborts (the renderer's capture at 16:50, crash report `Electron-2026-11-26-165919.ips`, frames symbolized to `Error::ThrowAsJavaScriptException` called from a binding's catch). With the define such a throw is dropped; `ChartWork` still catches in `OnOK`/`OnError` for the promise it settles.
- Traps: `sim::surfaceAt` locks the atlas per sample, use `SurfaceReader`; readers that call `readSurface` must call `overlayLive`; a `SurfaceReader` must not outlive its batch; the simulation tree is often mid-edit, retry builds.

## 6. Contracts with other streams

`@game/contract/GameBridge.ts` holds all of it; the far-cube contract **is in the file**:
- `WorldOptions.cubeReach?` (`cubes?` is ignored); `WorldInfo.faceWords` (9), `WorldInfo.cubes { reach, side, band, finestLevel, coarsestLevel, viewDistance }`, `WorldInfo.snowLadder`, `WorldInfo.far { viewDistance }` (kept for the renderer).
- `Quads = Uint32Array`, two words per quad: word 0 plane (bits 0–5), lowU (6–11), lowV (12–17), sizeU − 1 (18–22), sizeV − 1 (23–27), axis (28–29), sign (30); word 1 material (0–7), occlusion (8–15, two bits per corner in the order low-low, high-low, low-high, high-high).
- `ChunkMesh { key, origin, faces: Int16Array, quads: Quads | null }`: voxel chunks have empty `faces` and their quads in `quads`; water meshes and debris stay `Int16Array` with `faceWords` words.
- `CubeChunk { id, level, edge, origin, quads, water: { level, quads }[], dataLevel, wantedLevel }`; `CubeLevel { level, edge, wanted, shown, box }`; `Frame.cubes { added, removed, data, levels }`; `Frame.far` is always empty and kept for the renderer's capture rule.
- Readiness: `Frame.ready.far` is true when every level holds all the squares of ground it wants (those reaching into its box at full reach and band), each read from the level's source all over (`cubeFinal`) and none waiting for a swap, and every finished tile has been looked at; `Frame.pending.patches` is the number of squares still short of that. `ready.{site,local,region}` are about the tiles the cube levels read, nothing else. Per level `Frame.cubes.levels[k].{wanted, shown, settled, box}`: drawable is `box`, final is `settled === wanted`. Tiles: `Frame.tiles.{region,local,site,ground}.{wanted,finished}` and `Frame.ready.{site,local,region}`. Tile reaches are what the cube levels read (`cubeDataReach`), so every column a level wants can reach its data level.
- Answers to the renderer's questions (2026-11-26): two sheets of one water level in a chunk no longer occur (levels closer than 2 cm are one sheet since `SheetGap`; distinct sheets have disjoint footprints); a level holds every chunk with a face over each square inside its box's footprint at any height (`checkBoxes` in the tests checks it every frame); a riser one cube high is the side of the top cube and so carries the top's material; taller risers on slopes up to 45° with cover carry the cover (`risersCarryCover` checks it).
- Risers: a step's side carries the surface cover where the ground is a slope of at most 45° with cover on it, the bed at its depth where steeper or bare. Culling: only faces between solid and air of one level; no bottoms; nothing under the lowest neighbouring top; chunks without a face are not sent. Chunks under a finer level's box are kept (they are what shows when the finer level rests) and can be skipped by the renderer with a box test.
- One water sheet per water body per column of chunks: levels closer than 2 cm are one sheet.
- `CloudField.snowLadder`, `snowLine`, `seaLevel`; `Frame.weather.snowpack` is snow at the player's height.
- `CloudField.aerosolDepth`, `ashAloft`, `ashLevel` are forwarded unchanged from the simulation's cloud field (zero until a source exists); the inspector shows `WeatherReport::ash` and `aerosolDepth`. The atmosphere's side is in `docs/handoff/atmosphere.md`.
- Nothing measured from the world origin is `float32`: particles are relative to `Frame.particleOrigin`, quads to their chunk.
- Shared files: `@napi/src/addon.cpp` (`abandon`, `faceWords`, `cubes`, `snowLadder`, `favourCallingThread`; the map stream owns the chart lines), `@game/main/engine-worker.cjs` (transfer list entries for `quads`, cube chunks, `snowLadder`, `snowLine`, `aerosolDepth`, `ashAloft`, `ashLevel`; message kind `close`), `@game/main/engine.mjs` (`close`, addon pinned).
- Addon stamp: every link of the addon writes `.out/addon/derivean.node.json` beside it, `{ builtAt, commit, dirty, diffHash, sources }`: `builtAt` UTC ISO time of the link, `commit` HEAD then, `dirty` whether `sources` (`@simulation`, `@engine`, `@napi`) differ from HEAD or hold untracked files, `diffHash` SHA-256 of that diff plus the untracked files (recipe in `@napi/xmake.lua`), `null` when clean. Without git, `commit`, `dirty` and `diffHash` are `null`. The hash describes the tree at link time; other streams edit the same tree. The renderer's origin line reads it (`@game`, the renderer's).
- Launches of this stream (bench, native and bridge tests, live) set `DERIVEAN_STREAM=engine`; the renderer shows it in the origin line.
- Asked of the simulation: `springsIn` and `Aquifers` to read the set from `groundwaterRead`/`groundwaterSources` (section 4); rename `AshClass::Sand`; top material and cover kinds in `SurfaceSample` at every level; a non-blocking bed-at-depth query; a revision stamp per tile; eviction (section 4); destroy order of `World`.

## 7. How to build, run and verify

All from the repository root; xmake comes through mise.
- Build tests, bench, addon: `mise -C @napi exec -- xmake build --yes engine-tests`, `... engine-bench`, `mise -C @napi run build` (addon at `.out/addon/derivean.node`). A cold build takes minutes.
- Native tests: `.out/xmake/build/macosx/arm64/release/engine-tests`; silent on success, exit 0. The planet takes 1–3 minutes to evolve, the whole run 5–10 minutes on a busy machine.
- Bridge tests: `cd @napi && node --test test/*.test.cjs` (each file evolves its own planet).
- Types: `mise -C @game run check`; everything: `mise run check`.
- Bench: `.out/xmake/build/macosx/arm64/release/engine-bench [age] [crew threads, 0 = machine] [plain|favoured] [quick | ready [seconds]]`; `engine-bench [age] start` times the start step by step (planet, landing, the dry search, atmosphere) and then the ground tile the game builds after it opens. `ready` prints the readiness signals every 5 s and on change, with tiles finished/wanted per level and the cube levels not yet settled. `engine-bench <age> at x z height [seconds]` does the same for a game started at that place, `jump` for one started at its spawn and teleported there (height above the non-blocking surface), both with the requests in flight per level and the groundwater requests. It prints fill times, step percentiles for idle, walk, a 1.5 km teleport and a boost flight of 100 km, and per cube level chunks, faces and milliseconds.
- Capture (renderer's): `DERIVEAN_CAPTURE=<plan.json> mise run play` after `mise -C @game run build`.
