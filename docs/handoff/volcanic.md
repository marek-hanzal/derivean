# Hand-over: volcanic

Describes the present state of the `volcanic` workstream. Rewrite it when the state changes; it is not a diary.

## 1. Goal

Own `@simulation/src/volcanic/**` (+ `test/Volcanic*.cpp`, `tool/Volcanic*.cpp`): volcanic landforms that arise from simulated emplacement at the planet's magmatic centres during a region's replay, are carried down the levels as rock, and keep living in calendar time during play. Checked against `docs/reference-landscapes.md` E1–E4: stratovolcanoes 1–3 km over a 10–30 km base with concave flanks, shields of a few degrees, calderas 2–20 km across, lava fields and plateaus, cinder cones in groups, necks. The volume built must be what the planet's record says reached the surface. Nothing placed, nothing painted by noise.

Order of work given by the lead: replay edifices → vent state, schedule and inspector data → live emplacement through the session overlay and the time jump → atmosphere coupling.

(2) Beds tilt by n · ∇h where h varies: neighbouring cells differ in ln h by p50 0.007, p90 0.043 at the arc's local tile (0.012, 0.054 on the shield), so 800 m up a pile beds dip 5° in the median and 28° at p90 against the floor. Not seen in voxels: no slice since (the gorge waits for relief's walls).

- **Bed tilt in tall andesite piles** (lead's item 1; probes `.out/volcanic/voxels-tilt/tilt.cpp`, `tilt3.cpp` and their `.out`; local tile 835 674 at the arc, 43 866 andesite cells with 300 m of pile or more). The beds are an artefact. Counted up from the floor at the cell's pitch, the top beds dip p10 15°, p50 35°, p90 71°, p99 83° against the horizontal (against the floor p50 19°, p90 58°) while the ground there slopes p50 6°, p90 31°; in a tenth of the cells or more they rise into the hill (cosine of their rise to the ground's p10 −0.91). Real stratovolcano beds dip outward about parallel to the flanks of the time, steepest (some 30°) near the summit and a few degrees on the apron (general geology, no measured source read). Two causes: (1) bed n lies at n · pitch(x), so in piles 4–4.5 km thick a change of the flow thickness of 2 % between 64 m cells tilts the top beds 50–80°; (2) the pile's floor itself is rough at 64 m: beds at one pitch for the whole pile, parallel to the floor, would still dip p50 27°, p90 53°. Tried in the mirror and dropped: counting the beds down from the top as built (`allLaid`; diff `.out/volcanic/voxels-tilt/proto-count-from-built-top.diff`): p50 72°, worse, because these piles lost p10 3.1, p50 6.7, p90 9.1 km to erosion since they were built, so the depth below the built top exceeds the height above the floor. Proposed: (a) a pitch that does not vary across a pile (`Pitch` by magma, raised to the flow where one is thicker, as flood sheets are, which pond uniformly), the flow drawn at its own thickness inside it (`cut` = the flow, not the share of the pitch); and (b) beds counted from a smooth reference surface instead of the raw floor: the region's pile top as the finer levels inherit it, or the floor smoothed over a kilometre, so that they dip as the volcano's broad shape does and valleys cut across them. A full record of when each cell's floor and top were laid (isochron beds) would be the exact form, at the cost of two dates per cell through every level. **Design chosen by the lead: isochron beds** (to be built after the move; nothing coded yet). Real beds are former surfaces, so a bed is a time, not a count.
  - *Record.* Per cell, besides `laid` (age of the last emplacement, kept already): `begun`, the age at which the pile's floor was first buried, and `built`, the height above the floor the pile reached as built, before erosion (the height of the top as last laid: at each laying `built = rock − (datum − floor)` after adding, not a running total; erosion after the last laying only cuts the top off, which the mapping handles; a caldera's drop and the plate's sinking do not change it). Ages are years before the tile's time, as `laid` is, so they pass between levels unchanged. Ages are cut into slices on one grid for the whole world, the region's step (400 kyr), so slice k is the surface at age kΔ wherever the pile was growing then, and its boundary is continuous wherever `begun`, `laid` and `built` are, which the blends make them. ## 5. Decisions and why

- **One law of motion** (`fn/Relax.cpp`): a mobile layer on a height grid moves to lower neighbours while `fall × min(thickness, sheet) > yield(heat)`; heat falls with distance travelled (faster under water) and the layer sets where it is spent. Lava, hot ash currents and loose fall (a layer with a fixed angle of rest and no cooling) are the same law with different numbers (`constant/Rheology.hpp`). `relax` takes a bound on visits so play can advance a flow tick by tick; the replay runs it to rest. The local solve is exact levelling of a cell with its lower neighbours; the first version moved half of an excess per visit and was five times slower. A surface may stand above its rest by 2 % of a flow unit or 0.5 % of the fall the layer holds over one cell, whichever is more: finer than that the grid draws no slope, and loose fall on ground steeper than its angle of rest otherwise relaxed to millimetres for tens of seconds per cone.
- **Emplacement works on `Emplacement`** (`type/Emplacement.hpp`), plain heights with `Open`/`Passing`/`Closed` cells, not on `Domain`: the same code must run on live ground. `fn/Lay.cpp` is the only place that maps a domain onto it.
- **Rejected**: stamping cones; routing parcels down steepest descent (not time-steppable, and chaotic at lobe scale across tile edges); a per-vent patch simulated once per world (it could not see the tile's own erosion); a pile stored as thickness in `Ground` (erosion is done by other owners' code and would not keep it in step).
- **Pile** = per cell the depth of its floor below the datum (`VolcanicDomain::floor`); thickness is `rock − (datum − floor)`. It needs no bookkeeping when other processes lower the rock or uplift moves rock and datum. A tile hands down thickness (`VolcanicTile::pile`), which interpolates; a child turns it back into a floor.
- **No sealing of fresh rock** (lead's decision once the region's lowering defect was fixed in 54fdbd9): volcanic rock erodes as its rock does from the step it is laid. Finer levels multiply erodibility and weathering by `min(1, age / span)` only, because they settle for a fixed span whatever the age of the ground.
- **Isostasy** (agreed with the lead): the planet's `thickening` already raises the surface for all melt, so under a newly laid pile rock and datum sink by the pile's full thickness spread by two box passes of 14 cells (`fn/Settle.cpp`). Do not touch `thickening`.
- **Budget**: per planet cell and mark interval, `erupted(mark k+1) − erupted(mark k)` × cell area, dealt to the centres inside the cell by vigour (`fn/Source.cpp`). Cells with melt but no centre inside lose their volume; the survey prints the dealt share.
- **Schedule**: steps of a vent are awake in spells of four region steps (70 % awake) with log-normal unevenness 1.2; the conduit moves every 2–7 steps within 6 km (andesite) or 10 km (basalt) of the centre. Single eruptions are a seeded sequence of the vent (`fn/Chamber.cpp`): the chamber fails when it holds the next one's volume, so the replay and the calendar share one history.
- **Eruption size is the chamber's** (`fn/Chamber.cpp`): a vent's chamber stores 1e4 yr of its window-mean supply (`Vent::chamber`); an eruption lets out chamber × overpressure (5 MPa) × compressibility (basalt 2e-10 /Pa, gas-rich andesite 1e-9 /Pa) in the median, 0.1 % and 0.5 % of the chamber (Huppert and Woods 2002), log-normal with spreads 1.3 and 1.5, never more than the chamber. A centre supplied at 0.06 km³/kyr over its life holds 0.6 km³ and erupts 0.6e6 m³ (basalt) or 3e6 m³ (andesite) in the median; the largest arc centre holds 7.3 km³. The breadth of the planet's distribution comes from the spread of chamber sizes; a wide spread per vent (2.2 was tried) sends over 60 % of a large chamber's output out in chamber-emptying eruptions, which arcs do not show. Because the chamber is proportional to supply, a vent's recurrence does not depend on its supply: a basalt vent erupts about every 23 yr and an andesite vent about every 150 yr in the mean, and supply sets the size. The count is the number of supplied vents over Residence × overpressure × compressibility × the spread's factor; `Residence` (1e4 yr, the middle of the 1e3–1e5 yr of crystal and U-series ages) moves it a hundredfold over its range, so the count is an outcome of mid-range inputs, not a test of them. A chamber that grows faster than its supply (thermal maturation) would make large centres erupt larger and rarer; not done.
- **Caldera**: a single eruption that takes 30 % of its chamber and at least 1 km³ (basalt) or 2 km³ (andesite) founders a block 500 m or 1 200 m deep (`emptiesChamber`), as wide as its volume requires, at the END of its step (lead's decision): every vent's usual eruptions of the step are laid first, then the step's climactic eruptions in turn, each laying its fall, currents and ash before its roof goes down (`erupt`, `Blast`, `emplace` then `collapse`). **The collapse cuts the cone** (`founder`, `fn/Collapse.cpp`): the layering under the pit goes down as one block (`blockDrop`, `Laying::sink`, the datum in `settleLaying`; in play `NearField::sink` and `Deposit::sink`, `intervention/fn/Raster.cpp`); the surface goes down to a level floor across the ring, solved by bisection so that the void between the ground as it stood and the floor holds the volume that left the chamber (the drop of each cell is its share of the block × its height over the floor). A cone over the chamber is truncated (Crater Lake, Santorini); on level ground the drop is the block. What stood above the floor is cut from the top of the pile; where the floor lies less deep than the block, the pile thickens by the rubble that fills the pit to it. Mass is kept: rock and datum each lose the chamber's volume. Pinatubo-sized eruptions (5 km³) now empty a 5 km³ chamber, as Pinatubo did. A large chamber can empty several of its eruptions in a region step (the arc centre: up to 368 km³ in one step), so a region step can hold tens of them: `Episode::paroxysm` is all of a step's chamber-emptying eruptions together and founders as one block of their total volume (the calderas of a 400 kyr step nest at 1 km cells); live play founders each. At 1 km cells the region's slope failure (which lets a kilometre-wide cell stand at 0.45 of a rock face) turns the ring wall into a 15° funnel. **Record and re-cut** (`fn/Caldera.cpp`): the region domain records every caldera it cuts (`Caldera`: vent, site, volume, magma, date) with the surface as the roof went down (the step's eruptions laid, the climactic one's own included) on a square around it; the pit is a volume over the founder's block, the share-weighted lowering of the surface under it against that surface, less the mean lowering of a ring two cells beyond the rim (so a cone built later in the pit takes only its share, and what the edifice around gained or lost is taken out). `cut` is that right after the step's eruptions, `kept` = pit at the end of the region window / `cut` (0..1). Only the region tile whose core holds the site keeps the record (`VolcanicTile::calderas`), so one owner decides `kept`. Nested calderas keep a record each; the drops of the calderas recorded after one (later steps and later collapses of its own step), recomputed from their own records (`cutOf`), are taken out of its lowering, so a later collapse does not count as the earlier pit surviving. `Caldera::wall` is how deep the surface dropped at the ring wall against the block (from the record); the local re-cut is scaled by it, since the region's drop is no longer the block. A local domain (`sharpenCalderas`, in `beginVolcanic` after `inheritParent`) replaces the spline of the region's cut by its own: `kept × wall × (block on local cells − block on the region's cells carried down by the seeding spline)` on rock, and the unscaled difference on the datum (the layering went down as the block whatever filled the pit since; what fills it reads as pile), for every recorded caldera, on every cell not owned by a finished neighbour, and on `settledFrom`/`settledTo` of the open edge, which is reset to them each step (not under a finished neighbour, which made its own cut); the two cuts hold the same volume, so mass is kept over the tile cores. It is done only when every local tile whose cells (halo included) the cut reaches has the owner region tile among its `dependencies` (`landscape/fn/Build.cpp`, called directly), so neighbours agree; a caldera that fails this near a region tile edge stays as the region cut it in every tile (large calderas near region edges, radius over about 11 km, never qualify). The spline copied for the subtraction is Seed's (cubic weights at x / cell − 0.5); if Seed changes its kernel this must follow. The first attempt (rim over floor at one point, last caldera per vent, parent tiles found by each local domain's own reach) is gone.
- **Columnar joints** (`columnOpen`, `fn/Columns.cpp`; read in `relief/fn/Form.cpp`): a lava sheet of the pile 3 m or thicker cracks into upright columns on a jittered triangular lattice (near-regular hexagons) of width 0.09 × √thickness m, 0.15–2.5 m (Giant's Causeway 0.45 m in some 30 m, Devils Postpile 0.6–1 m in about 120 m); each flow its own pattern. Within a skin of 1.2 widths under a bare face (depth along the surface's normal) each column has weathered back by its own share (up to 0.6) of its width, so a face shows prisms; joints of columns at least four points wide also stand open, max(15 % of the width, one point). Only where the sheet itself forms the face (its top within a point of it), so no ash bed is left over air. Nothing finer than two points of the drawn run is drawn (a joint 2–7 cm wide sampled at 12.5 cm voxels was noise; review): a 14 m basalt sheet (0.34 m) shows at the voxel level only, a 34 m andesite sheet (0.52 m) with open joints. **Necks** (`neckOpen`): a plug cools from its wall, so its columns lie across the conduit: the same jittered triangular lattice on (bearing × n, height), n = round(2πR / width) columns around so it closes without a seam (the lattice repeats every n columns), width from the plug's full width (0.09 × √(2R)); within twice a width of the axis nothing is drawn; same skin, recess and drawing gates as a sheet, the skin read from the rock surface (a neck is one body whatever it cuts).
- **Pile records**: what is laid is kept per cell by kind. `Laying` keeps `flows` (the hot pumice currents of chamber-emptying eruptions only; the block-and-ash currents of the usual eruptions are laid with the fall, `fn/Lay.cpp`), `fall` (cone fall and those currents), and `wet`, the part of each layer laid below standing water (sea or lake) at the moment it was laid. The pile keeps `flowLaid` (only the hot currents that welded as they were laid: 15 m or more at the cell in one step above standing water (the cell's whole wet part counted against them), `Welding` in `fn/Volcanic.hpp`; the rest goes to `fallLaid`), `fallLaid` and `wetLaid` beside `lavaLaid` and `allLaid` (`VolcanicDomain`); what is not lava, currents or fall is wind-borne ash. The tile keeps their shares in parts of 255 (`VolcanicTile::lava`, `flows`, `fall`, `wet` and the `wide*` copies for seams), `PileShares` carries them through inheritance (`takeCell`, thickness-weighted `Parents::blend`), the seam blend and the readers (`PileColumn`, `PilePoint`). Ground and ledgers do not read them (currents still settle as fall does).
- **Members of the pile** (`bedIn`, `fn/Pile.cpp`; four materials appended to `material/constant/Materials.hpp`, ids 39–42, definitions in `material/definition/`): each pitch of the pile (one flow at its recorded thickness and the fragments between, `pitchOf`; 14 m basalt and 34 m andesite where no lava reached) draws from the cell's shares, seeded by vent and pitch number, whether it was laid under water and what its fragment part came down as. Dry lava is the vent's lava with columns, as before. Wet basalt is `PillowLava` (the lower 60 % of the flow) under `Hyaloclastite` (the glass breccia the pillows shed, as in tuyas and lava-fed deltas); wet andesite is `Hyaloclastite` whole. A fragment part drawn as welded currents is `Ignimbrite`, a columned sheet, where the pitch is dry and its fragment part (34 m pitch less the lava) is 15 m or more (densely welded zones need some 10–20 m, Smith 1960); drawn as fall (cone fall, block-and-ash currents, hot currents that did not weld) it is `LapilliTuff`; ash is `Tuff`. Welding is decided when the currents are laid, so erosion does not change it and every reader sees the same beds; a region step's paroxysm is all of its chamber-emptying eruptions together, so "laid at once" is a step's worth of them. A basalt pitch is thinner than welding needs, so basalt piles never weld, as basalt rarely does. Heat is not recorded: "hot" is "dry and of a chamber-emptying eruption". Wet beds are drawn by share, not by height, so pillows can lie over dry flows where the sea came and went. `volcanicUnit` rounds the shares to parts of 255 as the tile keeps them, so the bed it erodes is the one the voxels draw. **The region reads the members too** (`membersOf`, `fn/Pile.cpp`): a kilometre cell meets the whole pile at once, so it takes the parts the pitches come to over many of them (lava dry, pillows and glass breccia wet, welded ignimbrite for the share of pitches whose fragment part reaches `Welding`, which falls off over the ±0.15 the pitches draw about the cell's lava share, lapilli tuff, ash tuff), names the bed and the tile's surface rock after the largest part, and blends erodibility as a log-weighted mean and weathering, stable slope and abrasion linearly; other traits (grain shares, porosity) are the largest part's. Region 52 42 (arc, 12 244 pile cells, probe `scratchpad/volcanic/regionrock.cpp`): by the old rule andesite 1.6 % / tuff 98.4 %; now andesite 1.5 %, hyaloclastite 0.3 %, ignimbrite 0.1 %, lapilli tuff 3.8 %, tuff 94.3 %; region 32 6 (shield) stays basalt 100 %. `groundwater/fn/Beds.cpp` still reads a pile as basalt or tuff by lava share (groundwater's; `membersOf` could be shared). Lapilli tuff takes tuff's erodibility (9e-6; 1.2e-5 was tried first and the proving cone, which now erodes as lapilli tuff where its fall lies, stood 587 m against the test's 2 × shield 598 m; at 9e-6 it stands 636 m): coarse clasts need more stream power than ash, so it is not weaker than tuff, and the published ranges do not separate them. Property values (density, strength, porosity, permeability, joint spacing) are taken from published ranges for each rock beside the existing Tuff, Basalt and Andesite: welded ignimbrite 2 300 kg/m³, 80 MPa, columns 1–3 m; hyaloclastite 2 000 kg/m³, 15 MPa, permeable (1e-5 m/s, Icelandic hyaloclastite aquifers); pillow lava 2 750 kg/m³, 120 MPa, pillows 0.5–1 m, permeable between them (1e-5 m/s, oceanic layer 2A); lapilli tuff 1 500 kg/m³, 8 MPa, porosity 0.40.
- **Region styles**: basalt 97 % lava, 3 % ash (its scoria is the local level's cinder cones); andesite 40 % lava, 20 % fall around the vent, 20 % block-and-ash currents, 20 % ash; a chamber-emptying eruption 5 % fall, 60 % ash currents, 35 % ash. Large basalt outpourings travel as inflated sheets (sheet thickness × √(km³ per part), at most 5): plains instead of shields. Flows within one eruption get 20–100 % of the cooling length in turn, which is what makes a flank instead of a pancake.
- **Minor eruptions** (`fn/Minor.cpp`): of every episode younger than the local level's settling time the region leaves out 3 % of what it brings up besides a chamber-emptying eruption (`minorVolume`), but no more than 8 eruptions of the typical size (0.08 km³ basalt, 0.4 km³ andesite); the local level builds them as dated small eruptions (cinder cone + flow, dome, summit cone), each in the settling step that lies its age before the end. Without the cap the largest vent's "minor" eruptions reached 11 km³ and a local tile took hours. Their lava sets within 1.7 km so both sides of a local tile edge see a flow whole (local halo = 2 km).
- **Traps**: `Conduit` is taken by `groundwater/type/Cave.hpp` (ours is `Feeder`). `sed -i` on macOS has no `\|`. `domain.level` is stale after the last step's cutting; reflood before drawing water. A region-level `PlaneSpec` plane loses 20–140 m per step by itself (the region's hidden-stream lowering); scenes use granite and judge form, not survival. Thin deposits (< 2 m in a step) stay loose cover and are not pile.

```cpp
void outbreaksBetween(uint64_t seed, const Vent& vent, double fromYear, double toYear, std::vector<Outbreak>& into);   // era years: world age + calendar seconds / YearSeconds

```

Vents come from the region tile under the player (`Tile::volcanic->vents`). The engine, each time the calendar crosses a window (say a year ahead), asks `outbreaksBetween` for the vents of the region tiles around the player and hands `airSourcesOf` to `addSource` before time passes their start. **Atmosphere** (`docs/handoff/atmosphere.md`): `AirSource{id, position, height, start, end, ash[3] kg/s (lapilli, grit, dust), sulphur, vapour, heat}`, `addSource` before time passes the start, `ashFallAt` → kg/m² (convert with the deposit's bulk density). `airSourcesOf` sends the wind-borne ash and 30 % of the hot currents as lofted ash (3 % of it as dust), sulphur dioxide 3e-3 (basalt) or 1.3e-3 (andesite) of the melt's mass, water 0.7 % or 4 %. **Live deposits** (`intervention/type/Edit.hpp`, `intervention/fn/Raster.cpp`, owned here since the interventions stream closed):

```cpp
struct Deposit { Vec2 origin; double cell; int width, height; std::vector<float> drop, rock, loose[SedimentClasses], lava; Material kind; uint64_t vent; };   // m per cell, row-major
Edit deposit(std::shared_ptr<const Deposit> laid);                                     // EditKind::Deposit over the grid's rectangle
std::shared_ptr<const Deposit> depositOf(const Emplacement& field, const NearField& near, const Outbreak& outbreak);   // volcanic/fn/Outbreak.hpp
```

Each site cell takes the area mean of the grid over its square, so any cell size works and the volume is kept where the grid lies on live tiles (the edit makes every tile within 256 m of its rectangle live). Per cell: the floor founders by `drop` with its cover and its layering (`EditedTile::now.datum` goes down with it), `rock` is laid on it and welds the cover it buries into rock over the share of the cell it covers, `loose` lies on top by class; water keeps its depth on the new ground. Booked as `dug` (drop) and `filled` (the rest), so the solid budget closes. Volume is kept for any grid; form is not: a 16–64 m grid gives the site cells blocks with steps at the grid's cell edges. `depositOf` uses the replay's rule (`WeldDepth`, now in `constant/Rheology.hpp`): 2 m or more in a cell is rock, thinner lies loose (lava as coarse rubble, fragments half coarse, half sand). The history holds the deposit itself, not a recipe, so a jump reproduces it exactly whatever the ground looked like; what water does with it afterwards agrees within 1e-3 (as for strokes).

The engine's sequence (to wire):
1. Ahead of the calendar (say a year), `outbreaksBetween(seed, vent, eraYear(now), eraYear(now + window), list)` for the vents of the region tiles around the player (`Tile::volcanic->vents`); `eraYear = world age + calendar seconds / YearSeconds`.
2. For each, `airSourcesOf(outbreak, calendar.era, groundAtVent, sources)` and `addSource` each before time passes its start.
3. When the calendar passes the end of its phases (`time` + `blast` + `effusion`): an `Emplacement` around `outbreak.site` with its origin on the lattice of its cell (4 m for small eruptions; 16–64 m for a flow of a cubic kilometre, which covers tens of km²), `ground` and `water` from `surveyAt`, `rest` Open where the engine allows change and Closed beyond; `near = layOutbreak(field, outbreak)`; `intervene(world, deposit(depositOf(field, near, outbreak)))`. Lay it once; do not lay it again after a jump: the history has it.
4. Wind-borne ash: `ashFallAt` (kg/m²) / bulk density (about 1 000 kg/m³) as `loose[Fines]` (and `Sand` for grit) of a Deposit on the engine's own grid, when the fall has ended.

Costs: every site tile within reach of the rectangle becomes live and stays live (one km² per tile, its water followed in time), so a basalt flow of 0.1 km³ makes some 30 tiles live. Voxels (`simulation/fn/Live.cpp`, ground stream closed, now this stream's): rock a Deposit lays starts a `LivePile` on the cell (`EditedTile::pile`: floor below the datum, metres laid, of it lava, kind, vent), whose thickness is the rock above its floor, so water cutting it thins it and foundering takes it down whole; `changeAt` returns it as `CellChange::pile` with `CellChange::datum` (the layering's drop), `changeLattice` moves `GroundLattice::datum` and lays the live pile on the lattice's own pile (kind and vent of the top), `overlayLive` (Ground level) and `overlaySurface` (Site level) move `SurfaceSample::datum`. `testEruption` checks both on a one-node lattice.

## 8. Open questions

- **Pile under the arc**: the piles over 4 km are submarine edifices built up from a sea floor about 3.7 km deep (section 2); on land the thickest is 4.7 km. Whether 7.6 km at one cell is too much is a question of submarine edifices (a volcano rising 3–4 km from the floor of an arc basin, plus its own subsidence), not of subaerial sections. Secondary bound still open: the sink spread (28 km) is narrower than an arc plate's flexure (50–150 km), but the halo allows no wider.
- **Frequency**: the count is Earth's (31.5 against 20–28 a year for as much land) and the planet's supply on land is short by about 3 (lead's); with more supply the eruptions grow, the count stays. Open and mine: VEI 5–7 are rare or missing (no chamber above 7 km³; Earth's largest come from silicic reservoirs that store melt 1e5 yr, a magma this model does not have) and VEI 0–1 are somewhat more than Earth's.
- Vents under the sea are emplaced like any other (lava quenches to 12 % of its cooling length); not looked at.
