# Relief of the Region, Local and Site levels

## 1. Goal

The owner: the world must not look like a height map. A massif is one body that valleys are cut into, with faces, shoulders, debris under walls and connected crests; ground has edges that come from mechanics (rock that stands until it fails, water that cuts where it gathers, beds that differ, debris at repose); scale and statistics match real ground ([reference-landscapes.md](../reference-landscapes.md)). Owned here: the behaviour of `Level::Region`, `Local`, `Site` in `@simulation/src/landscape`, plus the tools `terra view`, `terra relief`, `terra block`.

Targets for an active humid range at Local (lead, 26 Nov, from real data; every attempt is measured against them with `terra relief` on the humid range and shown with the same views):

| Measure | Target | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mean slope | 25–32° | threshold hillslopes limited by landsliding: Himalaya ~30°, Alps ~25–30° |
| Ground above 60° | a few % where rock is strong and jointing wide, less in weak rock | rock faces as a long tail, from rock-mass strength |
| Valley floors | broad only where ice or a big river made them | troughs and big-river plains; tributaries narrow |
| Ice | sharpens: troughs with steep walls, cirques cut back into arêtes and horns, peaks little above the snow line | glacial buzzsaw (Alaska, Mont Blanc), not a snowfield on rounded ground |
| Held plane at Local | gullies under rain at 22.5°, without grid heading bias | badlands and gullied slopes; the present test only asks that it stays a plane (section 5), to be restored once incision is right |

The 26 Nov result (21.6°, 0.45 % above 60°) is rounded domes, which the owner rejects; the old one (37.7°, 18.7 %) was too steep and combed. Neither reads as a massif.

## 5. Decisions and why

- Stability is not judged pair by pair. `failFaces` (Stability.cpp) visits cells lowest first; each cell carries the foot of its slope (`FlowScratch::foot`) and may stand no higher above it than its rock holds over the straight-line run (`stableDrop`), and no higher above a lower neighbour than a fresh face. Tried and rejected: tests over runs of 1, 2, 4 … 128 cells in eight directions (octagonal pyramids, axis-aligned valleys) and an incremental profile from the neighbour (a one-cell ramp at a wall's foot brought the whole wall down).
- Strength (Strength.hpp/.cpp): Culmann wedge, H ≤ (4c/γ)·sin β·cos φ/(1 − cos(β − φ)), cohesion falling with the cube root of face height. `massCohesion` (kPa at 100 m, rock jointed as usual for its kind) and `massFriction` were appended to every material definition: granite 350/34°, gneiss 300/33°, sandstone 250/32°, limestone 300/34°, schist 120/27°, shale 25/20°. With these a usual granite face is ~90 m, massive granite (×4) ~255 m, a kilometre of granite slope ~43°. First values (granite 1100 kPa) gave Local mean slopes of 44°.
- `CoarseFlank` 0.45: a cell of a kilometre holds that share of a face's stable drop (a dissected flank). With 0.6 the Region sat at 33°.
- Soundness per cell = `Domain::sound` (rockmass cohesion over the material's usual, Structure.cpp) × (1 − 0.85·weakness) / quality, clamped 0.05–4. `QualitySpan` 48 cells (was 14): the fine part of the seeded quality made every cell erode at its own pace. `FractureSpacing` is 0 above ground level: fractures come from `fractureShare`.
- Debris: rock that breaks stays on its cell in the rock's weathering classes (Local, Site) or goes into the cell's water as load in the same step and is booked as erosion (Region, `FlowScratch::delivered`; without the booking the plate did not rebound and the land sank under the sea). `settleDebris` (Talus.cpp) finds the resting surface lowest cell first, runs what stands above it down slopes at ≥ 0.95 of repose and lays it as a cone (`heap`). Tried and rejected: pairwise sweeps (talus at 50–70° after 40 sweeps) and depositing the whole parcel on the first gentle cell (heaps 80–175 m high on one cell).
- Free faces retreat by weathering (`retreatFaces`): a column keeps how far its face has gone (`FlowScratch::retreated`) and comes down when that is a cell's width, leaving a ramp at `RampTan` 0.75. Lowering the column a little every step turned every wall into a ramp.
- Order in `shapeSlopes` above ground level: `wearDebris`, `breakSlopes`, `weather`, `creep`, `settleDebris`. Weathering first left 0.4–1.2 m of waste on every face, so no face counted as bare and shale never failed.
- A mantle of debris (over max(1 m, 0.1 cell) and resting at ≤ `RestingTan` 0.8) is its own foot: the face above an apron starts anew at its top.
- Lakes: a cell under standing water whose outlet has been cut below it within the step is cut as river bed (`drained`, `pool` in Fluvial.cpp). Before, a lake lost one cell per step.
- Stream wear on a face steeper than `FreeFall` 1.19 is that of the limit, not proportional to the fall (hidden-stream lowering ate walls at 18 m per step).
- Region drainage (`gathersWater`, Process.hpp): a channel needs `ChannelHeads[Region]` 2.5e7 m³/yr of gathered water (water beyond `SheetMargin` 3.2 sheets of the slope above the cell, Flow.cpp); a cell with less is lowered as by a stream of `HiddenStreams[Region]` on its own slope; gathered water takes one neighbour by a fixed draw with the odds of the fall line; water that reaches a rim outlet leaves (the rim was a gutter that re-entered the domain). Slope² weights and shares between two neighbours both locked valleys onto grid directions.
- Local and Site use the Region's drainage rule (`gathersWater`): channel heads 1e5 m³/yr at Local (about 25 cells' water), 400 at Site; hidden streams 1e5 at Local, none at Site. With the old rule (all water above 3000/6000 m³/yr) every cell past the first was a channel and a held plane combed itself every 9 cells, 70 m deep. A hidden stream at Site (400) striped a held plane one cell apart (deposition, fast-eroding cover and creep together; without any one of the three the stripes went); Site slopes are left to weather, creep and failure.
- Settling controller (`raise`, Uplift.cpp) at Local and Site: the deficit against the parent's surface is averaged over blocks of `HeldParents` 4 × 4 parent cells (64 × 64 fine cells, aligned on the domain corner, so the blocks of neighbouring tiles coincide), over evolving cells only, and blended by a smoothstep between block centres (reads only the two blocks either side: owned cells of two neighbours read the same blocks; a B-spline reached past the halo and gave a step at the seam). Lab matrix on the crease (`relief2/runs/B–F`): a 12-cell blur ribbed it every ~25 cells; blocks of one parent cell ribbed it; two loosened the ribs; four with the floor below left one valley with tributaries. Ground keeps the blur.
- The found rate may not pull ground down faster than the parent does (`FlowScratch::parentLift`, floor min(0, parent rate)); without it a block above its parent's surface was sunk and the crease lowered 2.6 m.
- River floor, Site only: at Site a cell that has gathered the Local channel head is not cut below min(its raised surface, `trend`): the Local level has already set that river's descent, and a Site tile (1 km) is too small to find its own. Only in domains led by a parent (`parentLift` set), so `terra block` is unaffected. At Local the pin is off since 26 Nov: the Region's surface averages a valley with its walls over a kilometre, so pinning Local rivers to it held every valley at the mean the block is held to (pin0 in section 3: +5.5° mean slope, faces ×3). Tried at Site too: the Site wall view then stands in a slot canyon cut below the Local floor.
- Ice (`glaciate`, Glacial.cpp): snow sheds off ground steeper than `AvalancheTan` 0.84 (40°), so a steep wall grows no ice of its own (a wall below the surface of a glacier beside it is still filled and ground); sliding speed 1.56·flux^0.2 m/yr (Aletsch-scale flux), spread by two box passes of 250 m half-width (reaching ~500 m) and weighted by thickness against the box mean (at most ×2); erosion 2.7e-7·u² m/yr (Herman et al. 2015) up to half the ice depth per step; the fill takes in any cell whose ground or water surface reaches a glaciated neighbour's ground (so a hollow, or a lake beside the ice, fills). On cells finer than `BedReach` (40 m half-width, so at Site only: radius 10 cells; Local's radius is 0 and unchanged) the avalanche slope is read off the surface averaged over that reach, every cell with more than half of that window under ice is ground, and the ice depth behind the u² weighting and the half-depth cap is the window's mean: read cell by cell on 4 m cells every rib steeper than 40° stayed bare and unground while the ice beside it cut down, and grew into a forest of one-cell needles hundreds of metres high (Site wall view of 26 Nov, `.out/terra/relief/run4/view-site-wall-fix1.png`). Known limits (review of 27 Nov, not yet acted on): at Local a 100 kyr step saturates the half-depth cap above a flux of ~1e7 m³/yr, so there the cut follows depth, not u²; each cold step holds glacial-maximum ice for its whole length; thickness weighting lets an overdeepening feed itself (no adverse-slope limit). Replaces erosion ∝ depth × slope on every covered cell, which lowered ridges as fast as troughs. Ice spreads sideways only by 10 passes into neighbours whose ground lies below the glacier's level, so a trough is as wide as the ice its flux draws.
- `settleDebris` also ends every undated step above ground level (`stepDomain`, after `cutAndFill`, `workShores` and `blowLoose`) and every `stepBlock` step after `cutAndFill`: a river cutting into fill left walls of loose sand of 80 m on the bedded block, and on 27 Nov the Site dune pass (`shapeDunes`, last step only) swept the foot of channel banks bare after the settle and left 7–10 m of cover above repose on the test-focus Site tile (`testReliefCover`). Nothing may move loose cover after it in a step.
- Glacial debris: debris rides with the ice along its flow lines; at a glacier's side, where only the level fill put ice, it goes down the water's course to the ice that carries it; beyond the front a moraine keeps at most min(`MoraineLimit` 40 m, `MoraineTan` 0.6 × cell) per step and the meltwater carries the rest down its course, laying it on the way (an outwash train); what reaches the rim, a finished neighbour or the sea is booked as `sedimentOut`. Before, everything above 40 m was lost unbooked and the side cells laid their own cut back as till.
- Hidden-stream wash in dry country (`measurePower`, Fluvial.cpp): the wash is max(gathered^m, f^(1−m)·own^m), m = `StreamExponent` 0.45, own = the cell's hidden stream, f = `runningShareOf` (aeolian/fn/Regime.cpp) = 1 − aridity·(1 − `FloodShare` 0.02), aridity = 1 − rain / evaporation demand. Derivation: an ephemeral stream that runs a share f of the year with the year's water carries Q/f while it runs, so its yearly work is f·(Q/f)^m = f^(1−m)·Q^m; f is the one the aeolian stream uses for how often a dry stream runs (one owner). Humid cells (aridity 0) are unchanged; gathered channels are unchanged (their flow is the routed one). Requested by the aeolian stream: the Region's hidden streams took back 99–100 % of the weathered cover of the trade-wind desert. Rejected: the proposed cover multiplier (1 − aridity) on its own (no derivation, and it acted on the cover rather than the water).
- Transmission losses: `routeWater` (Flow.cpp) calls the aeolian `sinkIntoBed` after the karst `sinkAt` of the same cell and takes it off the discharge; the loss is kept per cell in `Domain::seeped` and `Tile::seeped` (m³/yr; empty until some bed of the domain took water, then full size) for the groundwater to add to recharge. The slope length above a cell (`way`, behind `gathered`) is weighted by the water that reached the cell before evaporation, karst and bed losses: with the losses taken first, a dry reach that lost most of its arrivals got a vast `way` and no channel below it.
- Hand-on (consistency stream's `settledWay`): in `carryLoad` a finished neighbour's cell that hands its river on hands its load on too and books nothing as leaving; in `cutAndFill` it caps the room of the cell it hands to, as a share would.
- `settleDebris` orders the run-out by the surface as it rests after the shedding pass, and debris that still lands on a visited cell is laid as a cone. Before, the run-out used the order of the surface before failure, and what landed behind the pass was dumped on one cell: needles of 600–1500 m on the bedded block.
- `failFaces` (both the face term and the whole slope from the foot): a face that reaches below the column's own bed is two stacked faces, the rock under the bed up to what it holds and on it the bed up to min(its height above the bed's base, what its rock holds). Fixes the stepped-wall case (a thin weak bed behind a strong lip cut the lip down); a thin strong cap rides on what the weak rock under it holds. Tried: dropping the column to what the lower rock holds whenever that rock fails — it removed the steps (39 faces, test needs 40).
- The Local plane check (`test/LandscapePlane.cpp`) now reads: a featureless Local slope stays one (gullies p95 ≤ a quarter cell) and any water paths on it keep the fall line. Before, it required more than 200 paths, i.e. that a featureless plane dissects; under the gathered-water rule it does not (as at Region). The grid-heading guard at Local is therefore weaker until item 4 of section 4.
- Trap: the settling controller gains (`TrendGain` 0.5, `TrendLearn` 0.12) were tried at 1.0/0.5 and put back; they are unchanged.
- Trap: `FlowScratch` now carries state across the steps of one replay; never reuse one for another domain.

## 7. How to build, run and verify

- `mise -C @simulation run build`, `mise -C @simulation run test` (about 1–2 min when the tree compiles). Binary: `.out/simulation/build/macosx/arm64/release/terra`.
- `terra relief --at humid-range|cold-range|old-range|dry-plateau|flat-beds|fold-belt|granite|lowland|coast [--tiles 2] [--levels 123] [--crop 96] [--out dir]` — searches the place, prints the equivalent `--x --z --site-x --site-z`, then per level: slope distribution, relief in 1/5/16 km windows, surface shares and talus angle, drainage density, valley sections, crests, peaks and pits, rms against 3×3 and 17×17 means, faces above 60°, tile ledger; writes slope-class maps. Good at Local in a high range: mean slope 25–35°, about 50 / 10 / <1 % above 30 / 45 / 60°; talus p50 32–38°.
- `terra view --x --z --above m [--to-x --to-z | --heading deg --pitch deg] [--site m --local m --region m] [--snow 0] --out dir --name tag` — perspective picture from tiles with cast shadows and haze. `--snow 0` removes the closed-form snow; glacier ice the tiles hold is always drawn.
- `terra block --level 1|2|3 --rock name [--weak name --bed m --dip r] [--sound f] [--height m --floor cells --flow m3/yr --uplift m/yr --river 0 --steps n] [--out dir --name tag]`, and `--tilt degrees` for the grid-bias check. Reference runs: `--level 3 --rock granite --sound 4 --height 220 --floor 12 --steps 40`; `--level 3 --rock granite --height 180 --river 0 --floor 30 --steps 10`; `--level 3 --rock sandstone --weak shale --sound 2 --bed 24 --height 200 --steps 40`.
- `terra measure plane --levels 23` (measure stream) is the regression for drainage: Site planes must stay under 1 m of gullies.
- Before-set (old mechanics, old planet): `.out/terra/relief/before/relief-<kind>.txt` and `slopes-<kind>-L{1,2,3}.png`. Block pictures: `.out/terra/relief/blocks/`. Local view of the landing range: `.out/terra/relief/try3/view-landing-local.png`.
