# Interventions, hydrology, time

Written by the lead from the stream's final report; the stream has finished. Owns `@simulation/src/{hydrology,intervention}`, `test/{Hydrology,Intervention}*.cpp`, `tool/InterventionCommand.cpp`.

## Goal

What the player changes is fed back into the world: dug and built ground, water that fills and spills, a finite ocean in bodies that straits connect, time that can run, pause and jump — with closed budgets of water, salt and solid.

## State now

`testHydrology` and `testIntervention` pass in a driver linked against the library (the suite stops before them in another stream's check). The game does not use any of this yet.

- Water is one model on two grids: the planet (65 km, periodic) and groups of live Site tiles (4 m). A Site tile becomes live within 256 m of an edit's footprint and stays live; touching live tiles form one group.
- Quiet solver: evaporation and filling solved exactly, then settled (pits fill, full basins overflow, meeting basins merge, salt mixed lake by lake); stable at any step. Event solver: shallow water on eight neighbours with its own stable step while a group is awake after an edit and ticks are at most 4 s.
- Sediment at absolute 5-year marks: only stream power beyond what the untouched bed carried cuts; load drops as a delta in standing water.
- Edits are strokes (lower, raise, level along a segment with width and graded height, or a strait between two planet cells) kept as a time-ordered history. Jumping backwards rebuilds from the untouched world plus the history.
- Ocean: the planet's surface water is a closed stock in bodies, the lakes of the planet's flooding tree. Straits move water between bodies and nowhere else; there is no path out of the planet (see [Straits](#straits)).
- Three acceptance scenarios run as `terra intervention ocean|cascade|diversion|all [--out dir]` and close water to 1e-11 and solid to 1e-13 (seed 42 at 1.2 Gyr, an earlier planet state): straits from the ocean's anchor to the lowest closed depression above the sea (23.6 m above it) and within the ocean move nothing in 10 000 years and leave the four bodies as they were; a breached dam between lakes released 3 120 m³ and after 30 days all three stood as one; a diverted stream moved 27.5 million m³/yr into the new channel and kept it for a century. Flow through a strait is shown by the hydrology fixtures, since the planet's sea fills everything under its level until S3e.

## Straits

A strait (`hydrology/type/Ocean.hpp`, `Strait{a, b, area, sill}`) is a channel between two planet cells with its bed at `sill` along its whole length and a throat of `area`. It has no valve: water runs down whichever way the surfaces at its ends fall, as through Gibraltar or the Bosporus, and `Ocean::moved[i]` is what went through strait `i`, positive from `a` to `b`: a diagnostic of the throat, not a balance of bodies, since a body that parts and takes water back into one part can leave it at zero while the sea has changed. The ocean is the body that holds `Ocean::anchor`, the deepest cell of the body with the most water at the start (not the planet's deepest cell, which may lie in a small cut-off pit). `openStrait` refuses ends that coincide (an edit whose ends lie in one planet cell stays in history and cuts nothing), a throat with no area, and grids that drain across a rim; the planet grid has none.

- **Ends.** Each end stands at the surface over its cell: the level of the topmost basin on its cell's chain that holds water of its own, or its ground where the cell is dry. Water that leaves the channel at the lower end runs down the cell's slope into the first basin with room (`pour`, the same pouring `settle` does, `hydrology/fn/Settle.cpp`). The strait acts at its effective bed `s* = max(sill, ground at a, ground at b)`: a channel cannot carry water below the ground it is cut from. Two dry ends, level ends, an upper surface at or under `s*`, or a receiver that already lies in the donor's lake leave the strait still.
- **Flow** (`hydrology/fn/Strait.cpp`). Orifice flow `Q = C·A·√(2g·H)` ([credits](../credits/hydrology.md#torricelli-1644-kirchhoff-1869-lienhard-and-lienhard-1984-flow-through-a-straits-throat)) under the head of the upper surface over the higher of `s*` and the lower surface. Free outflow (lower surface under `s*` or lower end dry) moves only the donor: `dH/dt = −Q/A_u`. Submerged outflow moves both: `dH/dt = −Q·(1/A_u + 1/A_d)`, with `1/A_d = 0` while the lake at the lower end is full and passes on what it gets. Between events the wet areas stand, √H falls linearly and both surfaces are linear in H, so the time to each event is exact.
- **Events**, the first reached ends a segment: the donor's surface reaches its next cell (its area changes) or its base (it is empty, and the child under the upper end carries on while the other keeps its water and salt); the receiver's surface reaches its next cell, `s*` (free outflow turns submerged) or its spill (what comes next runs on, or the receiver and its sibling meet in their parent); the head runs out (level surfaces, or a donor fallen to `s*`: the link breaks); the round ends. After each one the ends, the direction, the donor, the receiver and the donor's reserve are found again; a reversed fall reverses the strait and its `moved` falls. Where the receiver lies in the donor's own lake the two have become one and the strait is still.
- **Several straits.** Straits between the same cells with the same bed are one throat of their summed area, its transfers credited to them by area. Throats take turns in rounds fixed in time from the newest cut (one hour, doubling to a year), each reading what the one before left: exact for a throat that shares no body with another, first order in the round otherwise, the same however time is fed in.
- **Ledger** (`hydrology/fn/Exchange.cpp`). One integration is one exchange, and bodies in it are known by their topmost basin as the lakes stand at the time. Each lake is mixed: what leaves carries the donor lake's strength just before the segment, what arrives mixes in at once. Every segment debits the donor and pours the same volume into the receiver in one call (`book`), neither going negative, and books it to both bodies. When lakes part their water and salt are shared out by water at one strength, and when they meet they are added; both are booked to the bodies they left and joined.
- **Crust.** The salt of a dry cell is its crust (`grid.salt`), for every dry cell, also one dry from start to end. A cell is wetted by an event: the receiver's surface passes a member's height, a basin takes its first water (its pit, and the cells under it at its base that its full children's surface only reached), or what spills on floods the parent. Then, before anything else happens to that lake, the crust dissolves into the lake and is booked to it as crust turned to solution; what the lake gives next carries it. A cell that dries keeps no crust, since its salt left with the mixed water, so a cell wetted and dried within one integration gives up its crust exactly once. Precipitation of salt on drying comes with evaporation in S4.
- **Closing.** The wet cells of the lakes that took part stand at their lake's level and strength, a dry cell keeps what crust it has, and every other cell stays exactly as it was. Each body is checked over its cells against its account (segments, crust, parting and meeting) to `1e-12` of its stock plus its turnover; a lake made of one that took part counts as taking part, so a sibling that merged with it and parted again keeps the strength they shared. The worst share is kept in `Ocean::unbooked`, not balanced away; the world budget carries it (`WorldBudget::unbooked`), the sea's imbalance is at least it, and `terra intervention` prints it. Globally the bodies hold the water they began with and the bodies with the crust the salt (`WorldBudget::crust`, `crustAtStart`); nothing leaves, the floor is closed. The per-segment asserts (`v ≥ 0`, `own ≥ 0`, nothing poured off the grid, crust ≥ 0) hold in debug builds only.
- **Changes.** `Ocean::changes` counts the integrations that changed a body's level or which cells are wet. Live tiles read the pristine sea level until it is above zero; `moved` cannot tell that.
- **Unchanged without a strait.** With no strait cut, the planet, the ocean, the budget and the live tiles are bitwise what they were before straits existed (`.out/s3v/` holds the comparison). The comparison was repeated after the ledger by bodies and the crust events.
- **Tests.** `test/HydrologySea.cpp`, `test/HydrologyStrait.cpp` and `test/HydrologyLedger.cpp`: free and submerged outflow against the closed form with vertical walls, the meeting level and time, the sill stop, a dry end above the sea, sloping bowls meeting at the level that holds their water, ticks against an hour and steps against a jump, two throats from one donor, salt carried A → B → C at the donor's strength before each segment (with Codex's 0.0875 kg case), a crusted bowl whose crust B passes on to C in the same round against the hand count, a floor wetted and dried in one integration giving up its crust once, two throats sharing a crusted bowl fed in one piece, in ticks and settled at the end of a round, and one throat settled exactly when a shelf is wetted or a lake parts, each ending in the same bodies and crust, a lake that parts and takes water back with the first strait's account passing through zero while the sea changed, the same parting booked by hand with the parts as bodies of their own and a change counted that no strait booked, a crusted bowl dissolving its crust while an unreached crust stays bitwise, a bowl and its fresh sibling merging and parting again within one hour, bowls merging, and a strait running back. `test/Intervention.cpp` cuts two straits on the planet, to a depression above the sea and within the ocean: nothing moves, `changes` stays 0, the budget closes and the replay is the same; and a fixture on the planet grid in the world (`Ocean::start`) runs a sea into a dry bowl through the `strait` edit, closes, and replays the same after a jump back.

### What is left for S3e and S6

- **S3e.** The ocean on the sphere: `fillOcean` as a flooding tree on the sphere's graph with true cell areas, so a depression under the sea stays dry until a strait reaches it and the acceptance case (a strait into a dry basin below sea level, Earth's Zanclean flood, [Garcia-Castellanos et al. 2009](../credits/hydrology.md#garcia-castellanos-et-al-2009-a-strait-floods-a-basin-below-sea-level)) runs on the planet rather than on fixtures. `anchor` moves to a cell of the same body when the tree is rebuilt. `seaLevelNear` says "dry" explicitly instead of returning `WorldFloor`.
- **S6.** The live world: the engine's `inVoid` and its loss branches, `budget.escaped` in `@napi`, the impermeable floor, and the counterparts of what live tiles exchange with the sea (`intervention/fn/Budget.cpp` books inflow, rain, evaporation and outflow apart). A player's dug channel becomes a strait edit there. S4 brings rain and evaporation to the bodies.

### Where S3v departs from the S3 design (§6)

- The bowl in the merging test fills to its spill, passes on into its sibling, and the two meet in their parent, which then rises to the sea; "joins the donor's sibling" was read as the receiver's sibling, since a donor cannot spill into a lake above it.
- The bathymetry fixture fills the western bowl by position (`x < 16`) rather than by its leaf, which is the same cells and keeps the fixture free of the tree it tests.
- `Ocean::unbooked` holds the worst per-body residual of the closing check; it reaches the world budget and its imbalance rather than stopping the game.
- `anchorOf` is public so that fixtures settle their own seas with an anchor.
- `intervention/fn/Scenario.hpp` declares `runStrait` in place of the old drain scenario, outside the S3v file list; the scenario now passes world points to the strait edit (`worldPoint`), where the drain scenario handed planet points to an edit that expects world points.
- `runStrait` (`terra intervention ocean`) runs on the world, not on a fixture: until S3e the world has no dry basin under the sea, so it cuts a strait to the lowest closed depression above the sea and one within the ocean and shows that nothing moves. The positive case runs through the world's edits and replay in `test/Intervention.cpp`.
- Settling inside a round where two throats share a body is not the same to 1e-9 as settling at its end: the throats take turns over the shorter span, which differs to first order in the round (2e-3 in the test's bowl). §6 asks 1e-9 for a split at the moment of wetting; that holds for one throat, and with two throats at the ends of rounds, which is what the tests check.
- A world can start from an ocean that was not filled from its planet: `Ocean::start` holds it, and `jumpTo` rebuilds from it instead of calling `fillOcean`. Only the fixture test sets it.
- The opposite segments around a parting cannot leave `moved` at zero with one part down and the other up in a basin tree: a part that parts stays full, so water returned into its sibling refills that sibling and spills on into the parent. The test instead finds the moment the first strait's account passes through zero, where the sea has changed, and books the parting by hand to show the parts as bodies of their own.
- `planet/constant/Scale.hpp` still calls what lies below `WorldFloor` a void water can escape into; that is the engine's `inVoid`, S6's to remove.

## Hooks

- Query, one line after each Site-level `readSurface`: `overlaySurface(world, position, sample)` (`intervention/fn/Intervention.hpp`); exact form `editedTile(world, key)`; per column `siteColumn(world, cellX, cellZ)`. The ground stream wired an overlay as `overlayLive`; check it uses these.
- Sea (`hydrology/fn/Ocean.hpp`): `seaLevelNear(world.ocean, planetPoint(seed, p))`, `seaLevelAt`, `seaDepthAt`, `openWaterAt`, `seaBodies` (each body's level, stock, the throats ending in it and whether it is the ocean), `world.ocean.changes` (whether the sea has changed), `crustOf`, `world.ocean.salinity`, `world.ocean.moved` (per throat). A strait is cut with the `strait(from, to, area, sill)` edit. Untouched tiles keep `Tile::water` at the pristine sea level; only live tiles follow a sea that moved.
- Time: `intervene`, `advance`, `tick`, `jumpTo`, `syncLive`, `budgetOf`; the clock is `world.interventions.calendar`.
- Threading: one thread drives time and edits; readers must not run during those calls.

## Known weaknesses

A flood that reaches a group's rim leaves it and rivers entering stay at the atlas's flow; jump and walk agree only within tolerance (water 1e-3, salt 2e-2, ground 1e-3, cut volume 5e-2); throats sharing a body take turns in rounds; a strait is an orifice, without the friction and length of a real channel; site-level sea exchange is booked but not fed back to the ocean stock; sediment does not move while a group is in event mode; no halite saturation; the era cannot change on an existing world. A 100-year jump takes 0.13–0.38 s per tile. The fixes after the second review round were not re-reviewed.
