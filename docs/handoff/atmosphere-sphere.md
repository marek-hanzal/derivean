# Atmosphere on the sphere (planet slice S4a)

Owns the new files of `@simulation/src/atmosphere/` whose names start with `Sphere` or `EddyBand` (`type/`, `fn/`, `constant/SphereAir.hpp`) and `@simulation/test/AtmosphereSphere*.{cpp,hpp}`. It is the numerical core of a barotropic atmosphere on S1's cubed sphere (`docs/handoff/geometry.md`), built ahead of slice S4, which switches the climate and the atmosphere over (plan: `.out/lead/handover/PLANET.md`, "S0 review outcome" wins; Codex's requirements: `.out/review-mailbox/from-codex/DREV-CLAUDE-002-01.txt` Q4 and `-03.txt` "Grad/div pro S4"). It is additive: nothing calls it, and today's weather on the flat 128² grid (`docs/handoff/atmosphere.md`) is unchanged.

## State

Built and tested: `simulation-tests atmospheresphere` passes in about 10 s (release, macOS arm64) and on Windows (clang-cl, `.out/lead/obsidian/obsidian.sh s4a-mp tests atmospheresphere`). Timed on 6 × 128² and 6 × 256² (below). Not wired into the stepped atmosphere, the climate, the bridge or the engine.

## The grid

The grid is the geometry's since S3a (`geometry/fn/SphereGrid`, `docs/handoff/geometry.md`, S3a): `makeSphereGrid(level)` builds cells, areas, centres, neighbours, oriented edges (left, right, from, to, length, normal) and vertices with their cells at one level of S1's quadtree; the level is the resolution parameter (7 → 6 × 128² cells of 72 km, 8 → 6 × 256² of 36 km, √ of the mean area). The weather's own discretisation on top of it is `makeSphereStencil(grid)` (`fn/SphereStencil`, `type/SphereStencil`): per edge `diamond`, `across`, `along`, `chord` and `offset` (`EdgeStencil`, read only to build the Laplacian and by the diagnostic operators, dropped by `releaseEdgeTables`), and the step's hot tables: per edge `carry`, the offset projected on the gradient weights, the upstream cells `behind` and `topology`, a 16-byte copy of the grid edge's left, right, from and to, per vertex the least-squares weights and its four cells `vertexCells` (a cube vertex's first repeated with weight zero). Operators, the flow and the Poisson solver take both, `(grid, stencil, …)`; the Laplacian's `SphereMatrix` is `type/SphereMatrix`. The move kept every number of this slice bit for bit (`.out/s3a/probe`).

- **Cells** are numbered `(face · side + j) · side + i` (`indexOf`, `cellAt`); a field is a `std::vector<double>` per cell (cell means, `CellField` in `fn/SphereOperators`); sin φ for f is the centre's z.
- **Edges**: one `SphereEdge` (geometry) per pair of neighbours (12 n²), `left` the lower index, the normal out of left. A flux is evaluated once per edge and applied to left and right with opposite signs, so face seams and cube vertices need no code of their own.
- **Vertices** (6 n² + 2, eight of them with three cells): the cells meeting there (geometry) with least-squares weights (`SphereStencil::weight`) that reproduce linear fields in the gnomonic tangent plane at the vertex. A plain mean is not enough: on a face edge the two faces' grid lines meet the edge obliquely (60° at a cube vertex), so the four centres' mean sits O(h) from the vertex, a quarter of a cell next to a cube vertex; with it the edge gradient had an O(1) error.
- **Edge gradient** (`gradientOf`): from the difference across the edge (right − left) and along it (to − from, vertex values), through the dual basis of the two chords in the tangent plane at the edge's midpoint (`across`, `along`). The chord between the two centres is not the normal (23° off at face 0, level 5, cell (29, 29), Codex's case; Putman & Lin 2007), and `across` carries that non-orthogonal term on every edge.
- **Diamonds**: the two geodesic triangles (left centre, from, to) and (right centre, to, from); they tile the sphere (tested to 1e-12).

## Operators (`fn/SphereOperators`)

- `divergenceOf(flux)`: Σ ± flux / area. Σ area · divergence over the closed grid cancels edge by edge (tested to 1e-13 of Σ |flux|).
- `streamFluxOf(ψ)`: the volume flux of the non-divergent wind v = up × ∇ψ through each edge, ψ(from) − ψ(to) at the edge's vertices. Round a cell these telescope: the wind's divergence is zero to rounding in every cell, and a uniform tracer stays uniform.
- `curlOf(edge vectors)`: circulation (vector · chord) over the area; curl(gradient) is zero to rounding.
- `makeLaplacian`: the stiffness K = Σ_edges diamond · Gᵀ G with G the edge gradient, Δψ = −Kψ / area (`laplacianOf`). It is the energy ½ Σ diamond |∇ψ|² of the discrete duality finite-volume method (Hermeline 2000; Domelevo & Omnes 2005) with the vertex unknowns replaced by their least-squares cell values, so it is cell-centred. Symmetric bit for bit by construction (rows add the same products in edge order), positive on every non-constant field, zero on constants (each G row sums to zero), 21 entries per row. Its Rayleigh quotient on Y₁ is 2/R² to 0.1 % at level 5. The rows are gathered in one flat buffer, each entry found through a small hash per row, in edge order (16 ms at level 7, 65 ms at level 8).
- `makeStencilMatrix` (`type/StencilMatrix.hpp`): the solver's copy of K. Rows at least two cells from a face edge all have the same 21 neighbours at the same index offsets, so they keep only their values, by blocks of up to 64 rows of a grid line, and the product sums eight rows at once in registers; the rows of the two-cell seam band keep compressed rows. Each row adds the same products in the same column order as the assembled matrix, so the product is the same bit for bit (tested on levels 1 to 7, which has two blocks per face row), 1.8–3× faster. `forEachProduct` hands each row's sum to the caller in ascending row order, so a loop can be fused with the product.
- `shareFaceInteriors`: the cube's rotations carry face 0, with its cell numbering, onto each other face, so the six faces' interiors agree, but only to rounding (1.2e-14 of the largest entry at level 6, 6.5e-14 at level 8), because each face's centres are computed from its own coordinates. The solver's K takes face 0's interior rows for all six faces, and each seam row's entry in an interior column the value of that interior row, so K stays symmetric bit for bit and positive (tested on levels 2 to 7), and the split form stores one face's values (10.7 MB instead of 64 MB at level 8, inside the 16 MB L2 cache). The product moves by rounding only; ψ by 1.7e-14, 4.0e-14 and 1.5e-13 of its largest value at levels 6 to 8 and the flow after 20 steps by 1.4e-14 to 4.9e-14, the CG iterations unchanged. Above 1e-10 of the largest entry the faces are left as they are (a grid whose faces are not images of each other).
- The shared product (`applyMatrix` on a shared matrix, Neon's DREV-NEON-007): grid line by grid line with the six faces inside, so one line of the shared interior (42 KB at level 8) stays in the L1 cache while all six faces use it; on arm64 a NEON kernel loads each of a row block's five grid lines once and forms the neighbours in registers (`vextq_f64`) instead of reloading the field per entry. Every row adds the same products in the same column order (the seam rows and the portable blocks through the same `seamProduct` and `blockProduct` as `forEachProduct`), fused as clang fuses `sum += a * b`, so the product is the same bit for bit as `forEachProduct` and the assembled matrix: memcmp of the product, a V-cycle, a solve to 1e-9 and ψ after 10 Rossby–Haurwitz steps on levels 5 to 8 against da0b77a, on arm64 and on x86-64 with AVX2 and FMA under Rosetta, where the portable path runs; `checkSharedFaces` on levels 2 to 7. This holds only while clang contracts within a statement (`-ffp-contract=on`, its default for C++, set explicitly for macOS and clang-cl in `@simulation/xmake.lua`): the NEON kernel always fuses, so a build with contraction off would make the kernel's product differ from the assembled one, which `checkSharedFaces` catches. On Windows (clang-cl, `/arch:AVX2`) the portable path gives the same bits as the arm64 kernel (mixed precision, below). CG's product takes the shared product too, with p · q as its own pass in dotOf's order; the solve is the same bit for bit whether p · q is summed apart or fused into `forEachProduct` (ψ and ζ after 40 Rossby–Haurwitz steps on levels 6 to 8, at 1e-9 and 1e-6, with and without the projected first guess). The fused loop is row by row and has no NEON kernel: a step at level 8 and 1e-9 from the last ψ takes 0.955 → 0.869 G instructions (−9 %), with the projected guess 0.389 → 0.370 G (−5 %); level 7 −7 % and −5 % (`.out/s4a-neon013/Bench.cpp`). Against da0b77a in alternating runs (`.out/s4a-neon007/Probe7.cpp`, thread time, best of 40 steps per level and tolerance, load average 37 to 48): the product 1.45 / 1.55 / 1.35× faster at levels 6 / 7 / 8; a step at 1e-9 8.1 → 6.5, 30.1 → 23.8 and 133 → 114 ms (1.25 / 1.27 / 1.16×), at 1e-6 5.7 → 4.6, 20.9 → 16.3 and 93 → 80 ms.

A flux-form alternative (`divergenceOf(normalFluxOf(gradientOf(ψ)))`) is also second order inside the faces but not symmetric, and its local error at face edges is larger (0.23 against 0.15 of the value for Y₄), so the symmetric K is the Laplacian.

Inside the faces the operator is second order. In the two-cell band along the face edges its local (truncation) error stays at about a tenth of the value: there the edge gradient is first order (below), because the chord between the two centres misses the edge's midpoint by O(h) along the edge. ## Poisson (`fn/SpherePoisson`, `fn/SphereMultigrid`)

`solveSpherePoisson(poisson, grid, source, result, tolerance, limit)` solves Δψ = ζ by conjugate gradients (Hestenes & Stiefel 1952) on K ψ = −area · (ζ − its mean). K is symmetric and positive on the subspace without the constant, the source's area mean is the one part no field on a closed sphere has as a Laplacian: it is removed and reported (`PoissonReport::sourceMean`), every residual stays in that subspace, and the result's area mean is set to zero. The report carries iterations, the final relative residual ‖Kψ − b‖/‖b‖ and `converged`. Below a tolerance of 1e-10 the residual comes from a fresh product of the result as returned, its mean removed, which stays in the solver's `product` for the caller (`PoissonReport::product`) (CG's recursive residual falls on to 1e-22 while the true one stops near 1e-13, so without it a tolerance below that would report success); from 1e-10 up it is CG's recursive residual, which stays within 1.3e-5 of the tolerance of the true one (levels 5 to 7, cold and warm starts; tested to a hundredth, with the V-cycle in float32 and in double; on level 8 within 5.2e-4 in double and 4.9e-4 in float32), and the product is saved; a solve that misses its tolerance says so and keeps its last iterate. Past the true residual's floor CG does not stay put: it moves the iterate away and back (level 5: from 3e-14 at iteration 12 up to 5e-9 at 17 and down to 5e-13 at 20; level 8, Y₂, tolerance 1e-30: 4e-6 after 100 iterations, in both precisions), so a tolerance below about 1e-12 buys nothing and the residual reported after the floor depends on the rounding. The solver does not stop at the floor or keep the best iterate; `checkFailure` cuts its impossible solve before the floor. `result` on entry is the first guess.

The preconditioner is one multigrid V-cycle (Brandt 1977) over the coarser quadtree levels down to level 1: the same Laplacian rediscretised on each level, a Chebyshev–Jacobi smoother (below), restriction by summing the four children (K is an integrated operator, so residuals add), prolongation by the parent's value, plain CG to rounding on the 24 cells of level 1. With the restriction the transpose of the prolongation and the same polynomial before and after, the cycle is symmetric and positive, as preconditioned CG needs (tested; `smoothing`, the polynomial's degree, must be at least 1, asserted). `makeSpherePoisson(grid, false)` uses the diagonal instead. The tolerance is checked before each V-cycle, so the last iteration does not run one whose direction would be thrown away.

The smoother is the Chebyshev iteration (Saad 2003, Algorithm 12.1) for K with the Jacobi preconditioner D⁻¹, of degree 3, on the interval [0.2, 1.05] · λmax(D⁻¹K) (Adams, Brezina, Hu & Tuminaro 2003): the part of the spectrum below 0.2 λmax is left to the coarser levels. λmax is estimated once per level when it is built, by 12 power steps from the grid-scale checkerboard with the Rayleigh quotient xᵀKx / xᵀDx, which approaches it from below: under 1 % short on levels 2 to 8 (tested to 5 % on levels 2 to 5). The polynomial grows only above 2θ = 1.25 times the estimate, so an estimate a fifth short would still be safe. Before the coarse correction the smoother starts from zero (its first residual is the source and needs no product); after it, its final residual is not formed. It takes the same CG iterations as two symmetric Gauss–Seidel sweeps but, unlike them, has no order among the cells, so it can be spread over workers without changing its result. Degrees 1 to 5 at level 7: 23 / 13 / 9 / 8 / 7 iterations to 1e-10, 49 / 45 / 42 / 48 / 52 ms. Fusing the smoother's vector update into the product (`forEachProduct`, writing the next direction beside the current one) or the restriction into the last residual is slower, not faster: a V-cycle at level 8 takes 21.9–23.7 ms fused against 16.6–17.4 ms with the product and the vector loop apart, so they stay apart. λmax is estimated only on the levels that are smoothed, and the finest level holds no correction or source of its own (`LevelUse`).

**Mixed precision** (the default; `makeSpherePoisson(grid, stencil, true, false)` keeps the V-cycle in double). Neon's DREV-NEON-013-01 (`.out/review-mailbox/from-neon/`), the low-precision preconditioner inside a double Krylov solver of Higham & Mary 2022, §8.2. The V-cycle runs in float32 on the levels above the coarsest (`SpherePoisson::narrow`, built by `narrowLevels` from the double levels, rounded once: stiffness, inverse diagonal, λmax); the coarsest is solved by CG in double, as are the outer CG, its residual, the product K p and the result. The double levels between the finest and the coarsest and the finest's smoother space and diagonal are then released. Before the cycle the residual is scaled by the power of two that brings its largest value into [0.5, 1), and the correction is scaled back, each factor applied in two halves so that neither overflows, exactly wherever the result is a normal number: a residual that falls to 1e-12 of the source neither underflows nor loses bits, and the cycle's result does not depend on the residual's size (tested bit for bit at 2⁻⁶⁰ and 2⁷⁰, finite for a subnormal residual; a zero residual, or one whose largest value is infinite or NaN, passes unscaled). The Chebyshev coefficients are computed in double and rounded once. The null mode is treated as in double: the coarsest solve removes its source's mean, and the constant a correction carries is removed by area at the end. On arm64 a float32 NEON kernel takes 16 rows at once (`fn/StencilNeon.hpp`), loading only columns −2 .. 17 of each grid line, so the last row of the last face reads nothing past the field; it equals the row sums bit for bit (tested). The float cycle has no sums in an order other than the cells': the smoother, restriction and prolongation work per cell, the coarsest CG's dot products run serially over 24 cells, and max |r| is exact. Spreading it over workers would keep its result.

## The barotropic flow (`fn/SphereFlow`)

`makeSphereFlow(grid, Ω)`, `setVorticity`, `setStep`, `stepSphereFlow(flow, grid, stencil, poisson)`: ∂ζ/∂t = −∇·(v (ζ + f)) with f = 2Ω sin φ (Charney, Fjørtoft & von Neumann 1950). `rotationRateAt(age)` = 2π / `deepTimeAt(age).siderealDay` from S2 (7.2921e-5 1/s today, tested).

- Flux form: each edge carries its volume flux (`streamFluxOf`) times the absolute vorticity at the edge's midpoint, so the area integral of ζ + f is kept to rounding (tested to 1e-14, after a day of the Rossby–Haurwitz wave: −3e-17) and that of ζ stays zero.
- The edge value is the mean of the two cells moved to the edge's midpoint by the edge gradient (`EdgeStencil::offset`, read as `SphereStencil::carry` = (offset · across, offset · along), 16 bytes an edge instead of 72; it moves the flow by rounding, ζ by at most 6e-15 of its largest value after 200 steps of the Rossby–Haurwitz wave at 1e-9 on levels 6 to 8, 4.6e-13 at 1e-6, enstrophy by 3e-15 of itself). With the plain mean a steady state drifted fifty times faster, at first order: a solid rotation over cube vertices changed by 7.5e-2, 4.1e-2, 1.9e-2 of its vorticity in a day at levels 4 to 6; with the move 5.7e-3, 1.4e-3, 3.9e-4. The plain mean would keep the enstrophy exactly before time discretisation; with the move it is not kept: in the Rossby–Haurwitz wave it falls by 6.5e-4, 1.8e-4, 4.5e-5 a day at levels 4 to 6. Neither energy nor enstrophy is conserved (today's Arakawa Jacobian keeps both), and unforced grid-scale vorticity grows slowly where the metric is worst: in 30 days at level 4 from noise, enstrophy +18 %, energy +6 %, root-mean-square vorticity × 1.36 in the cells touching cube vertices, × 1.18 along face edges, × 1.02 inside the faces; at level 5 × 1.10 at the vertices and +0.8 % enstrophy (`test/AtmosphereSphereDrift.cpp` bounds it at these sizes).
- Leapfrog with the Robert–Asselin–Williams filter (Robert 1966, Asselin 1972, Williams 2009) at the flat world's 0.1 and 0.53. Both assume equally spaced time levels, so the step is part of the state (`SphereFlow::step`, set by `setStep`): a different step restarts the history, and the first step after a restart (and after `setVorticity`) is a forward step. A day of the Rossby–Haurwitz wave at level 5 ending in a half step lands 1.7e-5 of the amplitude from a day of equal steps; a leapfrog step of length h₁ from levels h₀ apart lands 4.4e-3 away (with tendency F it adds 2h₁F where (h₀ + h₁)F is due). Without a positive step `stepSphereFlow` refuses (returns false).
- Each step builds its candidate (vorticity, the older level, ψ from the first guess below, fluxes) in work space and swaps it in only when the inversion converges (`SphereFlow::tolerance` 1e-9, `limit` 60). `stepSphereFlow` and `setVorticity` return whether they did; on false the state, history and wind are as before, `flow.inversion` reports the failed attempt, and a retry computes bit for bit what a clean run does.
- First guess of each step's ψ (Neon's DREV-NEON-019-01, `fn/SphereFlowGuess`): the combination of the last three accepted ψ closest to the new solution in the energy norm (Fischer 1998), over d₀ = ψₙ, d₁ = ψₙ − ψₙ₋₁, d₂ = d₁ − (ψₙ₋₁ − ψₙ₋₂), from a 3 × 3 Cholesky solve with G = dᵢ · K dⱼ (K ψ of each level kept, one product a step) and dropping a difference whose pivot is lost to rounding. In exact arithmetic it is never further from the solution in that norm than ψₙ or its extrapolation in time; CG stops on the residual, so that it costs no iterations under noisy forcing is measured (below), not guaranteed. `setVorticity` and `setStep` restart the history (one level, the last ψ scaled); a refused step leaves it as it was. `SphereFlow::projection` 0 starts from ψₙ itself. ## The eddy band (`fn/EddyBand`, `constant/SphereAir.hpp`)

**What today's filter does.** `shapeCirculation` (`atmosphere/fn/Circulation.cpp`) fills `PoissonSolver::pass` with a ring in wavenumber: gain max(0, 1 − ((|k| − 7) / 3.5)²) at |k| = hypot(x, z) cycles across `PlanetSpan` (16 777 216 m), for every wave with x ≥ 1. The waves that do not vary along X (x = 0, the zonal bands) are left out: the belts own them. Every `EddyEvery` = 4 wind steps (2 h) `refreshWind` asks `solvePoisson` for the vorticity filtered through it as `eddy`, and `stepCirculation` adds `growth(row) · eddy` to the tendency: vorticity of weather-system size (fastest at 2 397 km, the band from 4 793 to 1 598 km) grows at the rate the season's temperature gradient sets for its latitude (`EddyGrowth` × `storminess`) until the eddies' root-mean-square vorticity reaches `EddyLimit` (1.5e-5 1/s). Why: a single layer cannot be baroclinically unstable, so this closure stands in for the conversion of temperature contrast into weather systems (`docs/handoff/atmosphere.md`, §5).

**On the sphere.** The Laplacian's eigenfunctions are the spherical harmonics, with wavenumber √(l(l + 1)) / R, so the ring becomes a gain per degree l at the same physical wavenumber, applied to every order m ≠ 0 (m = 0, the zonal harmonics about Earth's axis, are the sphere's zonal bands). `SphereEddyWavelength` = `PlanetSpan` / 7 and `SphereEddyReach` = 0.5 reproduce today's ring in metres: degrees 8 to 24, peak 1.00 at 16, gain 0.03 at 8 and 0.13 at 24 (the gain and the band's bounds are tested against the flat formula for degrees 0 to 40, for these constants and for 4 000 km). `filterEddyBand` projects the field on the harmonics of the band by the grid's area quadrature (4π-normalised real harmonics, Holmes & Featherstone 2002 recursions) and synthesises gain · coefficient · Y. The Legendre recursion runs once per latitude, not per cell: cells whose |sin φ| agree to 1e-15 form a group (the cube's symmetries map up to 16 cells onto one latitude, equal to the last place, 2.2e-16 measured on levels 3 to 9, while distinct latitudes are 1.2e-11 apart at level 9), the cells south of the equator take P̄_lm(−z) = (−1)^(l+m) P̄_lm(z) through sums of even and odd degrees, and cos mλ and sin mλ are applied per order, outside the sum over l. The orders go outermost, since the gain does not mix them: per order the recursion runs once and keeps its P̄_lm for the band's degrees for the synthesis (3.4 MB at 6 × 256²), and the members' cos mλ and sin mλ are turned once. Groups go in blocks of 256. Every product is its own statement, so nothing is fused into a multiply-add whose choice of product would depend on the surrounding code (the result is the earlier two-pass filter's built without contraction, bit for bit on levels 6 to 8; against it with contraction 4.6e-13 of the band's largest value, 7e-17 of ζ's, after four chained passes). The result equals the band summed mode by mode at every cell to 7e-15 of its size (tested at level 4); against the per-cell recursion it differs by rounding, at most 6.6e-20 1/s (1e-12 of the field), because the sums are taken in another order and a group's cells use its first member's latitude. It is second order: a harmonic of the band comes back within 9.1e-3 at level 5 and 2.2e-3 at level 6, and harmonics outside the band (zonal, degree 1 to 7, 25 to 40) leak by as much. Constants give zero.

**Why this choice.** Spherical harmonics are what the flat FFT's waves are on a sphere: the same isotropic scale and the same exclusion of zonal waves, with nothing tuned anew. A filter built from the Laplacian (differences of Helmholtz solves, or a Chebyshev polynomial in K) would avoid the transform but cannot leave out m = 0 on its own and needs a polynomial of degree about 250 or tens of CG solves to resolve degree 8 against the grid's highest; the harmonics' cost is 84 M instructions at 6 × 128² and 336 M at 6 × 256² single-threaded every 2 simulated hours (before the orders went outermost 111 M and 444 M, then 55 ms and 220 ms), no tables (P̄_lm for the band's degrees would take 54 MB at 256² and save 15 % of the instructions but no cycles, the table streaming from memory; all degrees 0.9 GB). **Open for S4: which band.** The constants carry today's metres so the switch changes the geometry, not the closure, but the flat world defines the band in cycles round its planet (`EddyWave`, 7), and that is not Earth's band either. Three readings on Earth's radius: today's metres, 2 397 km, degree 16, zonal wavenumber 12 at 45°; the flat world's 7 cycles round the planet, 5 700 km, degree 6.5, zonal wavenumber 5 at 45°; Earth's fastest-growing baroclinic waves, about 4 000 km (about four deformation radii of 1 000 km), degree 10, zonal wavenumber 7 at 45°. Earth is the reference, which points at the third; the choice changes the closure, so the lead or the owner makes it. `makeEddyBand` takes any wavelength.

## Bands of latitude (`fn/LatitudeBands`, S4.2)

The sphere's counterpart of the flat world's rows, for the zonal means and the closures that read them (S4 plan, Codex DREV-CLAUDE-007-01 point 2). `makeLatitudeBands(grid, count)` makes `count` bands of equal angular width; a cell's latitude (asin of its centre's z, once) shares it between the two bands whose centres bracket it, linearly in latitude, and cells poleward of the outermost centres belong to the outermost band. The weights w_ib sum to one per cell, the band's area is B_b = Σ A_i w_ib, `bandMeans` divides Σ A_i w_ib f_i by it and `spreadBands` takes a band field back through the same weights, so both keep the area integral (to 1e-14, tested). `zonalWindVorticity` takes a zonal wind at the count + 1 boundaries and gives each band (rim_b u_b − rim_{b+1} u_{b+1}) / B_b, the circulation round the southern boundary minus the northern (Stokes); the sum over bands telescopes, so the vorticity spread to the cells has zero area mean to rounding. The boundaries are the circles that cut off the bands' own area, sin φ_b = 2 Σ_{k<b} B_k / Σ B − 1, not the nominal edges. Why: the cap south of such a circle has the area the weights give the bands south of it, Σ_{k<b} B_k = 2πR² (1 + sin φ_b) (Σ B = 4πR² to rounding), so B_b is the area of the true spherical band between two circles, and Stokes, ∫_cap ζ = −rim_b u_b (the cap on the right of an eastward rim), makes ζ_b the exact mean vorticity of that true band for any zonal wind. What is left against the band means of the cell vorticity is the weights' own quadrature, of second order. The nominal edges cut caps whose areas differ from Σ_{k<b} B_k at first order in the width at the poles (the outermost band takes every cell poleward of its centre). For the solid rotation the true band's mean has the closed form Ω (sin φ_south + sin φ_north), met to 2e-12 of 2Ω (tested; measured 2.3e-14 to 5.8e-13 on levels 5 to 7, growing with the cancellation of √(1 − s²) at the polar circles), which checks Σ A = 4πR² and the rims' rounding rather than the wind. Measured once: Stokes off the nominal edges was 2.8e-2 of 2Ω off the solid rotation at the poles (bands of one cell, level 6). On the circles, against the band means of the cell vorticity (tested, levels 5 to 7, the poles included), the solid rotation is off by 8.3e-4, 2.1e-4, 5.5e-5 of 2Ω and a pair of jets u = U cos φ sin² 2φ by 3.2e-3, 8.2e-4, 2.1e-4 of 8U / R: second order, the remainder being the cell means' own quadrature.

One band to the grid's side (two cells along a meridian, 2.8° at level 6, the flat world's row) is the choice. A wave that is not zonal leaks into the band means through the grid's quadrature: m = 1 cancels by the cube's symmetry (measured below 1e-15, tested below 1e-14), m = 4, which the cube's quarter turns leave, leaks 7.6e-4 to 9.6e-4 of a unit wave on levels 5 to 7 (tested; measured once: 1.2e-3 at level 4, 9.7e-4 at 8). Turned by α about the planet's axis (the cube's z; no other turn is tested) it leaks |cos 4α| times as much, since its sin 4λ part cancels by the cube's mirror: nothing at π/8, the same at π/4 (tested to 1e-14). Measured once: bands of one cell (2 · side) leak 1.4e-2 on every level, because an equatorial face's row of cells changes latitude along it in step with the bands; between the two the leak is not monotone in the count (0.0054 at 140 bands on level 6, as in Codex's probe, `.out/review-mailbox/evidence/DREV-CLAUDE-007/s4-review-probe.log`). The eddy band's output, which has no zonal part but for its own quadrature, leaks 2.5e-3 (2 397 km) and 2.0e-3 (4 000 km) of its RMS into the band means at level 6, 1.5e-3 and 2.3e-3 after a gain in latitude centred on 45° N, each tested a fifth above (`test/AtmosphereSphereBands.cpp`). `makeLatitudeBands` asserts every band has area; with count = side each band holds about two rows of cells.

The sums run in the cells' order. The latitude's asin and the boundary's square root are taken once at construction; asin is libm, so on another platform a cell's share may differ in its last bit, like `grid.centre` (the determinism queue).

## Forcing and the hour (`fn/SphereFlow`, `fn/SphereHour`, S4.1)

`stepSphereFlow(flow, grid, stencil, poisson, forcing)` takes the closures' `SphereForcing` (S4 plan, binding correction 1; Codex's DREV-CLAUDE-007-01, point 1): a tendency per cell (1/s², added to the advection's) and a friction rate r per cell (1/s, finite and ≥ 0; `admissibleForcing`). Over the step's span s (2Δt in the leapfrog, Δt in the forward step after a restart) x = before + s · (advection + tendency); friction keeps keep = 1 / (1 + s r) of it, implicitly; and the area mean is taken out where it is kept: next = keep · (x − c), c = Σ A keep x / Σ A keep. Subtracting the tendency's mean would not do, since a varying keep moves the mean with no tendency at all. Then the filter and the inversion as without forcing. `setVorticity` keeps the mean it is given in ζ (the inversion leaves it out of the wind only), so both levels are at zero mean once the start is; from a start with a mean the forced steps carry it out within steps. Tested at levels 3 and 4:
- both time levels keep a zero mean, to 1e-14 of Σ A |ζ| over 200 steps with a tendency whose own mean is not zero; from a start whose mean is 1e-2 of the largest ζ, 4e-16 after 30 steps;
- the forced path is the plain level plus span · F less one constant for every cell, forward and in the leapfrog (read back through the filter), and with a tendency of zeros the plain level to 1e-15;
- at constant r the level is keep times the level of the same step at r = 0, to 1e-14, forward and in the leapfrog; at varying r it is keep_i (x_i − c) with one c for every cell;
- an empty forcing is the plain step bit for bit, and the plain step and the tracer are bit for bit those of main 653d9bf on levels 4 to 6 (`.out/s41/probe/Same.cpp`); a forcing given as zeros takes the forced path, whose mean removal moves ζ by rounding;
- a value not finite, a negative rate or a vector of the wrong size is refused before anything changes, and so is an unconverged forced step, whose retry is a clean step bit for bit.

`advanceSphereHour(hour, flow, grid, stencil, poisson, source)` moves the flow over one window of 3600 s (S4 plan, binding correction 5; Codex's point 4). `source` (`SphereForcingSource`) fills the forcing of each step from the flow it steps from, into storage the window keeps, as closures that read the state do (relaxation, damping from the older level, eddy growth); an overload holds one forcing through the window. The source may read only the flow and a context fixed for the window: a try taken back asks it again for the same steps, and only so is the retaken window a clean run; time and the eddies (S4.3) go to it as the try's explicit context, which needs a parameter `SphereForcingSource` does not have yet (it takes the flow and the forcing, and the flow carries no clock): S4.3 adds it rather than a closure with its own state. Its output is emptied before each call (the capacity kept), so a term it fills on one try only does not reach the next. The window is a transaction: an exception from a try (the source, an allocation) puts the flow and the hour back as they came in and passes on, by moving the snapshots back, which allocates nothing, so it holds when memory has run out (Codex DREV-CLAUDE-007-04), and a window shorter than `shortest` is refused (`Shortest`, no steps) before the flow or the steps change (Codex DREV-CLAUDE-007-03). The window is split into `SphereHour::steps` equal steps, predicted from the windows before. Each step's true outflow Courant number, from the fluxes it steps with, is checked against `limit` 0.4 (Courant, Friedrichs & Lewy 1928) before it is taken. A window that exceeds it, or whose step does not converge, is taken back whole, the flow and its history restored, and tried again with twice the steps: at most `attempts` 4 tries, never more than `window / shortest` steps (`shortest` 30 s; a try that already took that many ends the tries, with the reason `Shortest`). A forcing that is not admissible ends the tries at once (more steps would not mend it). The reasons stay in `hour.refusals` for the caller to log; on false the flow is as before the window and `hour.steps` unchanged, so the same call repeats the same tries. After a window the count rises at once to keep the Courant number below `reserve` · `limit` (0.32), capped at `window / shortest`. It falls to the count needed only after `patience` 3 windows in a row, each taken at its first try, that would need at most `lower` 0.5 of it, so `setStep`, which restarts the leapfrog, runs only on a real change; a window taken only at a later try keeps its steps and starts the calm count afresh. Tested at level 4 on a solid rotation about the axis:
- two steps from Courant 0.36, spun up by 40 % an hour from the flow before each step, fail at the second; the window taken in four is the clean four-step run from its start with the same source, bit for bit, and the source was asked before each of the five steps taken;
- four refused tries double the steps and leave the flow as it was; the shortest step caps a rise and the doubling (two, then three steps at most three); a forcing not finite ends the tries at the first;
- a window taken only at its second try keeps its steps and clears the calm count, though `lower` 0.9 would call it calm;
- a source that throws at the second step leaves the flow (not started, no step) and the hour as they came in; a source that adds friction only at the coarse step does not hand it to the fine try, which is the clean fine run bit for bit; a window of 3600 s with a shortest step of 4000 s is refused; an allocation that fails at the second step, with every allocation after it failing too (the test binary's operator new behind a gate, `test/AtmosphereSphereRollback.cpp`), in the source or in the copy that takes a refused try back, leaves the whole flow and the hour as they came in;
- eight steps where three would do stay for two windows and fall to three at the third, the three windows bit for bit one run of 24 steps (no restart); a spin-up then raises them to four at once.

The window holds only the flow today. The transport's Courant contract (0.5 over the band's total flow, S4.6), the eddy band, the calendar, the tracers and the budgets join the restore with their owners. Each window copies the whole `SphereFlow`, work space included (about 65 MB at level 8), which the optimisation pass can trim. Bit identity of the controller between platforms is a goal, not a state: its decisions are deterministic over the same inputs, and natively Mac and Windows differ already in the libm behind those inputs (`grid.centre`).

## Damping (`fn/SphereDamping`, S4 plan binding correction 3)

`makeSphereDamping(grid, stiffness, decay)` and `addDamping(damping, grid, stiffness, level, tendency)`: the scale-selective damping −ν L² of the level a step starts from (`baseLevel`: the older level in the leapfrog, as today's flat step damps, ζₙ in a forward step), L = A⁻¹K with the flow's own K (`SpherePoisson::levels[0].stiffness`, unchanged as the elliptic operator), the biharmonic hyperdiffusion of global models (Jablonowski & Williamson 2011). It enters the step as a `SphereForcing` tendency, from a `SphereForcingSource`, separately from every other force here (Codex's DREV-CLAUDE-007-01, point 3).
- **The operator.** In the A-weighted product −ν L² is symmetric and ⟨ζ, −ν L² ζ⟩_A = −ν ‖L ζ‖²_A ≤ 0, so it only takes enstrophy out; both tested to 1e-13 at level 5 (measured 2e-15, 6e-16), and a constant is not damped. It is not a consistent ∇⁴: on Y₄² at level 5 it is off by 0.56 of the value inside the faces, 3.7 on face edges and 3.2 at the vertices (tested), the contract's caveat (below).
- **ν and the bound.** ν = 1 / (decay · λ²), λ the estimate of λ_max(A⁻¹K) by 60 steps of power iteration from the checkerboard (from below: 1.3e-5, 3.3e-4, 1.9e-3, 4.5e-3 under 3000 steps on levels 4 to 7, measured once), so the fastest mode decays in `decay` (6 h in the tests, the flat world's `GridDamping`). The check of a step uses the certified bound max_i Σ_j |K_ij| / A_i ≥ λ_max (Gershgorin 1931), 1.29, 1.35, 1.37, 1.38 times the estimate on levels 4 to 7 (measured once; tested below 1.4 at level 5). A step of span s keeps 1 − s ν λ² of an eigenmode of L before the filter, in [0, 1] while s ν λ² ≤ 1. The filter (strength 0.1, split 0.53) couples the two levels: a mode's two roots per step stay real up to s ν λ² = 0.976, the physical one positive and dominant and the leapfrog's computational one negative below 0.9746; between 0.976 and 2.06 the mode decays while its sign swings, and past 2.06 it grows (the roots of the filtered step's 2×2 recurrence; tested at 0.95, 1.05 and 2.5 on a mode started by the forward step). Below 0.976 a mode keeps its sign and never grows from step to step only as the forward step starts it: a state that excites the computational mode (another tendency, a burst of forcing) can swing and rise for a step, decaying faster than the physical root, inside the bound too. The check holds s ν bound² ≤ 1, as Codex sets it: on the spectrum that certifies s ν λ_max² ≤ 1 and no more. (estimate / bound)², 0.55 at level 5, is only an estimate of the ratio: the estimate is a Rayleigh quotient from below, so a lower one does not certify a tighter bound, and the margin to 0.976 is not proven. The cap is safe for the fixed, pure damping (Codex DREV-CLAUDE-007-06); it gives no monotone step, neither for an arbitrary history nor for a sum of forces. `dampingStiffness` = ν · bound², 8.5e-5 1/s at level 5 and 8.8e-5 at 7 for 6 h, so the leapfrog needs 2Δt ≤ 11 400 to 11 800 s, far above the Courant step. `SphereHour::stiffest` holds it: a window starts at no fewer steps than it needs, the count never falls below that, and a window that cannot meet it within `window / shortest` steps is refused (`Damping`, no steps; tested with stiffest 1/1800 s: four steps an hour from one, and refused at a shortest step of 1800 s; at 41/7200 s, where ceil(2 · window · stiffest) and the try's own test round apart, the count is raised to one the try passes). `stiffest` is the caller's; `advanceClosedHour` (Closures) sets it from the same damping its source adds.
- **Its K.** The damping carries the K object it was made from (`SphereDamping::stiffness`, compared by address, never read) and `addDamping` throws `std::invalid_argument` for any other, before the tendency changes, since ν and the bound are that K's (Codex DREV-CLAUDE-009-01; tested). A copied `SpherePoisson` is another K: its dampings are made anew; a moved one keeps its K.
- **The step with the filter.** On a flow of 1e-12 1/s without rotation, where advection (∝ ζ²) is out of the way, at level 5, the production step 900 s and 225 s (tested): the fast mode (the top of A⁻¹K) and Y₄² never grow in amplitude, enstrophy or energy from one step to the next and keep their sign, and each keeps (1 − 2Δt ν λ²) per two steps to 1e-3 over 6 h. The fast mode ends at 0.352 at 900 s and 0.364 at 225 s where exp(−t ν λ²) gives 0.368: the leapfrog's lagged damping is first order in Δt. Y₄² keeps 0.99993 in 6 h, and what it loses is ν ‖L m‖²_A / ‖m‖²_A over the time to 1e-3 (measured 3e-5; tested, and the test fails without damping). By the formula, not measured: at level 5 a mode of degree l decays in 6 h · (λ_max / λ_l)², 9 years for l = 4 and 14 days at the eddy band's 2 400 km (l ≈ 17), sixteen times longer per level finer. What leaves the mode stays below 1e-3 (6e-4 of the fast mode, 6e-5 of Y₄²).
- **Local bursts.** One cell inside a face, at a face edge's midpoint and at a cube vertex, for an hour at 900 s and 225 s: nothing grows from step to step, and the seams damp more than the inside, the enstrophy keeping 0.94 inside, 0.87 at the face edge and 0.89 at the vertex (both steps in the same tested ranges), since K's error there is amplified by its second application.
- **Past the bound.** At s ν λ² = 0.95 for the fast mode it keeps its sign and decays, at 1.05 its sign swings (five times in twelve steps) as it decays, and at 2.5 it swings about every second step and grows (tested), so a check is needed.

Whether this holds the seams' grid-scale noise without overdamping the physical eddies and moving the jets is S4.3's, in the joint runs with the closures (S4 plan); if not, the edge gradient's quadratic reconstruction comes first.

## Season (`fn/SphereSeason`, S4.3b)

`sphereSeasonOf(bands, day, temperature, base)` is the flat world's `seasonOf` with rows become bands (S4 plan, point 3; its constants copied into `constant/SphereAir.hpp` from `atmosphere/fn/Season.cpp` until the switch), from the zonal air temperature per band and the same of the annual-mean climate. The belts (`beltWind`) follow the sun 0.35° per degree of declination a month late and blow 1.6 times as hard as each half's subtropics (15° to 35°) are warmer than its subpolar bands (55° to 75°), against the same contrast of `base`, clamped to 0.5 to 1.8; each contrast is a difference of area-weighted means, so a constant cancels as between the flat world's equal counts of rows. The belts' wind sits on the bands' boundaries and the jet is its vorticity by Stokes (`zonalWindVorticity`), so it telescopes and `balancedJet` holds; the cells' surface branch is the belts' poleward wind on the boundaries and their lifting (MoistDepth · LiftTime per unit of convergence through the boundaries' circles) has no area integral either. Per band, the eddies grow at EddyGrowth · `storminess` of the centred temperature gradient (a polar band takes itself for the band past the pole, as the flat world's rows wrap there), and the thermal wind shears 12 m/s · sin 2|φ| per °C per degree of poleward cooling, at most 12 m/s. Each half needs a band centred in [15°, 35°) and one in [55°, 75°), as every count from 8 has (the grid's side, from 8 at level 3; of the counts below 8 only 4 does), and the base a positive contrast in each; both fields are one finite value per band and the day finite. Otherwise it throws `std::invalid_argument`, in release builds too. The insolation is S4.4's, from S2. `bandProfile(bands, latitude, value)` reads a profile given by strictly monotone latitudes (one pole-to-pole half of a flat world's rows, which run 90 → −90 → 90, or `zonalClimate`) onto the bands, linearly, for the prescribed temperature of S4.3's runs; fewer than two points, latitudes not strictly monotone or a point not finite throw. Determinism queue (S4 plan, Determinism): once a day the season calls libm, `asin` per boundary, `cos` in the belts' shift and in `declinationOn`, `exp` and `sin` in `beltWind`, `tan` in `storminess`, `sin` for the shear; Mac and Windows are compared by replay until those are owned. The season sets `SphereClosures::jet` between windows.

Tested at level 5: the belts' wind is 1.6 · `beltWind` at the shifted latitude and the surface branch its poleward wind by the belts' half, to 1e-12 on days 100 and 220, and round every boundary the jet holds −rim · u to 1e-12; jet and lifting have no area integral; on a base quadratic in |φ|, with the north's contrast 0.2, 1.5 and 3 times the base's and one northern band at a time warmed 2 °C, every boundary blows what the area-weighted contrasts of the two windows give, clamped to 0.5 and 1.8 and blended across ±10° of the belts' equator, to 1e-12; air cooling 0.5 °C per degree shears 6 m/s at 45° and grows eddies at EddyGrowth · storminess(0.5), four times as steep the shear is held at 12 m/s; the westerlies' equatorward edge in the north moves from 28° in February to 39° in August; a polar band's growth is storminess of half the gradient; the trades lift within 5° of the belts' equator and the subtropics 25° to 35° from it sink, each band by MoistDepth · LiftTime times the convergence through its rims. At level 4: a linear profile reads onto the bands exactly from either direction; a profile of the flat world's whole rows, of one point, with too few values, an infinite value or an infinite latitude is refused, and so is a season of seven bands (none in [55°, 75°)), of fields not one value per band, with a NaN, over a uniform or a reversed base, or on a NaN day. The guards, both clamps, the weights, the blend and the lifting were each checked to fail their test when removed or changed, and the windows' edges when moved past a band's centre (5.6° apart at level 5).

An explicit step's cost goes as h⁻³: the cells go as h⁻², the steps per hour as h⁻¹. The Poisson solve is still most of a step. Where the cost can come down further, not done here:
- Workers: the smoother, the matrix product and the band's blocks have no order among cells or blocks, so they can be split over threads and keep their result if the sums (`dotOf`, CG's fused r · r, the band's coefficients) are reduced in a fixed order. With one face's interior in the L2 cache the product should scale further than from memory (Neon's estimate 1.4–1.8× at level 8). - Mixed precision is in the code (Poisson, above); `.out/s4a/speed/mixed-precision.patch` is superseded.
- Tolerance 1e-6 instead of 1e-9 saves 30 %; a semi-Lagrangian step would lift the Courant limit; the band could be refreshed less often.

## Contract for the switch (S4)

- `makeSphereGrid(level)` and `makeSphereStencil(grid)` once per world, `releaseEdgeTables` after `makeSpherePoisson` unless the diagnostic operators are wanted; the level is the resolution choice (Q3) and nothing assumes one. Fields are per cell in the grid's order; the centre's z (sin φ) replaces `vertexLatitude`, and rows (`AirRows`) disappear: what today is per row (`season.jet`, `growth`, `drift`, the eddies' envelope) becomes a function of latitude per cell, or a zonal mean, which this slice does not provide (the m = 0 harmonics of `fn/SphereHarmonics` or area-weighted latitude bins would).
- `solvePoisson(..., &eddy)` becomes `solveSpherePoisson` plus `filterEddyBand` every `EddyEvery` wind steps.
- `stepCirculation`'s Jacobian becomes `stepSphereFlow`; S4 adds on top what today's step has besides advection: relaxation of the zonal mean, implicit friction (`dragKeep`), `growth · eddy`, biharmonic damping, the terrain's squeeze of the column, as tendencies inside the step (`SphereForcing`: a tendency and a friction rate per cell), not as edits of `flow.vorticity` between steps: an edit leaves ψ and the fluxes belonging to another ζ, and the only way to replace ζ is `setVorticity` (which re-inverts and restarts, so done every step it would turn the leapfrog into forward steps). A step that returns false has published nothing: retry it with a larger `limit` or a shorter step, or stop; never step on as if it had been taken.
- Damping must absorb the scheme's own grid-scale source at the cube vertices (The barotropic flow; `test/AtmosphereSphereDrift.cpp`), or the seams' edge gradient must become second order first. `fn/SphereDamping` is that term with its own proof (Damping); whether it suffices is S4.3's. For the biharmonic term K A⁻¹ K (A the cell areas) is positive semi-definite and so dissipates in the area-weighted norm: safe as grid-scale damping with a coefficient that goes as h⁴. It is not a consistent ∇⁴: on Y₄ its error is 0.56 of the value inside the faces and 3.7 on face edges at level 5, growing fourfold per level, since K's local error at the seams is amplified by the second application. Neither it nor K / area may be read pointwise as a physical quantity (diagnostics, a curvature someone consumes); only solutions of K are second order.
- `SphereFlow`, `SpherePoisson` and `EddyBand` hold their work space: one call at a time per object. Spreading the work over `Workers` needs one object per thread or the buffers moved out.
- Winds for transport are the edge fluxes (`flow.flux`, m²/s per metre of depth); the carried fields use `advectTracer` or a better scheme. Cell-centre wind vectors (for `weatherAt`, the clouds, the renderer) need a reconstruction from the edge fluxes, not provided yet.
- The step is Courant-limited: `advanceSphereHour` keeps it so (Forcing and the hour); stepping by hand, choose the number of wind steps per hour from `outflowCourant(grid, flux, step)` ≤ 0.4 and set it once with `setStep`, as a whole fraction of the window (an integer number of steps per hour). Changing it restarts the leapfrog (a forward step, first order for that step), so change it seldom and not per window from the current Courant number; one shorter step after `setStep` is for ending a run, not a window, since the next window would restart again.
- Ω from `rotationRateAt(age)` (S2's deep time), never `Coriolis` from `constant/Air.hpp`.

## Open

- The Laplacian's local error at face edges (about 0.1) and the first-order edge gradient and stream fluxes there: the solution is second order, but a wind derived from ψ near seams is first order (2.6e-3 of the largest flux at cube vertices, level 6), and the same error feeds the slow growth of grid-scale vorticity at the cube vertices. A quadratic reconstruction of the edge gradient would make it second order; whether the imprint matters is S4's to see in the weather.
- The tracer scheme diffuses across grid lines (l1 0.35 at level 6 for the cosine bell); humidity and heat in S4 may want FV3's dimensionally split PPM transport (Putman & Lin 2007).
- Cost (Timing): single-threaded, Poisson-bound, Courant-limited. Memory 330 MB at 6 × 256², measured before `carry` and `topology` added 12.6 MB each (an edge is 48 bytes of geometry, 104 of `EdgeStencil`, 16 of `carry`, 16 of `topology` and 8 of `behind`, a vertex 16 of `vertexCells`; the step reads the 16 bytes of `topology` instead of the grid's 48-byte edge; `releaseEdgeTables` frees the 82 MB of `EdgeStencil` once the solver is made), with about 180 MB more for a moment while `makeLaplacian` gathers its rows (448 bytes a cell).
- The eddy band's centre and width are today's metres, not Earth's (The eddy band, open for S4).
- Williamson et al. cases 2 and 5 need the shallow-water equations (a height field), beyond a barotropic core.
- Before the switch, from Codex's review of 89777e9 (`.out/review-mailbox/from-codex/DREV-CLAUDE-004-01.txt`), conditions for S4, not done here:
  1. K for the elliptic inversion is accepted, on the evidence of Y₁/Y₄ in every place and the solid rotation's fluxes; Domelevo–Omnes' result does not carry over directly to the eliminated vertices, and −A⁻¹Ku at face edges still does not fall like the interior error. Before switching: an unforced control run at level 6 and a run with the production closures, recording energy, enstrophy, maxima and growth by cube vertex / face edge / interior, and the jets' position and strength. If the artefact does not stay bounded at level 6 without over-damping physical eddies, fix the edge gradient before switching. If K is applied as local dissipation, verify that separately.
  2. Level 6 first, with real input on one thread: one profile with the Courant number, steps per hour, warm-start iterations, residual, fluxes and the trajectory's error against tolerance 1e-9; take 1e-6 if it fits the error budget of wind and transport. Then Neon measures the smoother and the sparse product on 1 / 4 / P workers, iterations and wall time per simulated hour. Choose a more elaborate time step from the Courant numbers of that profile: the usual semi-implicit treatment of fast linear terms does not lift the limit of explicit nonlinear advection; the candidate for a longer step is conservative semi-Lagrangian transport, checked for mass, range, phase error and cost at large Courant numbers.
  3. m ≠ 0 is the right counterpart of kx ≠ 0 about the rotation axis, and a gain by l of the radial FFT band. 4 000 km is a change of physical tuning: in a globe run, measure how much zonal energy leaks after the area quadrature and after multiplying by a latitude gain, and compare the distribution of energy by l, m and latitude against the control band of 2 397 km.
  4. The tests measure the solution's error (Y₁/Y₄), the growth at level 4 for one seed (drift), the wave's error with the step tied to the cell (Rossby–Haurwitz; a separate time order needs a fixed grid and a sweep of the step) and the bell's mass, extremes and shape under a given wind. For humidity and aerosols add a sharp front moving across the grid lines through a cube vertex and test the accepted Courant contract; at level 6 l1 ≈ 0.35, so measure spreading and diffusion too, not only the budget.
- Windows, debug builds and `-ffp-contract=off` are not checked; the bit-for-bit symmetry of K does not depend on contraction (the same expression is evaluated for both entries), and the split product equals the compressed one only if both are compiled alike (they are in one library).
