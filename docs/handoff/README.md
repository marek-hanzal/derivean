## What the owner decided during the work

These are binding and are not in the original briefs.

- **Look comes first.** The world must not read as a height map: no rounded forms everywhere, no uniform country. Edges, walls, canyons, rough inaccessible places, massive ranges with broad valleys under them, and different kinds of country as one travels. "A rock massif must be a massif, not a heap of hillocks."
- **Nothing is placed afterwards.** Stones, crags, cascades, hot-spring ground and every other feature must arise inside the simulation and be explainable. No props, no noise that shapes relief, no switched "modes" that stamp a landform where a classifier says so.
- **Measured against Earth.** Compare captures with photographs of real (plant-free) ground and the planet with the figures in the reference catalogue. The world has no life.
- **Cubes at every distance.** Far terrain is the voxel world at coarser cube sizes, drawn by the same pipeline as near voxels. No smooth terrain meshes, no interpolated normals.
- **WebGPU.** The renderer moves from WebGL to three's `WebGPURenderer`; the WebGL path is deleted at the flip.
- **View distance and fill first, optimisation last.** At least 250 km of view; generous budgets now, pulled down only when rendering cannot hold.
- **All cores.** Native threads for everything that can run in parallel. No GPU compute for the authoritative simulation (results must be bit-identical on every machine); GPU compute is for presentation.
- **Weather is causal.** Clouds come from water that evaporated; snow lies above a simulated snow line; nothing in the sky is random.
- **Volcanoes live during play.** A vent keeps a deterministic eruption schedule in calendar time; an eruption changes terrain, loads the atmosphere and leaves an aftermath. Frequency may be raised only through physical inputs (melt supply, magma properties), never through a multiplier.
- **Missing processes get built**: coasts and lake shores, wind and dry lands, volcanism, ice sheets, karst.
- **In-game map** on `M` (opens and closes).
- **The world reacts to the player where the consequences reach, not where the player stands** (26 Nov; required before any life work). A change wakes the ground around it at full resolution (Site, Ground) and its consequences spread through a coarse live zone (Local, Region) as far as they physically reach: a flood runs down the whole valley, a basin fills and spills, rivers change their discharge, whether or not the player is there. When the wave settles the zone sleeps again in its new state. Arriving later, finer tiles are rebuilt from the coarse live state, as a fine level inherits rivers and lakes from its parent today. The coarse level decides where water goes and how much; the fine level only adds detail that matches its sums, so the outcome never depends on the player's path and a time jump equals waiting. Today live tiles exist only at Site within 256 m of an edit, and a flood leaving a live group is lost: that is the gap this closes. It builds on level consistency (lakes and rivers the same at every level) and serves volcanoes (ash, lahars) and later life (fire, pests) with the same machinery.
- **Life is planned** (26 Nov): flora and small fauna from an origin of life, evolving from a founder; see [brief/life.md](brief/life.md). "No life" stays binding until that work starts, after the relief work and after the reactive world above.
- **Earth is the reference implementation** (26 Nov, about 20:00, first said of water gaps): "we copy Earth's behaviour; whenever our reference implementation, the real Earth, has the pattern, use it." Where Earth has a pattern, copy it; ask the owner only when Earth gives none. Example (consistency.md section 5): an antecedent gorge through a ridge stays, as on Earth where a river held its course while the ridge rose; the seam corridor only keeps the parent's course open, and the gorge's depth comes from the river's own erosion, not from the cut.
- **An Earth-sized planet** (26 Nov evening; it jumps ahead of everything queued): a sphere of radius 6 371 km, the real Sun and Moon as bodies, the planet's orbit, spin and axial tilt, so winter is colder from the planet's position against the Sun, not by a switch. No other bodies. Material is closed (water, rock, salt, sediment and air only move or transform; nothing escapes to a void or to space), energy is open (sunlight in, infrared out, the interior's heat). Earth's size 1:1: shrinking breaks the atmosphere's scale height, the Rossby radius, the plates and the horizon. Digging stops at a hard physical limit, each column's floor where its own geotherm reaches the temperature at which its rock creeps; no sci-fi, no magic, no survival mechanics for now. Plan, Codex's S0 review and the slices: `.out/lead/handover/PLANET.md` (its "S0 review outcome" overrides the rest), inventory of flat-world assumptions `.out/lead/handover/PLANET-inventory.md`.
- **The game is stored on disk** (26 Nov, about 23:55): the world is not in memory only, so storage is needed anyway, and the planet history can be computed once and read back. The sphere's history cost (S3, about 20–30 s parallel on L7) is therefore accepted. A disk cache of the history comes with the storage work, not as a blocker for S3.
- **Merges are the lead's** (26 Nov, about 23:55): branches from Codex and the sessions go into main after the lead's review, an independent reviewer and green tests, without asking the owner; what breaks is fixed forward. Nothing is pushed.
- **Platforms: macOS and Windows only** (27 Nov, about 01:30). Linux is not supported: no work on Linux-only failures, and cross-machine checks run on macOS and Windows (not WSL). The native build allows `macosx` and `windows` (port merged 27 Nov, `308cde7`: clang-cl, `-ffp-contract=on` on both, `/arch:AVX2`; [windows.md](windows.md), setup in `docs/windows.md`). The planet history is bit-identical on both; tiles still differ by libm (a deterministic maths library or committed tables is queued); the game window on Windows is not verified. Windows machine for tests: `ssh serakki-obsidian` (sftp too; clang-cl, xmake, node through mise).
- **Small measured deviations for speed** (27 Nov, about 09:30): in local landscape and wave physics a change may move results by about 1e-5 (for example tables in place of `powf`, `tanh`, `sinh`) when the simulation stays stable and the effect is measured. Such changes land as their own commits, apart from bit-exact ones, with the before-and-after numbers. Tables are generated once and committed as data, so macOS and Windows read the same values. Bit-identical results across thread counts and the two platforms stay binding.
- **Two machines, one result** (27 Nov, about 10:00): heavy builds, tests and long runs are spread over this Mac and the Windows machine (`ssh serakki-obsidian`) so neither idles while the other is saturated. A test passing on one platform stands for both during the work (do it first, fix it later); the whole suite runs on both when a larger chapter closes. Comparisons of fingerprints and timings stay on one machine, since tiles still differ by libm.
- Window title stays "DeRivean · Fable". The fixture demo ("The Stacked Channels") is gone; only the simulated world matters.
